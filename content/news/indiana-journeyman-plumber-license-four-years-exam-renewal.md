This explainer covers **Indiana only**, where plumbers are licensed through the Indiana Plumbing Commission and the
Indiana Professional Licensing Agency (PLA). It answers one practical question from the PLA's "Plumbing Licensing
Information" page: how do you qualify for the Indiana journeyman plumber license, and how often must it be renewed? It
describes that page as published on the date above. Nothing here describes a rule change, and nothing here applies to
any other state.

## Who can apply for the journeyman license?

The page sets an age minimum: applicants "Must be at least 18 years old". It then describes two application routes:

- **Approved apprenticeship school.** The applicant submits the Plumbing Apprenticeship Certificate of Completion, with
  the sponsor's signature on the approved-school section.
- **Plumbing trade.** The applicant shows either proof of a plumbing license from another state or proof of four years
  of experience in the trade. Under both options, the current plumbing employer must complete and sign an Affidavit of
  Experience in Plumbing Trade.

The page also quotes the state law behind these routes, Indiana Code 25-28.5-1-12: an Indiana resident must complete
at least four years in an apprenticeship program approved by the commission, or present a notarized statement of at
least four years of experience in the plumbing trade. It cites IC 25-28.5-1-32(2), (6) and (7) for the employment that
counts. For an applicant who is not an Indiana resident, the quoted law says a license from a state that has no
reciprocity agreement with Indiana must be presented to the commission to be eligible to take the examination.

The page's FAQ puts it in one line: "You must have at least four years in an apprenticeship program or be a licensed
plumber in another state" to qualify.

## Is there reciprocity with other states?

Not for this license. The FAQ states: "Reciprocity is not available for journeyman and contractor plumbers." An
applicant licensed elsewhere must apply and pay the fee; the application then goes before the board, and if it is
approved the applicant must take and pass the exam. The quoted law adds that a non-resident who lives in a state with no
plumbing licensing requirements must, before taking the exam, meet the same requirements as Indiana residents (four
years in a commission-approved apprenticeship or a notarized statement of four years of experience).

## How does the exam work?

Applicants "must have their application for examination reviewed and approved for eligibility by the Plumbing
Commission before" they register for the exam, which the page says is done on the PROV webpage (provexam.com). On
retakes, the FAQ states: "You can take the test seven times within a two-year period." The two years run from the first
scheduled exam date.

Two deadlines apply to the application itself. It is treated as abandoned if all the requirements are not submitted
within one year of filing. If it is denied, the page says the applicant may reapply or appeal with a petition within 18 days
of the date of the denial letter.

## How often is the license renewed?

The page states: "Journeyman Plumber licenses expire December 31st of every odd year". A renewal received after that
date carries a late fee, which is higher on or after March 1 of the following year. A license expired for three or more
years is reinstated by paying the current renewal fee and current initial application fee, submitting a letter of work
history or a resume, and making a personal appearance before the commission. The page does not mention continuing education for journeyman plumbers.

## What about apprentices?

The apprentice license has its own rules on the same page: applicants must be at least 17 and submit the Approved
Apprenticeship Program Sponsor Certification, signed by an approved school sponsor and listing the supervising
journeyman plumber or plumbing contractor's name and license number. The page states: "Plumbing Apprentice Licenses expire October 1st of every even year". At renewal,
apprentices must confirm they are still enrolled in an approved program.

## Where to check before you act

These are the requirements as shown on the PLA's plumbing licensing page on the date above. The Indiana Plumbing
Commission and the PLA are the authority, so confirm the current forms, fees, exam scheduling and renewal steps with
them before you apply. The statute text summarized here is as quoted on that page; the full Indiana Code 25-28.5 is
published by the Indiana General Assembly. These rules describe Indiana only; other states set their own. For other
