For concrete and masonry jobs, OSHA's construction Subpart Q follows its scope and definitions section (1926.700) with a short list of general rules:
29 CFR 1926.701, "General requirements." It has six paragraphs, (a) through (f), covering construction loads,
reinforcing steel, post-tensioning, riding concrete buckets, working under loads and one item of personal protective
equipment. This explainer reads the section as it stands in the eCFR (text current to November 19, 2026). It is an
explainer, not a rule change: the eCFR lists the section as issued at 53 FR 22643 (June 16, 1988) and amended at
59 FR 40730 (Aug. 9, 1994). It does not cover general industry workplaces under Part 1910.

## Where does 1926.701 apply?

Subpart Q's scope is in 1926.700(a): "This subpart sets forth requirements to protect all construction employees from
the hazards associated with concrete and masonry construction operations performed in workplaces covered under 29 CFR
part 1926." The same paragraph adds that "other relevant provisions in parts 1910 and 1926 apply to concrete and
masonry construction operations," so 1926.701 is not the only rule on a concrete job.

## What does OSHA require for protruding rebar?

1926.701(b), "Reinforcing steel," is one sentence: "All protruding reinforcing steel, onto and into which employees
could fall, shall be guarded to eliminate the hazard of impalement."

Two things in that text matter. The duty is tied to rebar "onto and into which employees could fall," and the goal is
stated as a result: the guarding must "eliminate the hazard of impalement." The section does not name a specific type
of cap or cover.

## Can a worker ride a concrete bucket?

No. 1926.701(d), "Riding concrete buckets," states: "No employee shall be permitted to ride concrete buckets." The
paragraph has no exceptions.

## Can anyone work under a concrete bucket?

1926.701(e), "Working under loads," has two parts:

- 1926.701(e)(1): "No employee shall be permitted to work under concrete buckets while buckets are being elevated or
  lowered into position."
- 1926.701(e)(2): "To the extent practical, elevated concrete buckets shall be routed so that no employee, or the
  fewest number of employees, are exposed to the hazards associated with falling concrete buckets."

Note the difference in wording. The ban in (e)(1) applies "while buckets are being elevated or lowered into position."
The routing rule in (e)(2) applies "To the extent practical" and aims for no employee or the fewest number of employees
exposed.

## When can loads go on a concrete structure?

1926.701(a), "Construction loads," states: "No construction loads shall be placed on a concrete structure or portion of
a concrete structure unless the employer determines, based on information received from a person who is qualified in
structural design, that the structure or portion of the structure is capable of supporting the loads."

The decision is the employer's, but it has to rest on information from "a person who is qualified in structural
design." When forms and shores may come off is a separate rule in 1926.703(e), covered in our post
[When Can Concrete Forms and Shores Be Removed?](/news/when-can-concrete-forms-shores-be-removed-osha-1926-703/)

## Who may stand behind the jack during post-tensioning?

1926.701(c), "Post-tensioning operations," has two rules for the time tensioning is under way:

- 1926.701(c)(1): "No employee (except those essential to the post-tensioning operations) shall be permitted to be
  behind the jack during tensioning operations."
- 1926.701(c)(2): "Signs and barriers shall be erected to limit employee access to the post-tensioning area during
  tensioning operations."

## What PPE does 1926.701 name?

1926.701(f), "Personal protective equipment," covers one task: "No employee shall be permitted to apply a cement, sand,
and water mixture through a pneumatic hose unless the employee is wearing protective head and face equipment."

That is the only PPE item in this section. General construction PPE rules are in Subpart E, which our PPE study guide
covers.

## What does 1926.701 not cover?

The section's six paragraphs are the ones above. Requirements for equipment and tools, cast-in-place concrete, precast
concrete, lift-slab construction operations and masonry construction are in other sections of Subpart Q
(1926.702 to 1926.706), which this post does not summarize.

## What this means for OSHA study

The points to know from 1926.701 are short and exact: protruding rebar that employees could fall onto or into must be
guarded "to eliminate the hazard of impalement"; no one rides concrete buckets; no one works under buckets while they
are being elevated or lowered into position; buckets are routed, to the extent practical, away from employees; loads go
on a concrete structure only after the employer's determination based on information from a person qualified in
structural design; only essential employees may be behind the jack during tensioning; and protective head and face
equipment is required for applying a cement, sand, and water mixture through a pneumatic hose.
