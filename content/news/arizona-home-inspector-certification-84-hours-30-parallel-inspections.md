Home inspector rules are set by each state that regulates the work, and this explainer covers **Arizona only**, where
home inspectors are certified by the Arizona State Board of Technical Registration. It answers one practical question
from the board's own rule, R4-30-247 ("Home Inspector Certification", in the version amended effective May 25, 2024),
and two of its web pages: what does an applicant need to become a certified home inspector in Arizona, and how long
does the certificate last? Nothing here describes a rule change, and nothing here applies to any other state.

## The short answer

The board's general information page sums it up: "In order to be a licensed home inspector in Arizona, pursuant to
R4-30-247, a home inspector candidate must pass the National Home Inspector Examination, submit a fingerprint clearance
card, submit evidence of successful completion of 84 hours of classroom training, and submit evidence of the successful
completion of 30 parallel home inspections (home inspections conducted with a supervising home inspector present)."

## Which exam, and how recent must it be?

R4-30-247(A)(1) asks for "Evidence of successful completion, within two years before the date of application, of the
National Home Inspector Examination". A pass older than two years at the date of application does not meet that
wording.

## What training counts?

R4-30-247(A)(5) asks for "Evidence of successful completion of 84 hours of classroom training or an equivalent course"
conducted by an educational facility licensed by the Arizona State Board for Private Postsecondary Education, or
accredited by the Distance Education Accrediting Commission, or by an accrediting agency approved by the United States
Department of Education. The course must cover the content areas the rule lists, from structural components, exterior
and roofing through plumbing, heating, cooling and electrical systems, insulation and ventilation, interiors, fireplaces
and solid fuel-burning devices, swimming pools and spas, and professional practice.

## What are parallel inspections?

R4-30-247(A)(6) asks for "Evidence of completion of 30 parallel inspections." The rule states: "The applicant shall
conduct these inspections on separate residential dwelling units and shall list them on a log provided by the Board."
The log includes the property address, the inspection date and the name and certification number of the supervising
home inspector. The rule states that the 30 parallel inspections and home inspection report "shall meet the standards in
R4-30-301.01", and the applicant keeps them for at least two years from the date of application.

The board allows time to finish them: "The Board may hold the applicant's package for a period of one year based solely
on the need for time to permit the applicant to complete the required parallel inspections."

R4-30-247(B) adds a sworn statement by the supervising certified home inspector or inspectors that the parallel
inspections meet the standards in R4-30-301.01, and a copy of one home inspection report that meets the standards in R4-30-301.01 and reports on at
least one immediate major repair as defined in the standards, along with the Report Checklist Supplement.

## What else goes in the application?

R4-30-247(A)(3) lists "A completed fingerprint card;", and R4-30-247(B)(5) refers to "a valid fingerprint clearance card
issued pursuant to Title 41, Chapter 12, Article 3.1". The board's FAQ adds: "If your clearance card application was
denied because of a past criminal conviction, you can apply to the Arizona Board of Fingerprinting for a good cause
exemption."

The application also includes background questions, a release authorizing the board to investigate the applicant's
education, experience, criminal and disciplinary action history, and a certification that the information is "accurate,
true, and complete". The FAQ states: "The Board will verify your answers to the background questions by searching public
records databases."

## How long is the certificate valid?

The FAQ states: "Home Inspector certification is valid for one year from the date of issuance." It adds that the board
sends courtesy reminders, "however it is the responsibility of the individual to complete the renewal timely."

At renewal, R4-30-247(D) states: "All certified home inspectors shall provide proof of financial assurance at the time
of each annual certification renewal." The general information page describes financial assurance as "an E&O insurance
policy or bond", and R4-30-247(D) requires notice to the board "within five business days of any loss of, or change in,
financial assurance." The three pages used here do not state a continuing education requirement for renewal.

## What if the certificate goes inactive?

Under R4-30-247(F), a home inspector reactivating an inactive certificate who has not practiced during that time as a
certified home inspector in another state requiring registration, for the previous five years, "shall take and pass the
National Home Inspector Examination."

## Where to check before you act

These are the requirements as shown in R4-30-247 and on the board's pages on the date above. The Arizona State Board of
Technical Registration is the authority, so confirm the current rule, forms and fees with the board before you enroll,
log parallel inspections or apply. These rules describe Arizona only; other states set their own. For the federal lead
disclosure and radon topics that home inspectors meet, see our [home inspector topics](/home-inspector/).
