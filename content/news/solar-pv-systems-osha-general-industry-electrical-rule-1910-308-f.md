Does federal OSHA say anything specific about solar panels? For people preparing for a solar PV certification, here
is what the general industry rules contain. In OSHA's general industry electrical standard (29 CFR Part 1910, Subpart S), the answer is one short
paragraph: 29 CFR 1910.308(f), "Solar photovoltaic systems." This explainer sets out what it requires, which
installations it applies to, and what it does not cover. Nothing here describes a rule change, and goraedu does not
cover any private solar certification exam.

## Where the solar rule sits

29 CFR 1910.301(a) says that "Sections 1910.302 through 1910.308 contain design safety standards for electric
utilization systems" and that this category includes "all electric equipment and installations used to provide
electric power and light for employee workplaces." Section 1910.308 is titled "Special systems," and paragraph (f) is
the solar part.

Its opening sentence sets the scope: "This paragraph covers solar photovoltaic systems that can be interactive with
other electric power production sources or can stand alone with or without electrical energy storage such as
batteries. These systems may have ac or dc output for utilization."

## What 1910.308(f) requires

The paragraph has two requirements.

**Keeping PV circuits apart from other systems.** Paragraph (f)(1), "Conductors of different systems," states:
"Photovoltaic source circuits and photovoltaic output circuits may not be contained in the same raceway, cable tray,
cable, outlet box, junction box, or similar fitting as feeders or branch circuits of other systems, unless the
conductors of the different systems are separated by a partition or are connected together."

**A way to disconnect the PV source.** Paragraph (f)(2), "Disconnecting means," states: "Means shall be provided to
disconnect all current-carrying conductors of a photovoltaic power source from all other conductors in a building or
other structure." It adds a limit on the switch itself: "Where a circuit grounding connection is not designed to be
automatically interrupted as part of the ground-fault protection system, a switch or circuit breaker used as
disconnecting means may not have a pole in the grounded conductor."

The words in these rules are defined in 29 CFR 1910.399. For example, a "Disconnecting means" is "A device, or group
of devices, or other means by which the conductors of a circuit can be disconnected from their source of supply." A
"Grounded conductor" is "A system or circuit conductor that is intentionally grounded." The same section also names a
solar photovoltaic system as a possible source of power in its definitions of "Premises wiring" and "Separately
derived system."

## Which installations it applies to

29 CFR 1910.302(b) sorts the Subpart S design rules by installation date. Paragraph (b)(4) lists requirements that
"apply only to electrical installations and utilization equipment installed after August 13, 2007," and "§
1910.308(f)—Solar photovoltaic systems" is on that list. So the solar paragraph applies to installations made after
August 13, 2007.

The coverage limits in 1910.302(a) also apply. Paragraph (a)(1) covers "electrical installations and utilization
equipment installed or used within or on buildings, structures, and other premises," and paragraph (a)(2) lists what is
not covered. Among the exclusions are "Installations under the exclusive control of electric utilities for the purpose
of communication or metering; or for the generation, control, transformation, transmission, and distribution of
electric energy" located in buildings used exclusively by utilities for such purposes or outdoors on property owned or
leased by the utility, or "on public highways, streets, roads, etc., or outdoors by established rights on private property."

## What about construction sites?

In the current eCFR text of the construction
electrical subpart (29 CFR Part 1926, Subpart K), the words "photovoltaic" and "solar" do not appear, so there is no
construction paragraph that matches 1910.308(f). Subpart K's installation rules have their own scope: 29 CFR 1926.402(a) says they apply to "installations, both
temporary and permanent, used on the jobsite," but not to "existing permanent installations that were in place before
the construction activity commenced." Our [OSHA construction electrical study guide](/osha/electrical/study-guide/) covers
Subpart K.

## What 1910.308(f) does not cover

The paragraph does not set rules for system design, sizing, product listing, utility interconnection or any
certification, and it does not mention any solar certification or exam. Those topics are outside what goraedu covers.
