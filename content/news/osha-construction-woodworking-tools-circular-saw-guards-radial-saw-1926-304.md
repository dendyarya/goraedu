OSHA's construction rules cover woodworking tools in 29 CFR
1926.304, "Woodworking tools," in Subpart I (Tools, Hand and Power). This explainer answers one practical question: what
guards and controls does OSHA require on circular saws and other woodworking tools used in construction? It reads the
section in the eCFR (text current to November 24, 2026). It is an explainer, not a rule change.

## What guards must a portable circular saw have?

1926.304(d) states: "All portable, power-driven circular saws shall be equipped with guards above and below the base
plate or shoe."

The paragraph then describes each guard:

- **Upper guard:** it "shall cover the saw to the depth of the teeth, except for the minimum arc required to permit the
  base to be tilted for bevel cuts."
- **Lower guard:** it "shall cover the saw to the depth of the teeth, except for the minimum arc required to allow
  proper retraction and contact with the work."
- **Return:** "When the tool is withdrawn from the work, the lower guard shall automatically and instantly return to the
  covering position."

## What do radial saws and table saws need?

- **Radial saws:** under 1926.304(g)(1), "The upper hood shall completely enclose the upper portion of the blade down to
  a point that will include the end of the saw arbor." The hood must "protect the operator from flying splinters, broken
  saw teeth, etc., and will deflect sawdust away from the operator." The sides of the lower exposed part of the blade
  "shall be guarded to the full diameter of the blade by a device that will automatically adjust itself to the thickness
  of the stock and remain in contact with stock being cut to give maximum protection possible for the operation being performed."
- **Hand-fed ripsaws:** under 1926.304(i)(1), each "shall be guarded by a hood which shall completely enclose that
  portion of the saw above the table and that portion of the saw above the material being cut." The hood must
  "automatically adjust itself to the thickness of and remain in contact with the material being cut," be "of adequate
  strength to resist blows and strains incidental to reasonable operation, adjusting, and handling," and be "made of
  material that is soft enough so that it will be unlikely to cause tooth breakage."
- **Hand-fed crosscut table saws:** under 1926.304(h)(1), each "shall be guarded by a hood which shall meet all the
  requirements of paragraph (i)(1) of this section for hoods for circular ripsaws."

## Do fixed woodworking machines need a disconnect switch?

Yes. 1926.304(a) states: "All fixed power driven woodworking tools shall be provided with a disconnect switch that can
either be locked or tagged in the off position." Paragraph (a) applies to fixed power driven woodworking tools.

## When must a saw's speed be marked?

1926.304(b) applies to "all circular saws over 20 inches in diameter or operating at over 10,000 peripheral feet per
minute." On those saws, the operating speed "shall be etched or otherwise permanently marked." The paragraph continues:
"Any saw so marked shall not be operated at a speed other than that marked on the blade. When a marked saw is
retensioned for a different speed, the marking shall be corrected to show the new speed."

## What other rules apply?

- **Self-feed:** "Automatic feeding devices shall be installed on machines whenever the nature of the work will permit."
  Feeder attachments "shall have the feed rolls or other moving parts covered or guarded so as to protect the operator
  from hazardous points" (1926.304(c)).
- **Personal protective equipment:** "All personal protective equipment provided for use shall conform to subpart E of
  this part" (1926.304(e)).
- **Consensus standard:** "All woodworking tools and machinery shall meet other applicable requirements of American
  National Standards Institute, 01.1-1961, Safety Code for Woodworking Machinery" (1926.304(f)).
