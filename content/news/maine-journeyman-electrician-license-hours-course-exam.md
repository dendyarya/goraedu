This explainer covers **Maine only**, where electricians are licensed by the Electricians' Examining Board. It answers one practical question from the board's journeyman license
page and from Maine's statute on electrician licenses (Title 32, section 1202-B): what hours, schooling and exam lead
to a Maine **journeyman electrician** license? Nothing here describes a rule change, and nothing here applies to any
other state.

## What does a Maine journeyman electrician do?

The board's page states: "A Journeyman Electrician is a licensed individual making electrical installations". The
statute (subsection 4, paragraph A) adds that the work is done "while employed by a supervising master electrician,
limited electrician or electrical company".

## What comes first: the exam or the license?

The exam. Subsection 4, paragraph B of the statute opens: "An applicant for a journeyman electrician license must
first pass an examination approved by the board." The statute then lists the routes that make an applicant eligible
for that exam.

## What is the main route: apprentice hours plus a course?

The first route in the statute, paragraph B(1), is "Completion of at least 8,000 work hours in electrical installations
as a licensed apprentice electrician" together with "a 576-hour course of study as adopted by the board" or "a course
of study approved by the United States Department of Labor, Employment and Training Administration, Office of
Apprenticeship" (or by a state apprenticeship agency recognized by the Office of Apprenticeship).

The board's page describes this route as: "Complete at least 8,000 hours of service as a licensed apprentice or
helper", plus a 576-hour program of study approved by the board or from an accredited institution. It lists the core
of that program as "45 hour courses each in Electricity I, Electricity II, Math I, Controls I and a current National
Electrical Code course", with the remaining hours made up of electives.

## Can I take the exam before I finish all 8,000 hours?

On this route, yes, under one condition. The statute says that an applicant who has completed that course of study and
at least 4,000 of the 8,000 required apprentice hours "may take the examination prior to completing the remaining required
work hours". The board's page puts it this way: "A licensed apprentice who has completed an approved apprenticeship
program of study" and has worked at least 4,000 hours as a licensed apprentice electrician is qualified to sit for the
exam.

## Do school programs count toward the hours?

The statute credits hours for three kinds of program:

- **Secondary school career and technical education:** under paragraph B(3), a graduate of an approved electrical
  program "is credited 1,000 work hours in the field of electrical installations" and "may take the examination upon
  graduating from the program". The statute adds: "The individual must complete any remaining work hours prior to
  applying for a journeyman electrician license". The board's page describes this route as: "Be a graduate of an
  accredited applied technology high school 2-year electrical program, have worked for 7,000 hours".
- **Community college:** paragraph B(4) covers "Completion of an electrical program at an accredited community college
  consisting of a 576-hour course of study" together with 8,000 work hours as a licensed helper electrician. The
  graduate "is credited 4,000 work hours in the field of electrical installations" and may take the exam upon
  completing the program.
- **Corrections programs:** paragraph B(5) covers "Completion of a vocational-electrical program of a state department
  of corrections", also with 4,000 hours credited toward 8,000 helper hours. The board's page groups this with the
  community college route: "Be a graduate of a Maine community college electrical program or a vocational-electrical
  program", with 4,000 hours of work under the supervision of a master or the equivalent.

A last route, paragraph B(6), covers comparable experience, education or training "completed within the State or
outside the State, that is acceptable to the board". The statute leaves that decision to the board.

## What is needed after passing the exam?

Paragraph C of the statute starts: "Upon passage of the examination, the board may issue the journeyman license" to an
individual who submits a license application, proves any required work hours and "provides evidence of completion
of a 45-hour course in the National Electrical Code" within the 2 years before applying. The board's page gives the license term as "Two years
from date of first licensure".

For renewal, the board's page lists: "Continuing Education - 15 Hour current National Electrical Code course as
adopted by the board." It also states: "Online submission of your renewal application should not be construed as
automatic renewal of your license."

## Where to check before you act

These are the requirements as shown on the Electricians' Examining Board's journeyman page and in Title 32, section
1202-B on the date above. The board is the authority, so confirm the current hours, courses, exam and forms with it
before you count hours, enroll in a program or apply. The senior journeyman license and reciprocity with
other states are on the same board page and are not covered here. These rules describe Maine only; other states set
their own. Our [electrician hub](/electrician/) covers the federal OSHA electrical safety rules electricians work
