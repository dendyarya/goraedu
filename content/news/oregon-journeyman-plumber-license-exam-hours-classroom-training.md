Plumbing licenses are issued at the state or local level. This explainer covers **Oregon only**, where
the journeyman plumber license is issued by the Building Codes Division (BCD) of the Department of Consumer and
Business Services. It answers one practical question from BCD's own application form, its licensing page and the
state's official License Directory: what hours and training do you need before BCD lets you take the journeyman
plumber exam? Nothing here describes a rule change, and nothing here applies to any other state.

## What does an Oregon journeyman plumber license allow?

The License Directory entry for the Journeyman Plumber (JP) license, which names "DCBS - Building Codes Division" as
the issuing agency, states: "A journeyman plumber may perform all plumbing installations, alterations, or remodels."

## Which paths lead to the license?

The License Directory lists three alternatives, any one of which qualifies:

1. "(1) A certificate of completion from a registered 4-year apprenticeship program, or a referral letter from
   either the registered training committee or Oregon Plumbing Board approved training program."
2. "(2) Verified proof of at least 576 hours of plumbing related classroom training in subjects listed in OAR
   918-695-0030(2)(a); -and- verified proof of 3,850 hours lawful commercial AND 3,850 hours lawful residential
   on-the-job experience as an apprentice or journeyman plumber."
3. "(3) Verified proof of 7,700 hours of lawful commercial and 7,700 hours of lawful residential on-the-job experience
   while holding a license with a similar scope of work to an Oregon journeyman plumber (individual installer
   license)."


## What does the application form ask for?

BCD's Journeyman Plumber License Application (form 440-2571) states: "To qualify as a journeyman plumber, you must meet
the requirements for one of the pathways listed in step 2." Step 2 offers two pathways. Pathway 1 asks for "Proof of
at least 576 hours of plumbing-related classroom training", "Proof of 3,850 hours of lawful commercial on-the-job
experience" and "Proof of 3,850 hours of lawful residential on-the-job experience". Pathway 2 asks for 7,700 hours of
commercial and 7,700 hours of residential work experience "lawfully obtained while holding a license with a similar
scope of work to an Oregon journeyman plumber (individual installer license)".

The form's step 2 does not describe the apprenticeship-certificate path that the License Directory lists first. Its
document checklist does ask for "Proof of completion of an out-of-state apprenticeship program recognized by the State
of Oregon." If you qualify through an apprenticeship, confirm
with BCD how to apply.

## Is there a limit on hours counted per year?

Yes. The form states: "For the purpose of qualifying for a license, the division will only consider 2,000 hours of
lawful work experience per year." It cites OAR 918-030-0030(4). It also says on-the-job experience "must meet the
minimum qualifications of 918-695-0030(2)(b) and (c)". BCD's Journeyman plumber license page adds: "You must list your
experience in the required work categories in OAR 918-695."

## Who verifies the experience?

The form states: "Verification of work experience (Form 440-2571A) must be included with this application for each
employer." Verification "must come from one of the following:" the "Current or previous employer actively involved in
the applicant's work"; if the employer "is out of business, deceased or otherwise unlocatable," "the
applicant's supervisor"; and if neither can be located, "a
co-worker directly involved in work performed by the applicant." Co-worker verification "must be accompanied by
employment records or other supporting documentation". Applicants relying on military experience must submit
"Official documentation from a supervising official showing type and approximate hours of work experience" or, if supervisory officials cannot be located,
"other reliable documentation verifying training and experience" (OAR 918-030-0050).

## What else goes in the packet?

BCD's licensing page says "an application must be submitted as a single packet with all required documentation." The
form asks for "Official transcripts of classroom training", "Proof of a high school diploma, GED, or equivalent" (it
adds: "A college degree will substitute."), a copy of every out-of-state license you hold, and a "2-by-2-inch
passport-style photo" that "will be printed on your license when it is issued."

## What happens after BCD approves the application?

The licensing page states: "After your application is received and approved, the division will mail you a letter of
authorization that details how to schedule the licensing exam." It continues: "If you pass the exam, your license
will be mailed to you."

## How long does the license last?

The License Directory states: "License renewal is every three years on April 1." Its continuing education entry reads:
"24 total required hours." It adds that "Continuing Education (if necessary) and fees will be explained on the renewal
form."

## Where to check before you act

These are BCD's requirements as shown on its pages and form on the date above. The form and page cite the Oregon
Administrative Rules (OAR 918-695 and OAR 918-030); read those rules for the exact conditions, and confirm the current
requirements with the Building Codes Division before you count hours or apply. For the trade's status on goraedu, see the [plumbing](/plumbing/) page and
