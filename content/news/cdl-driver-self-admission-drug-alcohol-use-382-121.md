FMCSA's drug and alcohol testing rule for commercial drivers, 49 CFR Part 382, has a short section on what happens
when a driver comes forward on their own: 49 CFR 382.121, "Employee admission of alcohol and controlled substances
use." This explainer asks: can a CDL driver admit drug or alcohol use without
being disciplined? It describes the regulation as published in the eCFR on the date checked (November 20, 2026);
nothing here describes a rule change.

## Who does Part 382 cover?

Section 382.103(a) says the part applies "to service agents and to every person and to all employers of such persons
who operate a commercial motor vehicle in commerce in any State" and are subject to the CDL requirements of Part 383
(or the Mexican or Canadian equivalents named there). Section 382.103(d) lists exceptions, such as employers and
drivers covered only by the Federal Transit Administration's testing rules in Part 655.

## What does 382.121 protect a driver from?

Section 382.121(a) states that employees "who admit to alcohol misuse or controlled substances use are not subject to the
referral, evaluation and treatment requirements of this part and part 40 of this title," but only if four conditions
are all met. (Section 382.605 says those referral, evaluation and treatment requirements "must be performed in
accordance with 49 CFR part 40, Subpart O.")

## Does the employer have to have a program?

The protection works only through an employer program. The first condition, 382.121(a)(1), is that "The admission is in
accordance with a written employer-established voluntary self-identification program or policy" that meets paragraph
(b). Section 382.121 does not itself require every employer to set up such a program; it sets out what a program must
contain to qualify. A driver whose employer has no qualifying written program cannot rely on this section.

## What conditions must the admission meet?

Besides being made under a qualifying program, the admission must meet three more conditions in 382.121(a):

- "The driver does not self-identify in order to avoid testing under the requirements of this part" (a)(2).
- The driver makes the admission "prior to performing a safety sensitive function (i.e., prior to reporting for
  duty)" (a)(3).
- The driver "does not perform a safety sensitive function until the employer is satisfied that the employee has been
  evaluated and has successfully completed education or treatment requirements in accordance with the
  self-identification program guidelines" (a)(4).

Section 382.107 defines "Safety-sensitive function" as "all time from the time a driver begins to work or is required
to be in readiness to work until the time he/she is relieved from work and all responsibility for performing work",
including driving, inspecting, loading and waiting to be dispatched.

## Can the employer discipline the driver?

Not for a qualifying admission. Under 382.121(b)(1), a qualifying program "must prohibit the employer from taking
adverse action against an employee making a voluntary admission of alcohol misuse or controlled substances use within
the parameters of the program or policy and paragraph (a) of this section". The protection is limited to admissions
that stay within those parameters; an admission made to avoid a test, or after the driver has reported for duty, does
not meet paragraph (a).

The program must also "allow the employee sufficient opportunity to seek evaluation, education or treatment to
establish control over the employee's drug or alcohol problem" (382.121(b)(2)), and "may incorporate employee
monitoring and include non-DOT follow-up testing" (382.121(b)(5)).

## What must happen before the driver returns to duty?

Under 382.121(b)(3), the program must let the driver return to safety-sensitive duties "only upon successful completion
of an educational or treatment program, as determined by a drug and alcohol abuse evaluation expert, i.e., employee
assistance professional, substance abuse professional, or qualified drug and alcohol counselor".

Under 382.121(b)(4), the program must also ensure that, before the driver performs a safety-sensitive function, the
driver undergoes "a non-DOT return to duty test with a result indicating an alcohol concentration of less than 0.02;
and/or" "a non-DOT return to duty controlled substance test with a verified negative test result for controlled
substances use".

## Is the admission reported to the Clearinghouse?

Section 382.705(b)(4) requires employers to report on-duty alcohol use, pre-duty alcohol use, alcohol use after an
accident and controlled substance use "by the close of the third business day following the date on which the employer
obtains actual knowledge, as defined at § 382.107". That definition of "Actual knowledge", written "for the purpose of
subpart B of this part", includes "an employee's admission of alcohol or controlled substance use, except as provided in
§ 382.121". So the definition those reports rely on sets aside admissions covered by 382.121. In the same section,
382.705(b)(5)(vi) lists the evidence an employer may submit for those reports, including "employee statements (other
than admissions pursuant to § 382.121)".
