This explainer covers **Idaho only**, where electrician licenses are issued through the Division of Occupational and
Professional Licenses (DOPL) and the Idaho Electrical Board. It answers one practical question from Idaho's own law
and DOPL's forms: what hours and what exam lead to a journeyman electrician license in Idaho? Nothing here describes a
rule change, and nothing here applies to any other state.

## What does Idaho law say about the journeyman license?

Idaho Code section 54-1007, subsection (1), says licenses go to people "as have by examination shown themselves to be
fit, competent and qualified" for the trades it names, including "journeyman electrician".

The statute then gives two routes to the journeyman license.

**The apprentice route.** Subsection (2) says an apprentice electrician "may take the journeyman's examination" if
he "has completed the required related instruction for electrical apprentices" approved by the Idaho state board for
career technical education, shown by "a certificate from an approved provider," and "has worked the number of hours
as prescribed by the Idaho electrical board". It adds a condition: "the apprentice shall have been registered with
the division of occupational and professional licenses as an apprentice" for all the time claimed.

**The experience route.** Subsection (4) says that "any person who can demonstrate eight (8) years of work
experience, defined as a minimum of sixteen thousand (16,000) hours, making electrical installations on the job,"
"shall be considered as qualified to apply for a journeyman electrician's license in this state."

## How many hours does the apprentice route need?

The statute leaves the number to the board. DOPL's Electrical Journeyman License Application (revised August 2,
2024) states it under the heading "Idaho Apprenticeship School and 8,000 Work Hours Pathway": a "Completion
Certifcate from a board approved Electrical Apprenticeship Program" and a Work Verification Form for 8,000 hours of
supervised electrical installation work. The form says these "Work hours can be obtained in or out of the State of
Idaho."

Under "Work Experience Pathway", the same form asks for a "Work Verification Form proving 16,000 hours of supervised
electrical installation work".

This post does not quote the board's administrative rule on hours, because we could not read it on the date above.
Ask DOPL which rule text applies to your hours.

## Which hours do not count?

Both pathways on the application carry the same note: "Work experience in appliance repair, motor winding, or
communications will not count towards a Journeyman license."

The application's work verification form also asks for the "Number of hours worked while holding an active
registration, if required by the state" and states: "Active registrations are required in Idaho for hours to count."

## What if you worked without an active apprentice registration?

DOPL has a separate "Active Registration Waiver Request" form. It states: "Submit this waiver in conjunction with an
application for licensure as an Idaho Electrical Journeyman," and "Standalone waiver requests will not be considered."
The applicant must give "justification for the lapse in registration" and include "W2s proving employment at a
licensed electrical company during the duration of unregistered practice and/or military documentation showing relevant experience". The form also says: "All waiver requests
must be received NO LATER THAN fourteen (14) calendar days prior to the next scheduled Board meeting." The form does
not say how the board decides these requests.

## Do residential electrician hours count toward the journeyman license?

Subsection (5) of the statute describes a separate residential electrician license: the residential exam, "a two (2)
year course of related instruction" and "four thousand (4,000) hours of electrical experience as an Idaho-registered
apprentice" or under the rules of the jurisdiction where the experience was earned. It then states that a journeyman
applicant "may utilize the experience hours identified in this section toward the work experience requirement for a
journeyman electrician license."

## Is there an exam, and when do you take it?

Yes. The application asks: "Have you taken and passed the applicable NASCLA examination through PSI?" and says
"(Applicants must pass the examination prior to applying for licensure)". Its checklist lists "Passing Examination
Score (Passing score required prior to application submission)".

The signature section of the same form also says the applicant has "one (1) year from the date of this application to
take and pass the required licensing examination". The form does not explain how these two statements fit together,
so ask DOPL about the order before you schedule the exam.

## What the pages do not say

The application does not state a classroom hour total, an exam passing score, exam content, renewal terms or
continuing education. The statute also allows licensing by reciprocity with some states; the application lists a
"Reciprocal Pathway" and separate sections for NERA and Oregon applicants, which this post does not cover.

## Where to check before you act

These are the requirements as shown on Idaho's official pages on the date above. DOPL and the Idaho Electrical Board
are the authority for the license, so confirm the current requirements, forms and rule text with them before you
count hours, register as an apprentice or book the exam. These pages describe Idaho only; other states set their own
rules. Our [electrician hub](/electrician/) covers the federal OSHA electrical safety rules electricians work under.
