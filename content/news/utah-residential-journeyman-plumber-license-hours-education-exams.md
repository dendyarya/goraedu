Plumbing licenses are issued by each state, and this explainer covers **Utah only**, where plumbers are licensed by the
Division of Professional Licensing (DOPL) in the Utah Department of Commerce. It answers one practical question from
DOPL's own plumbing pages: what hours, schooling and exams lead to a Utah **Residential Journeyman Plumber** license?
Nothing here describes a rule change, and nothing here applies to any other state.

## Which plumber licenses does Utah have?

DOPL's exam page states: "There are five Plumber license classifications in Utah". It lists them as Apprentice,
Journeyman, Master, Residential Journeyman and Residential Master. For the apprentice classification the page states:
"There are no exam requirements for this classification." This post is about the residential journeyman license; the
(non-residential) journeyman license has its own hour and education figures on DOPL's site.

## What is the first route: apprenticeship plus school?

DOPL's application page for the residential journeyman license lists three requirements for this route:

- "6,000 hours of experience as a licensed Apprentice Plumber", "working under the supervision of a licensed
  Journeyman or Master Plumber."
- "Completion of a 3-year (at least 432 classroom hours) approved apprentice plumber education program."
- "Pass the Utah Residential Plumber Theory and Practical exams."

The exam page describes the schooling for this route as "6 semesters or the competency exam equivalence; and" together
with "6,000 hours of residential plumbing experience as a licensed apprentice plumber."

## Is there a route without the school program?

Yes. The application page lists a second route: "12,000 hours of licensed plumber experience working under the
supervision of a licensed Journeyman or Master Plumber." followed by "Pass the Utah Residential Theory and Practical
exams." The exam page gives the same alternative as "12,000 hours of residential plumbing experience as a licensed
apprentice plumber."

In both routes, the hours are worked while licensed and under the supervision of a licensed journeyman or master
plumber, as the pages above state.

## Which exams are required?

The exam page names two exams for the residential journeyman license: the "Utah Residential Plumber Written Exam" and
the "Utah Plumber Practical Exam". It also states: "Preapproval for testing is NOT required."

DOPL's FAQ, answering whether someone can take the journeyman or master exam before finishing their work hours, states:
"No, state law requires that on-the-job work hours and school hours/grades be met before one is approved to take the
exam." DOPL's pages do not answer that question separately for the residential exams, so ask DOPL if your hours are not
yet complete. The exam page links the candidate information bulletin and a page to schedule a Division licensing exam.

## When do I submit the application?

After the exams. The exam page states: "Please do not submit your application for licensure until you have passed all
required exams" "and have met all other licensing requirements." DOPL's application page links its online application.

## What if I am already licensed in another state?

DOPL's application page describes an endorsement path. If your state holds a license DOPL considers equivalent, the page
asks for a "Verification of Licensure" from that state's licensing authority with the application; if not, it states:
"If the state you are licensed in shows no equivalent license to Utah, you will need to meet the licensing requirements
above." The page links a map of which states' licenses are treated as equivalent.

## Where to check before you act

These are the requirements as shown on DOPL's plumbing pages on the date above. DOPL is the authority, so confirm the
current hours, education rules, exams and forms on its site before you count hours, schedule an exam or apply. Renewal
and continuing education are on DOPL's separate continuing education page, which this post does not cover. These rules
describe Utah only; other states set their own. For other plumbing topics on goraedu, see our
[plumbing page](/plumbing/).
