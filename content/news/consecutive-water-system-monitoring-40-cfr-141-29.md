Some public water systems do not treat all of their own water: they receive finished water from another system and
deliver it through their own pipes. The practical question for a Drinking Water Operator is whether that
system still has monitoring duties under the federal drinking water rules, and what 40 CFR 141.29 allows. This
explainer reads 141.2, 141.3, 141.29 and a few provisions that name consecutive or wholesale systems, as published in
the eCFR on the date above. It does not describe a rule change.

## What is a consecutive system, and what is a wholesale system?

Both terms are defined in 141.2. A "Consecutive system is a public water system that receives some or all of its
finished water from one or more wholesale systems. Delivery may be through a direct connection or through the
distribution system of one or more consecutive systems."

A "Wholesale system is a public water system that treats source water as necessary to produce finished water and then
delivers some or all of that finished water to another public water system."

"Finished water" is water "intended for distribution and consumption without further treatment, except as treatment
necessary to maintain water quality in the distribution system" (141.2). Note the word "some": the definition also
covers a system that buys only part of its finished water.

## Does Part 141 apply to a system that only buys water?

Unless all four conditions below are met, yes. Section 141.3 says Part 141 "shall apply to each public water system, unless the public water system meets
all of the following conditions":

1. "Consists only of distribution and storage facilities (and does not have any collection and treatment facilities)";
2. "Obtains all of its water from, but is not owned or operated by, a public water system to which such regulations
   apply";
3. "Does not sell water to any person"; and
4. "Is not a carrier which conveys passengers in interstate commerce."

A consecutive system that sells water to its customers, for example, does not meet condition 3.

## What does 141.29 allow?

Section 141.29, titled "Monitoring of consecutive public water systems", reads in full:

"When a public water system supplies water to one or more other public water systems, the State may modify the
monitoring requirements imposed by this part to the extent that the interconnection of the systems justifies treating
them as a single system for monitoring purposes. Any modified monitoring shall be conducted pursuant to a schedule
specified by the State and concurred in by the Administrator of the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency."

So it is the State that "may modify" monitoring, only as far as the interconnection justifies, on a schedule EPA
concurs in. The section gives no automatic exemption.

In Part 141, "State" means, under 141.2, "the agency of the State or Tribal government which has jurisdiction over
public water systems", or the EPA Regional Administrator where the State or Tribe does not have primary enforcement
responsibility. So 141.29 does not itself take monitoring requirements away from a covered consecutive system; it lets the State
modify them. Ask the State what applies to your system.

## Who tells your customers when the wholesale system has a violation?

Section 141.201(c)(1) splits the job. "Public water systems that sell or otherwise provide drinking water to other
public water systems (i.e., to consecutive systems) are required to give public notice to the owner or operator of the
consecutive system; the consecutive system is responsible for providing public notice to the persons it serves."

## Who supplies the data for your Consumer Confidence Report?

Section 141.152(d) applies to community water systems: "A community water system that sells water to another community
water system must deliver the applicable information required in § 141.153 to the buyer system". The deadline is "By
April 1, 2027, and annually thereafter" or "On a date mutually agreed upon by the seller and the purchaser, and
specifically included in a contract between the parties". Section 141.152(d)(3) sets a separate October 1, 2027 date
for the information in 141.155(j) that a seller must give a buyer "that is required to provide reports biannually
according to § 141.155(i)". Our
[Consumer Confidence Report deadlines explainer](/news/drinking-water-consumer-confidence-report-deadlines/) covers a
community water system's own report delivery dates.

## Are there other consecutive-system rules?

Yes. Other Part 141 provisions also name consecutive or wholesale systems, and this post does not list them all. One example: under the Ground Water Rule, 141.402(a)(4)(i), a consecutive ground water system with a total
coliform-positive sample (collected under §§ 141.854 through 141.857 since April 1, 2016) "must notify the wholesale system(s) within 24 hours of being notified of the total
coliform-positive sample." What the wholesale system must do next is in our
[Ground Water Rule explainer](/news/ground-water-rule-triggered-source-water-monitoring-141-402/).
