For solar PV certification, the North American Board of Certified Energy Practitioners (NABCEP) lists the "NABCEP PV
Technical Sales Professional® (PVTS)" among its board certifications. NABCEP is a private certifying body, not a
government agency, and goraedu is independent of it. This explainer answers one practical question from NABCEP's own
Certification Handbook (the edition marked "2025 Update"): who can take the PVTS exam, what the exam is like, and how
the certification is kept. Nothing here describes a rule change.

## Is the PVTS certification a license?

No. The handbook's Disclaimer Statement says "NABCEP issues voluntary credentials" and states: "NABCEP credentials are
not professional licenses issued by a government agency and do not authorize a credential holder to practice." Whether a state or city requires its own license is a separate
question the handbook does not answer.

## What must every applicant do?

Section 9.1 opens: "To become board certified and maintain certification, the Applicant must:" The list includes "Be
at least 18 years of age", "Meet prerequisites of related experience and education as outlined below", "Sign and agree
to uphold the NABCEP Code of Ethics", completing an application, paying application and exam fees, and "Pass a written
examination". This post does not give fee amounts.

## What sales experience counts?

Section 9.2.1 asks for "Verifiable Sales Experience" in "PV installation systems sales-related activities". It adds: "At least half
(½) of the installations must have resulted in completed sales AND completed installations" and "No more than one (1)
project may be submitted to a relative or person residing at the applicant's address."

Section 9.3 counts experience in Sales Credits by system size: "System size of 1 - 24kW = 2 Sales Credits", "System
size of 25kW – 999kw = 3 Sales Credits" and "System size of 1MW and up = 4 Sales Credits".

## What are Category A and Category B?

- **Category A:** "Applicants must have 8 Sales Credits of verifiable sales experience with 100% of projects sold
  within the previous 2 year period with a minimum of 50% sold and installed".
- **Category B:** "Applicants must have 4 Sales Credits" under the same conditions, "AND hold a:" "Master’s or
  Bachelor’s degree in science or engineering from an accredited institution", or an "Associate’s degree in solar or
  renewable energy from an accredited institution", or a "Current Master or Journeyman Electrician’s License (expired
  licenses will not be accepted)", or a "Current State or government issued Solar Contractor’s License (expired licenses
  will not be accepted)".

## How is the experience documented?

Section 9.4 asks for "a signed letter on company letterhead from a member of senior management" "verifying the
Applicant’s role in the sale of the PV system and the Applicants experience in the field." Sections 9.4.1 and 9.4.2 list further project records, such as "a copy of Electrical and/or Building Permit(s) and Final Inspection Report(s)" and "the fully signed contract for the project."

## What training is required?

Section 9.5.1 requires proof of "a minimum of 10 hours of the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA)
Outreach Training Program for the Construction Industry, or government equivalent." Section 9.5.2 states: "A minimum of
fifty-eight (58) prescribed hours of advanced training is required for each candidate." Of these, "Forty (40) hours must
have been taken with an accredited institution and cover 'advanced' PV topics as outlined in the NABCEP PV Technical
Sales Job Task Analysis (PVTS JTA)", and "Eighteen (18) hours may be obtained from non-accredited, non-certified
sources". "All training must have been completed within the five (5) calendar years prior to the submission of the
application."

## What is the exam like?

Section 15 states: "Each certification examination (except System Inspector exams) consists of seventy (70) equally
weighted multiple-choice questions, with four (4) choices per question." "Sixty (60) of the questions are scored" and
"ten (10) of the questions are un-scored pilot questions." "The time limit for the examination is four (4) hours." The
handbook gives no separate length for PVTS. Section 17.2 lists the code book provided at the exam for
"(PVIP,PVDS,PVIS,PVCMS,PVSI)", which does not include PVTS.

Once approved, a candidate "is eligible to take the exam for a period of One (1) year from the date of application
approval" (Section 14.6).

## How is the certification kept?

Section 20.1: "Certification is valid for three (3) years following the date of issuance." Under Section 9.7,
"Certificants may submit their Recertification Application during the third year of their current certification." Section 9.7.1 asks for "30 hours of continuing education broken down into two
sections": "18 hours specific to the PVTS JTA" and "12 hours specific to Renewable Energy of which 2 hours must be
Building and/or Fire Codes". Section 9.7.2 asks for a letter "signed by their employer stating the Applicant is still
actively involved in the solar industry".

Section 21.1.2 offers an alternative for any certification: the certificant may "successfully complete" "the current
NABCEP Certification Examination related to their NABCEP certification" before the certification period ends.
