This explainer covers **Alabama only**, where home inspectors are licensed by the Division of Construction Management
(DCM). It answers one practical question from the DCM rules in the Alabama Administrative Code and the DCM renewal
form: what does it take to get and keep an Alabama home inspector license? It describes those pages as published on the
date above. Nothing here describes a rule change, and nothing here applies to any other state.

## Do you need a license to inspect homes in Alabama?

Yes. Rule 355-17-1.04 says: "All Home Inspectors and EIFS Inspectors shall have and maintain a current license issued
by the Division of Construction Management." This explainer is about the Home Inspector license only; the EIFS
Inspector license has its own requirements in Rule 355-17-1.07.

## What qualification do you need first?

Rule 355-17-1.06(1) opens with "To initially become a Home Inspector, one must:" and then lists the steps. The first,
in paragraph (1)(a), is to "Have one of the following four qualifications":

- membership in and adherence to the ethical standards of one of the professional bodies the rule lists, at the time
  of application;
- approval or certification by one of the agencies the rule lists "to inspect residential construction";
- "A high school diploma or its equivalent, work experience for at least one year as a home inspector, and completion
  of at least 100 home inspections for compensation"; or
- "Alabama licensure on or after January 1, 1998, as a general contractor, registered professional architect,
  registered professional engineer, or residential home builder."

## How much education and supervised inspection is required?

Paragraph (1)(b) asks for a combination of approved education and home inspections "under the supervision of a
qualified Home Inspector". The applicant must either:

- "Complete a minimum of 120 hours of approved home inspector education and participate in 35 home inspections", or
- "Complete a minimum of 35 hours of approved home inspector education and participate in 100 home inspections."

The rule adds that "Approved home inspector education shall include instruction on writing home inspection reports."
Approved courses are those approved by the professional bodies listed in the rule or by the Alabama Home Inspectors
Advisory Committee.

Paragraph (1)(d) also requires the applicant to "Draft Home Inspection reports for 25 home inspections. Ten of these
reports must be reviewed and corrected by a qualified Home Inspector."

## Which exams do you take?

Paragraph (1)(c) requires two written exams. The one on the Standards of Practice and ethics "will be the
American Society of Home Inspectors Standards and Ethics Examination", and the one on performing home inspections "will
be the Examination Board of Professional Home Inspectors National Home Inspectors Examination." The rule says "Both
tests must be completed within 18 months before submitting an application for a Home Inspector license" and that
applicants pay testing fees "directly to the organizations conducting the testing."

## What insurance is required?

Paragraph (1)(e) requires "bodily injury and property damage liability insurance covering the applicant’s home
inspection operations", with separate minimums for property damage, for any one person and for more than one person,
plus a minimum amount of errors and omissions coverage. The rule sets each amount; check the rule for the current
figures. Paragraph (1)(f) then asks for "an accurate, complete, and timely application for license with the Director,
along with the appropriate fee."

## How often is the license renewed?

Every year. Rule 355-17-1.04 says "A license is current only during the calendar year for which it is issued" and
"Unless renewed, a license automatically shall expire at the end of the calendar year for which it was issued."
Rule 355-17-1.08(2)(d) says renewal applications "must be filed with the Division of Construction Management by
November 1 of the year preceding the year for which the license is to be renewed", and those received after November 1
"may, in the sole discretion of the Director, be rejected for that reason alone." The renewal application includes "a
signed declaration that the licensee has completed the required continuing education."

Under Rule 355-17-1.08(2)(b), the licensee must also "Demonstrate continued compliance with the requirements found in
Rules 355-17-1-.06(1)(a) and (e)", that is, the qualification and the insurance. The DCM renewal form says "Proof of
insurance is REQUIRED to renew the license."

## How many continuing education hours are required?

For Home Inspector renewals (from the 2021 license year on), Rule 355-17-1.08(2)(a) says to "Complete 15 hours of approved continuing education per year." Licensees "must retain
proof of completion of this requirement for three years", and the DCM "will randomly audit licensees for proof that
continuing education was completed." The current DCM renewal form repeats: "For renewals, 15 hours of continuing education is
required."
