OSHA's construction rules include 29 CFR 1926.441, "Batteries and battery charging," in Subpart K (Electrical), under the heading
"Safety Requirements for Special Equipment." This explainer answers one practical question: what does OSHA require where
batteries are kept, handled or charged on a construction job? It reads the section in the eCFR (text current to
November 24, 2026). It is an explainer, not a rule change. This post covers only the federal OSHA rule and says nothing about any license or exam.

## Where can unsealed batteries be kept?

1926.441(a)(1) states: "Batteries of the unsealed type shall be located in enclosures with outside vents or in well
ventilated rooms and shall be arranged so as to prevent the escape of fumes, gases, or electrolyte spray into other
areas."

Only (a)(1) is limited to unsealed batteries; the other paragraphs do not name a battery type. The section does not
define "unsealed."

## How much ventilation is required?

The section sets a purpose, not a number. 1926.441(a)(2) states: "Ventilation shall be provided to ensure diffusion of
the gases from the battery and to prevent the accumulation of an explosive mixture."

## What about racks and floors?

- "Racks and trays shall be substantial and shall be treated to make them resistant to the electrolyte."
  (1926.441(a)(3))
- "Floors shall be of acid resistant construction unless protected from acid accumulations." (1926.441(a)(4))

## What protective equipment must be provided?

1926.441(a)(5) states: "Face shields, aprons, and rubber gloves shall be provided for workers handling acids or
batteries."

## How close must quick-drenching facilities be?

1926.441(a)(6) gives a distance: "Facilities for quick drenching of the eyes and body shall be provided within 25 feet
(7.62 m) of battery handling areas."

1926.441(a)(7) adds: "Facilities shall be provided for flushing and neutralizing spilled electrolyte and for fire
protection."

## Where can batteries be charged?

Paragraph (b) covers charging:

- "Battery charging installations shall be located in areas designated for that purpose." (1926.441(b)(1))
- "Charging apparatus shall be protected from damage by trucks." (1926.441(b)(2))
- "When batteries are being charged, the vent caps shall be kept in place to avoid electrolyte spray. Vent caps shall be
  maintained in functioning condition." (1926.441(b)(3))

## Is there a general industry rule too?

Yes, a shorter one. OSHA's general industry electrical rules are in Subpart S of Part 1910. Section 1910.305 covers
"Wiring methods, components, and equipment for general use," and its paragraph (j) is headed "Equipment for general
use." Within it, 1910.305(j)(7) reads: "Storage Batteries. Provisions
shall be made for sufficient diffusion and ventilation of gases from storage batteries to prevent the accumulation of
explosive mixtures."

Forklifts and other powered industrial trucks have their own paragraph, 1910.178(g), "Changing and charging storage
batteries," which starts with the same words as 1926.441(b)(1): "Battery charging installations shall be located in
areas designated for that purpose." Our
[forklift fuel, batteries and docks study guide](/forklift/forklift-fuel-batteries-docks/study-guide/) covers that
rule; this post does not.
