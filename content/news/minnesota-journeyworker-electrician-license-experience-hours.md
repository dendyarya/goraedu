Electrician licensing is set at the state or local level. This explainer covers **Minnesota only**,
where electrical licenses are issued by the Department of Labor and Industry (DLI). It answers one practical question
from DLI's own pages and the Minnesota Statutes: what experience leads to the Class A journeyworker electrician
license exam, and how does a worker without a license get that experience counted? Nothing here describes a rule
change, and nothing here applies to any other state.

## How much experience does the Class A journeyworker license require?

Four years. Minnesota Statutes section 326B.33, subdivision 2(b), states: "An applicant for a Class A journeyworker
electrician license shall have had at least four years of experience," and the statute says that experience must be
acceptable to the commissioner and in wiring for, installing and repairing electrical wiring, apparatus and equipment.

DLI's "Experience requirements - electrical license" page puts it in months: "Class A journeyworker
electrician, the minimum total is 48 months, including:"

## Which kinds of work count, and how much of each?

DLI's page splits the 48 months by type of work, with minimums and caps:

- Wiring for and installing electrical wiring, apparatus and equipment: at least 24 months, with up to 48 months of
  credit.
- Maintaining and repairing electrical wiring, apparatus and equipment: no minimum, up to 24 months of credit.
- Line work: up to 3 months of credit.
- Installing elevators: up to 6 months of credit.
- Wiring and maintaining technology circuits or systems: up to 6 months of credit.
- Wiring and maintaining process control circuits or systems: up to 12 months of credit.
- Planning, laying out and supervising installations: no credit.

By our arithmetic, that means at least half of the 48 months (24 of 48) has to be wiring and installing work.

## Is there experience that does not count?

Yes. DLI's page repeats credit limits from Minnesota Rules, part 3800.3520. For experience gained young, the credit is
listed as "before age 17, none;". The same list gives no credit ("none") for experience "acquired during the school term of a
department-approved two-year, post high-school electrical course".

The page adds that experience must be acceptable before the exam: "an applicant for an electrical license shall have
acceptable experience, as defined by subpart 4, before examination".

## Can school count toward the 48 months?

Partly. DLI's page states that "a maximum of one year of experience credit will be allowed for the successful
completion of a two-year, post high school" electrical course approved by the board. Section 326B.33 also allows that
one year of credit.

## Do you have to register while you gain experience?

Yes, if you do electrical work that requires a license. DLI's "Registered unlicensed electricians" page states:
"Must be registered as unlicensed individual if performing electrical work required to be performed by a licensed
individual." Section 326B.33, subdivision 13, states: "Unlicensed individuals performing electrical work for a
contractor or employer shall register with the department".

Registration matters for the exam. DLI's page states that "Electrical work experience earned after Jan. 1, 2009,
qualifies toward an individual's license examination" requirements only if the individual is registered. The statute
also denies credit for Minnesota work done after that date by an applicant who was not registered with or licensed by
the department.

## What supervision does a registered unlicensed individual need?

DLI's page states that a registered unlicensed individual "is limited to performing electrical work" under a list of conditions, including "under the supervision of an individual licensed to perform the electrical work" and "when the licensed and registered unlicensed individuals are employed by the same contractor or employer". Check the full list on DLI's page. It also states they are "Prohibited from performing electrical work without direct
supervision."

## How is experience reported?

Through the yearly registration renewal. On the registration term, DLI's page states: "One year; registration expires
on the last day of the month of the new registration." It adds: "Must report annual electrical work experience when
renewing registration." The statute states: "Registrations of unlicensed individuals expire one year from the date of
original issuance and every year thereafter."

For the exact documentation DLI asks for with an exam application, check DLI's own forms.

## What happens after you are licensed?

Under section 326B.33, subdivision 19, personal licenses other than those listed separately "expire two years from
the date of original issuance and every two years thereafter." DLI's "Continuing education - electrical" page lists
the row "Journeyworker electrician (AJ, BJ)" with 12 hours under "Code" and 4 under "Non Code," and states: "Licenses are not renewed unless continuing education credit are completed within the license term immediately preceding the license expiration date, or until hours of credit are earned and license renewal fees are paid."

## How this relates to OSHA's "qualified person"

A state electrician license is not the same thing as OSHA's federal "qualified person" training rules for workplace
electrical safety. Our earlier explainer covers that difference:

For the federal electrical safety rules themselves, see the [OSHA construction safety](/osha/) study material.
