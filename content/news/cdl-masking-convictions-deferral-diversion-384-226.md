A Commercial Driver's License (CDL) holder facing a traffic charge may hear about deferrals or diversion programs that
would keep a conviction off the CDLIS driver record, which 49 CFR 384.225 requires the State to keep for commercial
learner's permit (CLP) and CDL holders. Federal CDL rules speak to this directly in 49 CFR 384.226, "Prohibition on
masking convictions." This explainer reads that section, and the CDLIS driver record rules next to it, as they stand in
the eCFR. It does not describe a rule change, and it does not give legal advice about any charge or court case. The rule tells States what they must not do; it
does not decide any individual case.

## Who the rule is addressed to

Part 384 is written to the States, not to drivers. Section 384.101(a) gives its purpose: "to ensure that the States
comply with the provisions of section 12009(a) of the Commercial Motor Vehicle Safety Act of 1986 (49 U.S.C.
31311(a))." Under 384.101(b), the part "Includes the minimum standards for the actions States must take to be in
substantial compliance" with each of the 22 requirements of 49 U.S.C. 31311(a), and "Specifies the consequences of
State noncompliance."

## What 384.226 says

The whole section is one sentence:

"The State must not mask, defer imposition of judgment, or allow an individual to enter into a diversion program that
would prevent a CLP or CDL holder's conviction for any violation, in any type of motor vehicle, of a State or local
traffic control law (other than parking, vehicle weight, or vehicle defect violations) from appearing on the CDLIS
driver record, whether the driver was convicted for an offense committed in the State where the driver is licensed or
another State."

Read piece by piece, the sentence:

- **Covers three practices:** masking, deferring imposition of judgment, and diversion programs, when they "would
  prevent" the conviction from appearing on the CDLIS driver record.
- **Covers CLP and CDL holders,** that is, holders of a commercial learner's permit as well as a CDL.
- **Covers "any type of motor vehicle,"** so the rule is not limited to violations committed while driving a
  commercial motor vehicle.
- **Leaves out three kinds of violation:** "parking, vehicle weight, or vehicle defect violations."
- **Covers out-of-State convictions too:** "whether the driver was convicted for an offense committed in the State where
  the driver is licensed or another State."

## What counts as a conviction

Part 384 borrows the Part 383 definitions: "The definitions in part 383 of this title apply to this part, except where
otherwise specifically noted" (384.105(a)). Section 383.5 defines conviction broadly: "an unvacated adjudication of guilt, or a determination that a person has
violated or failed to comply with the law in a court of original jurisdiction or by an authorized administrative
tribunal, an unvacated forfeiture of bail or collateral deposited to secure the person's appearance in court, a plea of
guilty or nolo contendere accepted by the court, the payment of a fine or court cost, or violation of a condition of
release without bail, regardless of whether or not the penalty is rebated, suspended, or probated."

Under that definition, the payment of a fine or court cost counts as a conviction, and a conviction counts "regardless
of whether or not the penalty is rebated, suspended, or probated."

## What the State must post and keep

Section 384.225, "CDLIS driver recordkeeping," says what goes on the record. For a CLP or CDL holder, paragraph (a)(1)
requires the State to post and maintain "All convictions, disqualifications and other licensing actions for violations
of any State or local law relating to motor vehicle traffic control (other than parking, vehicle weight, or vehicle
defect violations) committed in any type of vehicle."

For "A person required to have a CLP or CDL," paragraph (b) requires the State to record and maintain convictions,
disqualifications and other licensing actions for such violations "committed while the driver was operating a CMV."

Paragraph (c) sets the timing: the information must be available to the authorized users "within 10 days of" receiving
the conviction or disqualification information from another State, or "The date of the conviction, if it occurred in
the same State."

Paragraph (d) sets how long it stays: the State must "Retain on the CDLIS driver record record all convictions,
disqualifications and other licensing actions for violations for at least 3 years or longer as required under §
384.231(d)." (The doubled word "record" is in the eCFR text.)

## Who can see the record

Paragraph (e) limits who may receive the information (the users or their authorized agents). States and the Secretary of Transportation get "All information
on all CDLIS driver records." The driver gets "All information on that driver's CDLIS driver record" obtained from the
State according to its procedures. A motor carrier or prospective motor carrier gets the driver's record "After
notification to a driver."
