Welding certification tests a welder's skill, but the machine still has to be wired to the building or jobsite power
supply, and OSHA's electrical rules say how it must be possible to cut that power. This explainer answers one practical
question: does each welding machine need its own disconnect switch? It reads OSHA's general industry rules (29 CFR Part
1910, Subparts Q and S) and construction rules (29 CFR Part 1926, Subparts J and K) as they stand in the eCFR (text
current to November 23, 2026). It is an explainer, not a rule change.

## The short answer

Yes, unless the machine has a disconnect built in, and in construction a motor-generator arc welder needs one even
then. 1910.306(d)(1) requires a disconnecting means in the supply circuit of each arc welder "not equipped with a
disconnect mounted as an integral part of the welder"; 1926.406(c)(1) uses the same exception only for AC transformer
and DC rectifier arc welders. Both rule sets require a switch or circuit breaker for each resistance welder. The details
differ by welder type and by whether the work is general industry or construction.

## Arc welders in general industry

29 CFR 1910.306(d)(1) states: "A disconnecting means shall be provided in the supply circuit for each arc welder that is
not equipped with a disconnect mounted as an integral part of the welder. The disconnecting means shall be a switch or
circuit breaker, and its rating may not be less than that necessary to accommodate overcurrent protection."

The welding section itself says the same in its own words. 29 CFR 1910.254(c)(3)(i) requires that "A disconnecting
switch or controller shall be provided at or near each welding machine which is not equipped with such a switch or
controller mounted as an integral part of the machine." It adds that the switch "shall be in accordance with subpart S
of this part," that "Overcurrent protection shall be provided as specified in subpart S of this part," and that "A
disconnect switch with overload protection or equivalent disconnect and protection means, permitted by subpart S of
this part, shall be provided for each outlet intended for connection to a portable welding machine."

## Resistance welders in general industry

For resistance welders, 1910.306(d)(2) states: "A switch or circuit breaker shall be
provided by which each resistance welder and its control equipment can be disconnected from the supply circuit. The
ampere rating of this disconnecting means may not be less than the supply conductor ampacity. The supply circuit switch
may be used as the welder disconnecting means where the circuit supplies only one welder."

So a resistance welder needs a disconnect for the welder and its control equipment, sized at least to the supply
conductors, but one switch can do both jobs when the circuit feeds a single welder.

## Does the rule apply to older installations?

The scope of the general industry rules is in 29 CFR 1910.302. Paragraph (b)(1) lists the requirements that apply to
all installations "regardless of when they were designed or installed," and 1910.306(d) is not on that list. Under
1910.302(b)(2), "Every electrical installation and all utilization equipment installed or overhauled after March 15,
1972, shall comply with the provisions of §§ 1910.302 through 1910.308," except as noted in paragraphs (b)(3) and
(b)(4); neither of those lists names 1910.306(d).

## On a construction site

The construction arc welding rule, 29 CFR 1926.351(d), ends with a cross-reference: "See § 1926.406(c) for additional
requirements." That paragraph sets the jobsite version:

- 1926.406(c)(1): "A disconnecting means shall be provided in the supply circuit for each motor-generator arc welder,
  and for each AC transformer and DC rectifier arc welder which is not equipped with a disconnect mounted as an integral
  part of the welder."
- 1926.406(c)(2): "A switch or circuit breaker shall be provided by which each resistance welder and its control
  equipment can be isolated from the supply circuit. The ampere rating of this disconnecting means shall not be less
  than the supply conductor ampacity."

Two differences from the general industry text stand out. In construction, a motor-generator arc welder needs a
disconnecting means in its supply circuit; the built-in-disconnect exception is worded only for AC transformer and DC
rectifier welders. And 1926.406(c)(2) does not contain the general industry sentence that lets the supply circuit
switch serve as the welder disconnect when the circuit supplies only one welder.

29 CFR 1926.402(a) sets the scope: these sections apply to "installations, both temporary and permanent, used on the
jobsite," but "do not apply to existing permanent installations that were in place before the construction activity
commenced." Under 1926.402(b), they do cover "portable and vehicle-mounted generators used to provide power for
equipment used at the jobsite."
