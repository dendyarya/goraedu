Bench grinders and portable grinders are common tools on construction sites, and OSHA's construction rules give them
their own section: 29 CFR 1926.303, "Abrasive wheels and tools," in Subpart I (Tools, Hand and Power). This explainer
answers a practical question: what does the rule require before and while a grinding wheel is used? It reads the
section as it stands in the eCFR (text current to November 23, 2026). It is an explainer, not a rule change: the eCFR
lists the section's most recent amendment at 58 FR 35175 (June 30, 1993). It covers construction work under Part 1926;
general industry workplaces have their own abrasive wheel rules in Part 1910.

## What has to happen before a wheel is mounted?

1926.303(c)(7) states: "All abrasive wheels shall be closely inspected and ring-tested before mounting to ensure that
they are free from cracks or defects."

The rule applies to "All abrasive wheels," not only to bench grinders. The section does not describe how a ring test is
done; for that and other details, 1926.303(d) points to the American National Standards Institute standard B7.1-1970
(see below).

Mounting also has two rules of its own in 1926.303(c)(8): "Grinding wheels shall fit freely on the spindle and shall not
be forced on. The spindle nut shall be tightened only enough to hold the wheel in place."

And 1926.303(a) requires that "All grinding machines shall be supplied with sufficient power to maintain the spindle
speed at safe levels under all conditions of normal operation."

## What guard does a bench or floor grinder need?

1926.303(c)(1) requires floor stand and bench mounted abrasive wheels used for external grinding to have safety guards
(protection hoods), and limits how much of the wheel may be exposed:

- "The maximum angular exposure of the grinding wheel periphery and sides shall be not more than 90°, except that when
  work requires contact with the wheel below the horizontal plane of the spindle, the angular exposure shall not exceed
  125°."
- "In either case, the exposure shall begin not more than 65° above the horizontal plane of the spindle."
- "Safety guards shall be strong enough to withstand the effect of a bursting wheel."

Under 1926.303(b)(2), the guard "shall cover the spindle end, nut, and flange projections," must be mounted to keep
proper alignment with the wheel, and "the strength of the fastenings shall exceed the strength of the guard." The stated
exceptions only allow the spindle end, nut and outer flange to be exposed: where the work provides a suitable measure of
protection to the operator (and the guard's side covers may be omitted where the work entirely covers the side of the
wheel), and on machines designed as portable saws.

## How close must the work rest be?

1926.303(c)(2) states that floor and bench-mounted grinders "shall be provided with work rests which are rigidly
supported and readily adjustable. Such work rests shall be kept at a distance not to exceed one-eighth inch from the
surface of the wheel."

1926.303(e) adds, for offhand grinding machines, that work rests must be "designed to be adjustable to compensate for
wheel wear" and kept "adjusted closely to the wheel with a maximum opening of 1/8 inch (0.3175 cm) to prevent the work
from being jammed between the wheel and the rest, which may cause wheel breakage." Two more rules follow: "The work
rest shall be securely clamped after each adjustment. The adjustment shall not be made with the wheel in motion."

## Do portable grinders need guards?

Yes, with stated exceptions. Under 1926.303(c)(3):

- **Cup type wheels** used for external grinding are protected by "either a revolving cup guard or a band type guard"
  in accordance with ANSI B7.1-1970.
- **All other portable abrasive wheels** used for external grinding need safety guards (protection hoods). When guards
  are required, 1926.303(c)(5) says they must keep proper alignment, be strong enough "to retain fragments of the wheel
  in case of accidental breakage," and "The maximum angular exposure of the grinding wheel periphery and sides shall not
  exceed 180°."
- **Exceptions:** safety flanges are used instead "When the work location makes it impossible" to use a guard, and no
  guard is required "When wheels 2 inches or less in diameter which are securely mounted on the end of a steel mandrel
  are used."

For **internal grinding**, 1926.303(c)(4) requires safety flanges (protection flanges), except for wheels 2 inches or
less in diameter securely mounted on the end of a steel mandrel, and "If the wheel is entirely within the work being
ground while in use." Under (c)(6), safety flanges may be used "only with wheels designed to fit the flanges."

## Is eye protection required?

Yes. 1926.303(c)(9) states: "All employees using abrasive wheels shall be protected by eye protection equipment in
accordance with the requirements of subpart E of this part, except when adequate eye protection is afforded by eye
shields which are permanently attached to the bench or floor stand."

## What does the ANSI standard add?

1926.303(b)(1) and (d) incorporate ANSI B7.1-1970, "Safety Code for the Use, Care and Protection of Abrasive Wheels":
guards must conform to it, and "All abrasive wheels and tools used by employees shall meet other applicable requirements"
of that standard. This explainer covers only what 1926.303 itself states; it does not summarize the ANSI standard.

## What this means for OSHA study

The points to know from 1926.303 are: inspect and ring-test every abrasive wheel before mounting; the wheel fits freely
and is never forced on; guards on floor stand and bench mounted wheels used for external grinding
expose no more than 90° (125° when work is below the spindle's
horizontal plane), starting no more than 65° above it; portable wheel guards expose no more than 180°; work rests stay
within 1/8 inch of the wheel and are never adjusted with the wheel in motion; portable wheels 2 inches or less in diameter securely mounted on the end of a
steel mandrel are exempt from the guard rule (external grinding) and the safety-flange rule (internal grinding); and eye protection is required unless a permanent eye shield gives adequate
protection.
