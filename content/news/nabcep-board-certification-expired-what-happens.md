The North American Board of Certified Energy Practitioners (NABCEP) runs solar PV board certifications such as PV
Installation Professional (PVIP). NABCEP is a private certifying body, not a government agency, and goraedu
is independent of it. This explainer uses NABCEP's Certification Handbook ("2025 Update") to answer one question: what happens if the three-year period ends before you recertify? Nothing here
describes a rule change.

## When does a board certification expire?

Section 20.1 of the handbook states: "Certification is valid for three (3) years following the date of issuance."
Section 21.1.3 explains the cycle: "Each recertification cycle will be a three (3) year period," and "The first
recertification cycle begins on the date of initial certification". You do not have to work the date out yourself:
"The expiration date is printed on the NABCEP Certificate and is listed in each Certificant's account on myNABCEP."

NABCEP also sends reminders. Section 21.15.4 says certificants "are sent recertification notices by email to the email
address listed in their myNABCEP account on nabcep.certemy.com at one (1) year, six (6) months, three (3) months, one
(1) month and the day before their certification expires."

## When should you apply?

Section 21.1.4 opens the window in the last year: "Certificants may submit their Recertification Application during the
third year of their current certification."

The same section recommends a buffer: certificants "are encouraged to file a Recertification Application and pay the
Recertification Fee at least ninety (90) days prior to the expiration of their certification." It also puts the
record-keeping on you. Section 21.15.3 states: "NABCEP does not track Certificant's Continuing Education credits."

## What happens on the expiration date?

Section 21.16 states that certificants "who fail to satisfy the recertification requirements prior to the conclusion
of the current certification cycle will become inactive and will be removed from the NABCEP professional directory."
It adds: "Once a Certificant has expired they are not certified by NABCEP nor can they claim to be certified."
Section 21.1.4 says the directory removal is immediate: expired certificants "are removed from the Professional
Directory immediately upon expiration of the certification."

Section 21.16.2 limits how the credential may be used after that. A certificant "who is expired or whose certification
has been suspended or revoked may not represent himself or herself as an active Certificant or as certified by NABCEP
and may not use the NABCEP Certification Mark" until NABCEP gives notice "that the relevant recertification
requirements have been satisfied or their active certification status has been reinstated".

## Can you still recertify after it expires?

Yes, for one year. Section 21.1.4 states: "Expired Certificants may renew their certification at any time within one
(1) year following their certification expiration date." During that "expiration period", "an individual will be
permitted to recertify without having to re-apply or re-exam."

Two conditions come with it. First, the year is not a grace period in which you stay certified: "During this one-year
period, the Certificant is not certified by NABCEP nor can they claim to be certified." Second, it costs more:
certificants "who fail to recertify before their certification expires will be required to pay a Recertification Late
Fee in addition to the Recertification Fee". The handbook points to NABCEP's website for the fee; this post does not
list fee amounts.

Renewing later in the cycle does not move the date. Section 21.1.4: "The Certificant's expiration date will not
change regardless of when in the renewal cycle the individual meets recertification requirements." It continues:
"Certificants renewing within the first, second, or third year of the renewal period, or prior to expiration, will all
have their new expiration date set as three years from the previous expiration date."

## What if more than a year has passed?

Then the shortcut is gone. Section 21.1.4: "Once the one-year certification renewal period has ended, expired
Certificants must re-apply and re-exam in order to become certified again." Section 21.16.1 adds that they "must
reapply and meet all current certification requirements, including passing the appropriate NABCEP exam."

## Is there another way to recertify?

Section 21.1.2 offers one for the continuing education part: "As an alternative to meeting CE requirements for any
certification, the Certificant may schedule, and successfully complete, the current NABCEP Certification Examination
related to their NABCEP certification."

## Which credentials does this cover?

The handbook says it "contains information about NABCEP's board certification programs", and its list of board
certifications includes PVIP and PV System Inspector (PVSI). For PVSI, Section 21.1.4 has its own paragraph with
similar rules: expired System Inspectors "may renew their certification 1 year following their credential expiration
date", are "not credentialed by NABCEP" during that time, and after that year "must re-apply and re-exam in order to
become credentialed again" (see our
[PVSI post](/news/nabcep-pv-system-inspector-pvsi-eligibility-exam-recertification/)). The PV Associate is not on
that list; NABCEP's handbook page links a separate Associate Handbook (see our
[PV Associate post](/news/nabcep-pv-associate-credential-who-can-take-the-exam/)).
What recertification itself takes is in our
[PVIP recertification post](/news/nabcep-pv-installation-professional-recertification-every-three-years/).
