This explainer covers NABCEP's own program rules as published on the date checked above. The North American Board of
Certified Energy Practitioners (NABCEP) is a private organization, not a government agency. Its Certification Handbook
says: "NABCEP is a non-profit credentialing organization." goraedu is independent and is not affiliated with or
endorsed by NABCEP.

The practical question: what must you document before NABCEP lets you sit for its PV Installer Specialist (PVIS)
exam? Nothing here describes a rule change.

NABCEP's Board Certifications page says the PVIS "was designed for those who are highly competent with all aspects of
PV installation processes". For eligibility, that page links to Section 6 of the Certification Handbook, whose cover
is marked "2025 Update". The facts below come from that section unless another one is named.

## Does the PVIS replace a state license?

No. The handbook states: "NABCEP credentials are not professional licenses issued by a government agency and do not
authorize a credential holder to practice." Section 6 calls the PVIS "a voluntary certification" and adds: "It is not
intended to prevent qualified individuals from selling, servicing, or installing PV systems or to replace state
license requirements." Any electrical or contractor license your state requires is a separate matter.

## What are the basic requirements?

Section 6.1 lists them. An applicant must "Be at least 18 years of age", "Meet prerequisites of related experience
and/or education as outlined below", "Complete an application form documenting all requirements", "Sign and agree to
uphold the NABCEP Code of Ethics" and "Pass a written examination". It also lists paying NABCEP's application and exam
fees; this post does not restate amounts.

## What has to be documented to qualify?

Section 6.3 says every applicant, "regardless of background, education, experience or geographical location," must
document three items:

- "Completion of a minimum of 10 hours of OSHA Outreach Training Program for the Construction Industry training
  (or government equivalent)"
- "Completion of 24 hours of advanced training as defined in Section 6.7.2 below"
- "Completion of installations with a decision-making role equaling at least 6 Project Credits"

Credits depend on system size: "System size of 1 – 999kW = 2 Project Credits" and "System size of 1MW and up = 3
Project Credits". By our arithmetic, three installations of 1 to 999 kW, or two of 1 MW and up, would reach the 6-credit minimum.

## Which installations count?

Section 6.2 sets the minimum criteria. "The Applicant shall have performed in a decision-making role, which had
material impact on the installation." Each installation "must have been completed within the two (2) calendar years
prior to the application submission date", and the systems must have "a minimum rating of 1 kW DC (STC)."

## How do you prove each installation?

For every system, Section 6.4.1 asks for a short system summary: its location, completion date, array size and
inverter details, and contact names and phone numbers for the installation contractor, the contractor on the permit or
inspection, and your supervisor or manager.

Section 6.5 asks for "a copy of Electrical and/or Building Permit(s) and Final Inspection Report(s)" from the local
Authority Having Jurisdiction. Where permits and inspections are not issued, an inspection report from one of the
independent inspectors the section names is required instead. It states: "Inspections may not be completed by the
Applicant, Installation Contractor or customer." Its note adds that NABCEP "cannot accept site plans, drawings and/or
permit applications in lieu of the actual approved permits".

For the decision-making role, Section 6.6 requires "a physically signed letter on company letterhead from their
supervisor/manager." The letter must list the job addresses and "clearly explain the role(s) and the decision-making
authority the Applicant held for the system installation."

## What training counts toward the 24 hours?

Section 6.7.2 says: "All training must have been completed in the five (5) calendar years prior to the submission of
the application." The 24 hours are "18 hours of advanced PV installation principles and practices" based on NABCEP's
PVIS Job Task Analysis, plus "6 hours of the National Electric Code (NEC)". For the 18 hours, the handbook says:
"Course(s) must have been taken with an accredited institution."

Training needs "a formal training format, with a teacher-learner structure", and hours are counted as "contact hours"
between teacher and learner. Accepted provider types listed include "Institutions accredited by an agency recognized by
the federal Department of Education, or government equivalent" and "U.S. Department of Labor Registered Apprenticeship Training Programs". The
section rules out courses from private organizations or businesses that are not accredited. Under Section 6.9, each
course is documented with a certificate of completion or a transcript.

Section 6.8 sets out a separate PV Installer Specialist Veterans Pathway; read it directly if it applies to you.

## How long does the certification last?

Section 2.4 states: "Certification is valid for three (3) years from the date of issuance."
