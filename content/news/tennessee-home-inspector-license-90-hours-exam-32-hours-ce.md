This explainer covers **Tennessee only**, where home inspectors are licensed through the Home Inspector Licensing Program of the Tennessee Department of Commerce
and Insurance. It answers one practical question from four of the program's own web pages: what does Tennessee ask
for to get a home inspector license, and what does it ask for at renewal? Nothing here describes a rule change, and
nothing here applies to any other state.

The program's license page explains why the license exists: "The Home Inspector Licensing Program was enacted on April
18, 2005, to ensure that only qualified persons are licensed home inspectors, and that the licensed inspector offers
Tennessee Consumers a professional, educated opinion of the condition of their homes."

## Who can apply?

The license page lists a minimum age: "Be at least eighteen (18) years of age." Applicants apply online through the
"Initial Home Inspector License" application at core.tn.gov.

## What education is required?

The page lists two education items: a "Copy of high school or GED certificate", and "Successful completion of ninety
(90) hours of commissioner-approved training on home inspections and the preparation of home inspection reports."
The approved courses are listed by the program on its education page. The pages used here do not say how the 90 hours
must be split between topics.

## Which exam, and how long does a pass count?

The license page asks for "proof of passage of the state-selected National Home Inspector Examination (NHIE)" and
states: "Exam scores are valid for five years." The program's exam page says the same in longer form: "The exams are
valid for five years from the date of completion for licensure purposes." The exam page sends applicants to the NHIE's
own site to register. The pages do not state a passing score.

## What insurance must an applicant show?

The license page asks for two kinds of coverage, shown on a certificate in the standard Accord format: "Coverage for
General Liability must include a minimum policy coverage", with the minimum amount stated on the page, and "Coverage
for Errors and Omissions (E&O)." For errors and omissions, the page states: "There are no minimum limits specified in
the statute, so you may set your own policy limits." It adds that E&O "Coverage must be valid and current at the time
the application is submitted."

The page also sets details for the certificate itself:

- "In either the Insured or Description of Operations section, list the applicant's first and last name."
- "It must have a valid policy number, as TBA is not accepted."
- The certificate holder must be the "Tennessee Home Inspector Licensing Program" at the address the page gives.

## What if you are already licensed in another state?

The same page describes a reciprocity path. The applicant must "Provide a Letter of Good Standing (or similar
documentation)" from the state where they hold an active license, and show either "90 hours of commissioner-approved
Qualifying Education (QE)" or "32 hours of commissioner-approved Continuing Education (CE)", along with a passed NHIE
and the same insurance as a new applicant.

## What does renewal require?

The renewal page's section is headed "How to Renew an Active License (Within 30 Days of Expiration)." It asks the
licensee to "Provide a certification of completion for thirty-two (32) hours of commissioner-approved Continuing
Education" and warns: "A transcript of credits cannot be accepted." The education page ties the same 32 hours to
Rule 0780-05-12-.05(2). Renewal also asks to "Provide proof of insurance in the standard Accord format."

The renewal page also states that anyone seeking renewal "more than sixty (60) days after the
expiration date of the license is required to reapply for licensure", meeting all initial licensure requirements again.
It also states: "There is no retired status for Home Inspectors."

The pages used here do not state how many years a license lasts before it must be renewed, which topics count as
continuing education, or whether extra hours carry over.

## Can a license be put on hold?

Yes. The renewal page describes an inactive status. An inactive licensee "may not directly or indirectly engage in or
conduct or advertise" home inspection business, and "No continuing education shall be required for renewal of an
inactive license." To return to active status, the page asks for current insurance, a sworn statement that the licensee did not
violate the Act or its rules while inactive, and, "If more than two years have passed since the license was placed in
an inactive status," 32 hours of commissioner-approved continuing education completed in the
24 months before the application.

## Where to check before you act

These are the requirements as shown on the Tennessee Home Inspector Licensing Program's pages on the date above. The
Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance is the authority, so confirm the current rules, forms and fees with the
program before you enroll in a course, book the exam or apply. These requirements describe Tennessee only; other states
set their own. For the federal lead disclosure and radon topics that home inspectors meet, see our
[home inspector topics](/home-inspector/).
