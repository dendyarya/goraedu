For solar PV certification, the North American Board of Certified Energy Practitioners (NABCEP) lists the "NABCEP PV
System Inspector™ (PVSI)" among its board certifications. NABCEP is not a government agency; goraedu is
independent of it. This explainer answers one practical question from NABCEP's own Certification Handbook (the
edition marked "2025 Update"): who can take the PVSI exam, what the exam is like, and how the certification is kept.
Nothing here describes a rule change.

## What the PVSI certification is, and what it is not

Section 10 of the handbook calls it "a voluntary certification" and states: "It is not intended to replace state
license requirements." Whether a state or city requires its own license or registration to inspect PV systems is a
separate question that the handbook does not answer.

## Are there prerequisites?

Section 10.2 states: "There are no specific pre-requisites to taking a NABCEP PV System Inspector Exam."

It adds what the exam assumes: "The Exams are intended to test the knowledge of someone who is familiar with PV
systems." "Knowledge of applicable codes and ordinances is presumed." Applicants "should know how to assess the safety
and operation of a system, be able to verify code compliance via interpretation of design plans and building
documents, conduct on-site inspections, and report results."

Section 10.1 opens: "To become certified and maintain certification, the Applicant must:"

- "Be at least 18 years of age"
- "Complete an application"
- "Sign and agree to uphold the NABCEP Code of Ethics"
- "Pay Application and Examination Fees to NABCEP"
- "Pass a written examination"
- "Once certified, complete continuing education and experience requirements, submit a recertification application,
  and pay a recertification fee, within the three (3) year recertification timeframe"

This post does not give fee amounts.

## How long do I have to take the exam after I apply?

Section 14.4 says an applicant "will receive notice of eligibility or ineligibility to sit for the certification
examination within thirty (30) days of submission." Once accepted, a System Inspector candidate is eligible to take
the exam "for a period of One (1) Year from the date their application is approved" (Section 14.6). "If a Candidate
does not take the exam before the one year expires, they must complete a new application and pay the exam fee to take
the exam."

## What is the exam like?

Section 15 states: "The System Inspector certification exams consist of forty-five (45) equally weighted
multiple-choice questions, with four (4) choices per question. Forty (40) of the questions are scored and five (5) of
the questions are un-scored pilot questions." The pilot questions are not identified. "The time limit for the System
Inspector examination is two (2) hours."

Section 17.2 ("What is Provided at the Exam") lists, for PVSI, the "2017 National Electrical Code, NFPA 70" and a "Basic
scientific calculator on-screen during the exam."

## What if I do not pass?

Retakes work differently for inspectors. Section 19.8 gives the general retake rule for "All Candidates, except System
Inspectors," and then states: "System Inspector candidates who do not pass their System Inspector Exam may retake the
exam by submitting another application through https://nabcep.certemy.com/".

## How long is the certification valid?

Section 20.1: "Certification is valid for three (3) years following the date of issuance."

Section 10.3 sets the PVSI recertification rules. "Certificants may submit their Recertification Application during
the third (3rd) year of their current certification period," electronically through their myNABCEP account. The
continuing education rule is that applicants must complete "at least 8 hours of continuing education related to the
National Electric Code (NEC) over the course of their 3-year certification cycle." (The handbook writes "National
Electric Code" here.)

Section 21.1.2 adds an alternative that applies to any NABCEP certification: instead of meeting the continuing
education requirement, the certificant "may schedule, and successfully complete, the current NABCEP Certification
Examination related to their NABCEP certification"; the handbook adds that a passing score is required.

## What if my PVSI certification expires?

Section 21.1.4 says individuals with expired System Inspector certifications "may renew their certification 1 year
following their credential expiration date." During that year, "the System Inspector is not credentialed by NABCEP,
nor may claim to be so," and expired inspectors are removed from NABCEP's online Professional Directory immediately upon expiration. Someone who meets the
continuing education requirements in that period may recertify without re-examination by paying a recertification
fee plus a late fee. After that year, "expired System Inspectors must re-apply and re-exam in order to become
credentialed again."

## Where federal safety rules fit

NABCEP's certification is separate from federal OSHA electrical rules, which apply on the job (29 CFR 1910 Subpart S
in general industry and 29 CFR 1926 Subpart K in construction). Our study guides quote them:
[general industry electrical](/osha-general-industry/electrical/study-guide/) and
[construction electrical](/osha/electrical/study-guide/). goraedu's
[solar PV page](/solar-pv/) covers the OSHA construction safety rules for installation work; it does not cover NABCEP
exams.
