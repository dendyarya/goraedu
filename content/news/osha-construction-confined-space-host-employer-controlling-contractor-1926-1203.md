A construction site often has several employers on it at once, and a manhole, tank or transformer vault can be a
confined space (the note to 1926.1201(a) lists them among its examples), some of which are permit-required. This explainer answers one practical question: under OSHA's construction rules, which employer must find
these spaces, and who must tell whom about their hazards? The answer is in 29 CFR 1926.1203, "General requirements," in
Subpart AA (Confined Spaces in Construction), read in the eCFR (text current to November 24, 2026). It is an explainer,
not a rule change.

## Where does Subpart AA apply?

Section 1926.1201(a) says the standard protects "employees engaged in construction activities at a worksite with one or
more confined spaces", subject to the exceptions in paragraph (b). Paragraph (b) excludes construction work regulated by Subpart P (Excavations), Subpart S
(Underground Construction, Caissons, Cofferdams and Compressed Air) and Subpart Y (Diving).

## Who are the three employers?

Section 1926.1202 defines them:

- **Host employer:** "the employer that owns or manages the property where the construction work is taking place."
- **Controlling contractor:** "the employer that has overall responsibility for construction at the worksite."
- **Entry employer:** "any employer who decides that an employee it directs will enter a permit space."

## Who must identify confined spaces?

Every employer. Section 1926.1203(a) states: "Before it begins work at a worksite, each employer must ensure that a
competent person identifies all confined spaces in which one or more of the employees it directs may work, and identifies
each space that is a permit space, through consideration and evaluation of the elements of that space, including testing
as necessary."

If there are permit spaces, the employer that identifies or receives notice of one must, under 1926.1203(b), inform
exposed employees "by posting danger signs or by any other equally effective means," and inform "its employees'
authorized representatives and the controlling contractor" in a timely manner and "in a manner other than posting," in
both cases "of the existence and location of, and the danger posed by, each permit space." An employer that identifies,
or receives notice of, a permit space and has not authorized the employees it directs to work in that space must "take
effective measures to prevent those employees from entering that permit space" (1926.1203(c)). An employer that decides
employees it directs will enter a permit space must have a written permit space program that complies with 1926.1204
implemented at the construction site, available for inspection by employees and their authorized representatives before
and during entry operations (1926.1203(d)).

## Who passes information to whom before entry?

Paragraph (h), "Permit space entry communication and coordination," sets the chain:

1. **Host employer to controlling contractor.** Before entry operations begin, the host employer must provide, "if it has
   it," the location of each known permit space, the hazards or potential hazards in each space "or the reason it is a
   permit space," and any precautions that the host employer or a previous controlling contractor or entry employer
   implemented (1926.1203(h)(1)).
2. **Controlling contractor to everyone affected.** The controlling contractor must obtain the host employer's
   information and give it, plus any additional information it has on those subjects and the precautions that the
   host employer, the controlling contractor or other entry employers implemented, "to each
   entity entering a permit space and any other entity at the worksite whose activities could foreseeably result in a
   hazard in the permit space" (1926.1203(h)(2)).
3. **Entry employer to controlling contractor.** Each entry employer must obtain all of the controlling
   contractor's information on permit space hazards and entry operations and "Inform the controlling contractor of the permit space program that the entry employer will follow,
   including any hazards likely to be confronted or created in each permit space" (1926.1203(h)(3)).

## When must entries be coordinated?

Under 1926.1203(h)(4), the controlling contractor and entry employers must coordinate entry operations when "More than
one entity performs permit space entry at the same time," or when entry "is performed at the same time that any
activities that could foreseeably result in a hazard in the permit space are performed."

## What happens after entry?

Paragraph (h)(5) closes the loop: the controlling contractor "must debrief each entity that entered a permit space regarding the permit space program
followed and any hazards confronted or created in the permit space(s) during entry operations," the
entry employer "must inform the controlling contractor in a timely manner" of the program followed and any hazards
confronted or created, and the controlling contractor "must apprise the host employer of the information exchanged with the entry entities".

A note to paragraph (h) states that unless a host employer or controlling contractor "has or will have employees in a
confined space, it is not required to enter any confined space to collect the information specified in this paragraph
(h)."

## What if there is no controlling contractor?

Section 1926.1203(i) states that the controlling contractor's role "must be fulfilled by the host employer or other
employer who arranges to have employees of another employer perform work that involves permit space entry."
