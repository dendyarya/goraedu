This explainer covers **Maine only**, where plumbing licenses are issued by the Plumbers' Examining Board. It answers one practical question from the board's own pages and Maine's
plumbing law: what hours, training and exam lead to a journeyman plumber license in Maine? These are Maine's rules
only, nothing here describes a rule change, and nothing here applies to any other state.

## What are the two experience routes?

The board's Individual Licenses page lists, under the journeyman plumber license's examination requirements, two
experience routes:

- **Trainee route:** "A minimum of at least two years with 4,000 hours of work in the field of plumbing installations"
  "as a licensed trainee plumber under the supervision of a licensed master plumber".
- **Journeyman-in-training route:** 2,000 hours of plumbing installation work as a licensed journeyman-in-training
  under a licensed master plumber, "as long as the work experience is obtained within four years" "of the date upon
  which the applicant was issued a journeyman-in-training license."

Maine's statute, 32 M.R.S. §3501, subsection 2, sets out the same two routes. For the trainee route it requires "A
minimum of at least 2 years with 4,000 hours of work in the field of plumbing installations" and "obtaining a passing
grade as determined by the board on the journeyman's examination".

## How do I start as a trainee plumber?

Through a job with a licensed master plumber. Under §3501, subsection 3, "The board may issue a trainee plumber license
without examination" to a person who submits a written application "and who provides satisfactory evidence that the
person is employed by a licensed master plumber", or by an employer of one. On the board's Individual Licenses page,
the requirement listed for the trainee license is a "Completed Application".

## What is a journeyman-in-training license?

It is a separate license for people who finished a plumbing course and already passed the journeyman exam. The board's
Individual Licenses page lists the requirements:

- "Completion of a plumbing course consisting of one year or 2 semesters at a board-approved community college; or"
- "Completion of a two-year plumbing course at a career and technical education program" approved under Maine's
  education law as a secondary student, "or in a registered Department of Labor apprenticeship program; and"
- "A passing grade of 70% (or higher) on the journeyman plumbers examination."

The statute (§3501, subsection 2-B) describes the course as "a plumbing course consisting of one year or 2 semesters at
a board-approved technical college or community college", or in an apprenticeship or career and technical education
program, and it names no separate two-year length for those programs. Because the board page and the statute word the
program length differently, check with the board if you are unsure whether your program counts.

## How long does the journeyman-in-training license last?

Four years, and only once. The board page calls it a "4 year non-renewable license". The statute says: "A
journeyman-in-training license is valid for a single nonrenewable period of 4 years" "and may be issued only once to
any individual." The 2,000 work hours for the journeyman license have to fall within that four-year window.

The board's Journeyman-in-Training FAQ, under "When and how can I apply for a journeyman license?", states: "As a
licensed Journeyman-in-Training you will need to work for one year and obtained 2000 hours of work experience." The
application includes an affidavit from your supervising master plumber "attesting to the work experience and work
hours completed." The statute's wording names the 2,000 hours and the four-year limit; the FAQ also mentions one year
of work, so ask the board how it counts your time.

## What score do I need on the exam, and when can I book it?

The board's Journeyman Plumbers FAQ answers "Do I need to pass a test?" with: "Yes. You must get 70% correct in order to
pass." The Journeyman-in-Training FAQ uses the same 70% figure. Both FAQ pages say you need the board's approval first:
"You must receive approval from the Board prior to scheduling your exam."

## How long is a journeyman license good for?

The board's Individual Licenses page gives the term of the journeyman plumber license as "Every two years based on
first licensure date." Renewal fees and late rules are on that page; this explainer does not list them.
