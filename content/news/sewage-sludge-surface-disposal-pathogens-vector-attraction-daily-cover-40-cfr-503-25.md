Subpart C of the federal sewage sludge rule, 40 CFR Part 503, covers surface disposal. A practical question for anyone
who runs or supplies a disposal unit is whether the sludge must be treated to reduce pathogens and vector attraction
before it is placed there, and what daily cover has to do with it. The answer is in 40 CFR 503.25, "Operational
standards—pathogens and vector attraction reduction", which points to the options in 503.32 and 503.33. This explainer
reads the eCFR text (latest version in the eCFR versioner as of November 13, 2026, checked November 18, 2026); it is not a rule change. Your State program and your permit may add
requirements of their own.

## Which sludge and which units does 503.25 cover?

Section 503.25 applies when sludge is "placed on an active sewage sludge unit". Section 503.21 defines the terms:
"Active sewage sludge unit is a sewage sludge unit that has not closed", and "Sewage sludge unit is land on which only
sewage sludge is placed for final disposal. This does not include land on which sewage sludge is either stored or
treated."

The section treats sewage sludge and domestic septage separately. Section 503.9(f) defines domestic septage as "either
liquid or solid material removed from a septic tank, cesspool, portable toilet, Type III marine sanitation device, or
similar treatment works that receives only domestic sewage." The same paragraph excludes material from a tank or
similar works that receives commercial or industrial wastewater, and grease removed from a restaurant grease trap.

## Must the sludge meet a pathogen class?

For sewage sludge other than domestic septage, 503.25(a) states: "The Class A pathogens requirements in § 503.32(a) or
one of the Class B pathogen requirements in § 503.32 (b)(2) through (b)(4) shall be met when sewage sludge is placed on
an active sewage sludge unit, unless the vector attraction reduction requirement in § 503.33(b)(11) is met."

So the sludge must be Class A or Class B, unless the daily cover option in 503.33(b)(11) is used. The three Class B
alternatives in 503.32(b)(2) through (b)(4) are a fecal coliform test on "Seven representative samples", treatment in
one of the "Processes to Significantly Reduce Pathogens described in appendix B of this part", or treatment in an
equivalent process "as determined by the permitting authority."

Note that the Class B site restrictions in 503.32(b)(5) (crop and turf harvesting, grazing and public access waiting periods) are
written for sludge "applied to the land" (503.32(b)(1)(ii)); 503.25(a) does not refer to them.

## What is the daily cover option?

Section 503.33(b)(11) states: "Sewage sludge placed on an active sewage sludge unit shall be covered with soil or other
material at the end of each operating day." Section 503.21 defines cover as "soil or other material used to cover sewage
sludge placed on an active sewage sludge unit."

Daily cover does two jobs in 503.25. It is one of the vector attraction reduction options, and when it is met, the
pathogen class requirement in 503.25(a) does not apply.

## Which vector attraction options can be used?

For sewage sludge other than domestic septage, 503.25(b) states: "One of the vector attraction reduction requirements in
§ 503.33 (b)(1) through (b)(11) shall be met when sewage sludge is placed on an active sewage sludge unit." Section
503.33(a)(4) says the same. Those eleven options include, among others:

- (b)(1): volatile solids "reduced by a minimum of 38 percent";
- (b)(6): pH raised "to 12 or higher by alkali addition" and held as the paragraph describes;
- (b)(9): injection, where "Sewage sludge shall be injected below the surface of the land";
- (b)(10): incorporation into the soil "within six hours after application to or placement on the land, unless otherwise
  specified by the permitting authority";
- (b)(11): daily cover, quoted above.

Read 503.33(b) in full for the conditions and test details of each option.

## What about domestic septage?

Section 503.25 sets no pathogen class for domestic septage placed on an active unit; paragraph (a) is written for
"sewage sludge (other than domestic septage)". Paragraph (c) sets the vector attraction rule: "One of the vector
attraction reduction requirement in § 503.33 (b)(9) through (b)(12) shall be met when domestic septage is placed on an
active sewage sludge unit." That is injection, incorporation, daily cover, or (b)(12): "The pH of domestic septage shall
be raised to 12 or higher by alkali addition and, without the addition of more alkali, shall remain at 12 or higher for
30 minutes."
