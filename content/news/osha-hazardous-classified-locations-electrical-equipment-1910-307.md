Paint spray booths, gasoline dispensing stations and grain handling plants all have one thing in common under OSHA's general industry
electrical rules: they can be "hazardous (classified) locations," where electrical equipment and wiring must meet the special requirements of 29 CFR 1910.307.
For anyone working toward electrician licensing, this explainer sets out what 29 CFR 1910.307 says may be installed
there. It covers only the federal general industry rule (not construction) and no state's licensing rules. Nothing here
describes a rule change.

## What makes a location "hazardous (classified)"?

Section 1910.307(a)(1) states that it "covers the requirements for electric equipment and wiring in locations that are
classified depending on the properties of the flammable vapors, liquids or gases, or combustible dusts or fibers that
may be present therein and the likelihood that a flammable or combustible concentration or quantity is present." It
lists occupancies where such locations "may be found," including "aircraft hangars, gasoline dispensing and service
stations" and "paint-finishing process plants." It adds: "Each room, section or area shall be considered individually in determining its classification."

The definitions are in 29 CFR 1910.399:

| Class | What makes it hazardous (1910.399)                                                                                                                    |
| ----- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| I     | "flammable gases or vapors are or may be present in the air in quantities sufficient to produce explosive or ignitable mixtures"                      |
| II    | "hazardous because of the presence of combustible dust"                                                                                               |
| III   | "hazardous because of the presence of easily ignitable fibers or flyings," which are "not likely to be in suspension in the air in quantities sufficient to produce ignitable mixtures" |

Each class has a Division 1 and Division 2. In Class I, for example, Division 1 includes a location "In which ignitable
concentrations of flammable gases or vapors may exist under normal operating conditions." Division 2 includes a
location where the hazardous liquids, vapors or gases "will normally be confined within closed containers or closed
systems from which they can escape only in the event of accidental rupture or breakdown of such containers or systems,
or as a result of abnormal operation of equipment." Each definition has further conditions in 1910.399.

Class I may instead use zones. Section 1910.307(a)(4) states that in Class I locations, "an installation must be
classified as using the division classification system" or "using the zone classification system," while "In Class II
and Class III locations, an installation must be classified using the division classification system."

## The three ways equipment can qualify

Section 1910.307(c) is the core rule: "Equipment, wiring methods, and installations of equipment in hazardous
(classified) locations shall be intrinsically safe, approved for the hazardous (classified) location, or safe for the
hazardous (classified) location."

- **Intrinsically safe.** "Equipment and associated wiring approved as intrinsically safe is permitted in any hazardous
  (classified) location for which it is approved."
- **Approved for the location.** "Equipment shall be approved not only for the class of location, but also for the
  ignitable or combustible properties of the specific gas, vapor, dust, or fiber that will be present." It "shall be
  marked to show the class, group, and operating temperature or temperature range, based on operation in a 40-degree C
  ambient, for which it is approved," and "The temperature marking may not exceed the ignition temperature of the
  specific gas or vapor to be encountered." Paragraph (c)(2)(ii) lists provisions that modify this marking rule for specific equipment.
- **Safe for the location.** Such equipment "shall be of a type and design that the employer demonstrates will provide
  protection from the hazards arising from the combustibility and flammability of vapors, liquids, gases, dusts, or
  fibers involved."

"Approved" has a specific meaning in 1910.399: "Acceptable to the authority enforcing this subpart," which it names as
"the Assistant Secretary of Labor for Occupational Safety and Health."

## Can Division 1 equipment go in a Division 2 area?

Yes, within the same class and group. Section 1910.307(e) states: "Equipment that has been approved for a Division 1
location may be installed in a Division 2 location of the same class and group." General-purpose equipment or equipment in general-purpose enclosures "may be installed in Division 2 locations if the employer can demonstrate
that the equipment does not constitute a source of ignition under normal operating conditions."

## What about conduit and documentation?

On conduit, 1910.307(d) states: "All conduits shall be threaded and shall be made wrench-tight. Where it is impractical
to make a threaded joint tight, a bonding jumper shall be utilized."

On records, 1910.307(b) requires that areas classified under the zone system, and areas under the division system
"established after August 13, 2007," "shall be properly documented." The documentation "shall be available to those
authorized to design, install, inspect, maintain, or operate electric equipment at the location."

## Who decides under the zone system?

For Class I zones, 1910.307(g)(4)(i) states: "Classification of areas and selection of equipment and wiring methods
shall be under the supervision of a qualified registered professional engineer."
