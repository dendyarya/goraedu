Total trihalomethanes (TTHM) and haloacetic acids (five) (HAA5) are disinfection byproducts with maximum contaminant
levels in 40 CFR 141.64. Under the Stage 2 rule in 40 CFR 141 Subpart V, compliance is judged location by location, and
the rule also sets an **operational evaluation level** (141.626). This explainer answers one practical
question from the eCFR text: when is that level exceeded, and what must the water system do next? It is an explainer, not
a rule change.

## Which systems does Subpart V cover?

Section 141.620(b) states: "You are subject to these requirements if your system is a community water system or a
nontransient noncommunity water system that uses a primary or residual disinfectant other than ultraviolet light or
delivers water that has been treated with a primary or residual disinfectant other than ultraviolet light."

The MCLs that apply under Subpart V are in 141.64(b)(2)(i): 0.080 mg/L for TTHM and 0.060 mg/L for HAA5, which "must be
complied with as a locational running annual average at each monitoring location" beginning on the Subpart V compliance
date in 141.620(c).

## When is the operational evaluation level exceeded?

Section 141.626(a) gives the calculation. A system has exceeded the operational evaluation level "at any monitoring
location where the sum of the two previous quarters' TTHM results plus twice the current quarter's TTHM result, divided
by 4 to determine an average, exceeds 0.080 mg/L, or where the sum of the two previous quarters' HAA5 results plus twice
the current quarter's HAA5 result, divided by 4 to determine an average, exceeds 0.060 mg/L."

In other words, it uses three quarters of results at one location, with the current quarter counted twice:

- **TTHM:** (previous quarter 2 + previous quarter 1 + 2 × current quarter) ÷ 4, compared with 0.080 mg/L.
- **HAA5:** the same calculation, compared with 0.060 mg/L.

This is a different number from the locational running annual average (LRAA) used for MCL compliance. Under 141.625(b),
a system is in violation of the MCL "when the LRAA exceeds the subpart V MCLs in § 141.64(b)(2), calculated based on four
consecutive quarters of monitoring" (or fewer quarters if the MCL would be exceeded regardless of later results).

## What must the system do after an exceedance?

Section 141.626(b)(1) states: "If you exceed the operational evaluation level, you must conduct an operational
evaluation and submit a written report of the evaluation to the State no later than 90 days after being notified of the
analytical result that causes you to exceed the operational evaluation level." It adds: "The written report must be made
available to the public upon request."

The 90 days run from when the system is notified of the analytical result (141.626(b)(1)).

## What does the operational evaluation look at?

Under 141.626(b)(2), the evaluation "must include an examination of system treatment and distribution operational
practices, including storage tank operations, excess storage capacity, distribution system flushing, changes in sources or
source water quality, and treatment changes or problems that may contribute to TTHM and HAA5 formation and what steps
could be considered to minimize future exceedances."

## Can the evaluation be narrower?

Yes, with State approval. Section 141.626(b)(2)(i) states: "You may request and the State may allow you to limit the
scope of your evaluation if you are able to identify the cause of the operational evaluation level exceedance."

Two conditions follow in (b)(2)(ii): the request "does not extend the schedule in paragraph (b)(1) of this section for
submitting the written report," and "The State must approve this limited scope of evaluation in writing and you must keep
that approval with the completed report."

## How is the exceedance reported?

Separately from the evaluation report, the quarterly report under 141.629(a)(1) must be sent to the State "within 10
days of the end of any quarter in which monitoring is required." For each monitoring location it includes, under
141.629(a)(1)(v): "Any operational evaluation levels that were exceeded during the quarter and, if so, the location and
date, and the calculated TTHM and HAA5 levels." Under 141.629(b), Subpart V monitoring plans and results are kept "as
required by § 141.33."

## Does a single high sample change the monitoring schedule?

It can. Section 141.625(a) says a system that monitors a location "annually or less frequently than annually under §
141.621 or § 141.623" must "increase monitoring to dual sample sets once per quarter (taken every 90 days) at all
locations if a TTHM sample is >0.080 mg/L or a HAA5 sample is >0.060 mg/L at any location." Under 141.625(c), it may
return to routine monitoring after at least four consecutive quarters of increased monitoring when "the LRAA for every
monitoring location is ≤0.060 mg/L for TTHM and ≤0.045 mg/L for HAA5."
