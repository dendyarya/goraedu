When a laboratory reports a drug test as confirmed positive, the report goes to a Medical Review Officer (MRO),
who must complete verification of the result. This explainer asks: can an employer take a CDL driver off safety-sensitive
duty in the meantime? The answer is in FMCSA's testing rule, 49 CFR 382.119, "Stand-down waiver provision", which points
to the Department of Transportation's testing procedures in 49 CFR 40.21. It describes the regulations as shown in the
eCFR, which is current to November 19, 2026; nothing here describes a rule change.

## What is a "stand-down"?

Section 382.107 defines "Stand-down" as "the practice of temporarily removing an employee from the performance of
safety-sensitive functions based only on a report from a laboratory to the MRO of a confirmed positive test for a drug
or drug metabolite, an adulterated test, or a substituted test, before the MRO has completed verification of the test
results."

It covers the time after the laboratory reports but before the MRO finishes verification. The same section defines "Safety-sensitive function" as "all time from the time a driver begins to work or is required to
be in readiness to work until the time he/she is relieved from work and all responsibility for performing work."

## Is a stand-down allowed?

Not by default. Section 382.119(a) states: "Employers are prohibited from standing employees down, except consistent
with a waiver from the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration as required under this section."

The Part 40 rule says the same thing in its own words. Section 40.21(a) states: "As an employer, you are prohibited from
standing employees down, except consistent with a waiver a DOT agency grants under this section." Section 40.21(e) adds:
"You must not stand employees down in the absence of a waiver, or inconsistent with the terms of your waiver."

## How does an employer ask for a waiver?

Under 382.119(b), an employer subject to Part 382 that seeks a waiver "shall follow the procedures in 49 CFR 40.21" and
"must send a written request, which includes all of the information required by that section to the Administrator,
Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration". Under 382.119(c), "The final decision whether to grant or deny the
application for a waiver will be made by the Administrator or the Administrator's designee." Under 382.119(d), the
employer is sent a copy of the decision, "which will include the terms and conditions for the waiver or the reason for
denying the application for a waiver."

Section 40.21(b)(1) says which agency handles a request when an employer has employees under more than one DOT agency:
"the concerned DOT agency is the one whose drug and alcohol testing rules apply to the majority of the covered employees
in your organization."

## What does a waiver allow?

Section 40.21(b) states that a waiver, "if granted, permits you to stand an employee down following the MRO's receipt
of a laboratory report of a confirmed positive test for a drug or drug metabolite, an adulterated test, or a substituted
test pertaining to the employee."

Under 40.21(d), a waiver may be granted only if the Administrator or designee finds that, in the context of the employer's organization, there is "a high probability that the
procedures you propose will effectively enhance safety and protect the interests of employees in fairness and
confidentiality." Under 40.21(d)(1), the Administrator or designee "may impose any conditions he or she deems
appropriate on the grant of a waiver." Under 40.21(d)(2), the waiver may be immediately suspended or revoked if the
employer fails to protect effectively employees' interests in fairness and confidentiality, fails to comply with 40.21, or fails to
comply with the waiver's conditions.

## What must the employer's stand-down policy promise?

Section 40.21(c)(2) lists what the proposed written policy must include. Among them:

- Stand-down applies "only with respect to the actual performance of safety-sensitive duties" (c)(2)(iv).
- No action "adversely affecting the employee's pay and benefits pending the completion of the MRO's verification
  process", including "continuing to pay the employee during the period of the stand-down" (c)(2)(v).
- The stand-down period "will not exceed five days, unless you are informed in writing by the MRO that a longer period
  is needed to complete the verification process" (c)(2)(vi).
- If the MRO verifies the test negative or cancels it, the employer must "return the employee immediately to the
  performance of safety-sensitive duties" (c)(2)(vii)(A), and the employee "suffers no adverse personnel or financial
  consequences as a result" (c)(2)(vii)(B).

## What this means for CDL study

For Part 382 review, the key points from the text are: a stand-down means removal based only on the laboratory's report,
before MRO verification; it is prohibited unless FMCSA has granted a waiver; and a waiver is requested in writing under
the 40.21 procedure. These sections do not mention the CDL knowledge test. To review the rest of Part 382, see our
[CDL drug and alcohol testing study guide](/cdl/drug-alcohol-testing/study-guide/) and
[practice test](/cdl/drug-alcohol-testing/practice-test/), the [CDL hub](/cdl/), or our explainer on
[who can see a CDL driver's drug and alcohol test records](/news/cdl-who-can-see-drug-alcohol-test-records-382-405/).
