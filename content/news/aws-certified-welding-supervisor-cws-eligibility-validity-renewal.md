The Certified Welding Supervisor (CWS) credential is run by the American Welding Society (AWS). AWS is a private
certifying body, not a government agency, and this explainer uses only AWS's own CWS program page for facts about its
own program. It answers one practical question for welding certification: who qualifies for the CWS, and how long does
it stay valid? Nothing here describes a rule change. Whether an employer, code or contract requires a CWS is outside
this post.

## What is the CWS for?

AWS's page states: "The CWS program is designed to recognize individuals who possess the knowledge and skills to
effectively manage welding activities." It is a separate credential from the Certified Welding Inspector (CWI).

## What education and experience does AWS ask for?

AWS lists these requirements:

- Education: "High school diploma or equivalent (GED)", with "Welding-related training or coursework preferred but not
  required".
- Experience: "A minimum of 3 years of welding experience with a high school diploma", with "A minimum of 2 years of
  welding supervision experience preferred".

The page's FAQ states: "Candidates must have a minimum of three (3) years of practical welding experience in a
fabrication, construction, or other welding-related industry." It also says that "three years of relevant teaching
experience in welding or a closely related field may be substituted" for one year of practical experience, provided
proper documentation is submitted.

Supervision experience is listed as preferred, not required.

## What are the exams?

AWS states: "AWS conducts the CWS Part A (Fundamentals of Supervision) and Part B (Welding Practices & Economics)
exams". The page says they are computer-based exams given at Prometric test centers. It does not state the number of
questions, the time allowed or the passing score; it links AWS's CWS Examination User Guide and its Exam References and
Editions Guide, which hold those details.

## How long is the CWS valid?

AWS states: "The CWS credential is valid for three (3) years." It then explains: "A CWS can renew their CWS credential
twice: once at year three (3) and once at year six (6)."

For those renewals, AWS states: "Each of the two renewals require written documentation to support evidence of
continued activity in the occupational position of welding supervisor." In other words, the renewals at years three and
six rest on documented work as a welding supervisor.

## What happens at nine years?

After two consecutive renewals, "a total of nine (9) years of continuous certification", AWS lists recertification
requirements. One of them is: "The certificate holder shall successfully pass the CWS Part A and Part B exams or be
exempt from the exams by demonstrating continued education equivalent to 80 professional development hours (PDH) over
the nine (9) year period of certification with 20 PDH being completed in the immediate three (3) year period preceding
the nine-year (9) recertification."

Another listed requirement applies either way: "Completed and submitted documentation to support evidence of continued
activity in the occupational position of welding supervisor." So at nine years the holder documents continued work as a
welding supervisor and then either retakes both exams or shows 80 PDH over the nine years, with 20 of them in the last
three years.
