This explainer covers **Colorado only**, where electrician
licenses are issued by the State Electrical Board through the Division of Professions and Occupations (DPO). It answers
one practical question from the board's own pages: how much continuing education (CE) does a licensed Colorado
electrician need to renew, and what counts? It explains the requirements as published on the date checked; it does not
describe a rule change, and nothing here applies to any other state.

## Who has to complete continuing education?

Licensed electricians at three levels. The board's Continuing Education page says that "All Colorado-licensed
Residential Wireman, Journeyman, and Master Electricians" "must complete 24 hours of Continuing Education (CE) during
each three-year license period" "before they may renew their license."

There is one exception: the first renewal of a license for which passing a licensing examination was a condition of issuance. The page states:
"This requirement does not apply for the first renewal of the license for which, as a condition of issuance," "the
applicant successfully completed a licensing examination."

## How often do Colorado electrician licenses renew?

Every three years. The board's Applications and Forms page says electrician and electrical contractor licenses "renew
every three years," and tells licensees: "View your expiration date on your online account." It also notes that
"renewals open approximately six weeks prior to the expiration date."

The same page lists online options to reinstate an expired license, separate reinstatement checklists for journeyman
electricians and for master and residential wireman electricians, and forms to change a license to inactive status or
reactivate it. It does not state their requirements or fees, so check those forms directly.

## How many hours, and in which topics?

The 24 hours come from a fixed list. The CE page states: "CE hours must be selected from among the Core Competencies
listed below:" and lists five:

- Grounding and Bonding
- National Electrical Code Changes
- Wiring Methods
- Theory and Calculations
- Safety Related to the Electrical Industry

Two limits apply within that list:

- **Code changes are required.** "A minimum of four (4) hours is required in National Electrical Code Changes."
- **Safety is capped, not required.** "No more than four (4) hours will be credited in safety courses related to the
  electrical industry;" "however, there is not a requirement to take courses in safety training."

The page sets no minimum for the other three topics.

## Where can you take CE?

Only from providers that have attested to the board. The CE page states: "CE courses will only be accepted by the Board
if they are taken with a CE provider/vendor" "that has attested to the Board that their courses and instructors meet the
requirements of Board Rule." In its provider section, the page adds that "Only courses taken with attested providers
will qualify for a Colorado electrician’s license renewal requirements."

The choice of course is the licensee's responsibility: "It is the responsibility of all licensees to ensure CE courses
and providers meet the requirements in Board Rule." The page also gives a way to report a provider whose courses do not
meet the rule, by e-mail to the board.

## What records should you keep?

Your own certificates. The board does not get completion records from providers. The CE page states: "Licensees are
responsible for maintaining their own CE documentation;" "CE providers/vendors do not supply the Board with CE
completion records."

It asks licensees to keep them for seven years: "Please retain all copies of your completed course certificates for
seven (7) years," "in the event you are selected for an audit." It also states: "Audits of compliance will be conducted
after each license period."

## What this means for license holders and exam takers

For someone studying for a first Colorado license, the exception above means the CE requirement does not apply to the first renewal of a license for which passing the licensing exam was a condition of issuance. After that, plan for 24 hours per three-year period,
with at least four on National Electrical Code changes, and keep every certificate.

## Where to check before you act

These are the State Electrical Board's requirements as shown on its pages on the date above. The board's pages and its
Board Rules are the authority, and requirements can change, so confirm the current rules with the board before you renew
or sign up for a course. For federal OSHA electrical safety rules, see the
