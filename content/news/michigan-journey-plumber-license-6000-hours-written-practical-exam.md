This explainer covers **Michigan only**, where plumbers are licensed through the Bureau of Construction Codes in the
Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs (LARA). It answers one practical question from two LARA documents, its
"Obtaining a Plumbing Journeyperson License in the State of Michigan" flowchart and its journey plumber application form:
how do you qualify for the Michigan journey plumber license, and what exams do you take? Nothing here describes a rule
change, and nothing here applies to any other state.

## Who can apply?

The application form states: "All applicants must not be less than 18 years of age". The experience requirement starts
with apprentice registration. The flowchart says an apprentice "Must register as an apprentice plumber 30 days after
employment." and must "Work at least 6000 hours over a period of 3 years as a registered apprentice plumber". It also
warns: "Apprentice plumbers MUST maintain and renew their registrations in order to qualify to apply to become a plumbing
journeyperson."

The form says journey applicants "must provide, with this application, documentation verifying the applicant has not
less than 6,000 hours" of experience "gained over a period of at least 3 years as an apprentice plumber in the practical
installation of plumbing" "under the supervision of a master plumber." That documentation is a notarized letter, or
letters, from current and former employers.

## Is there a military route?

Yes. The flowchart sums up the requirement as: "Must have 6000 hours over 3 years as a registered plumber apprentice. OR
equivalent military experience." Its military path says to "Serve as a plumber or equivalent for at least 6,000 hours",
to "Be honorably discharged 1 year prior to application" and to "have a signed affidavit from commanding officer
attesting to plumbing experience."

The application form also has a military examination waiver, under its military and uniformed service waivers heading.
It says "Licensure without examination is possible IF the applicant:" provides proof of being a member of the armed
forces or uniformed services, a veteran, or a dependent of a member or veteran, and also has "an active, valid plumbing
license in at least 1 other state of the United States." Both conditions must be met; the waiver is not open to other
applicants who are licensed in another state.

## What exams do you take?

Two. The form states: "Effective December 15, 2021, application for Journey Plumber will be for BOTH a written
examination and practical skills examination." It adds: "You must pass both parts to achieve licensure." For the written
part, the form says the applicant "will be notified by the third-party testing agency (PSI)" "with instructions on how
to schedule your written examination." On the form, the applicant signs a statement that begins "I certify that I
understand I must pass both the written and practical skills examinations". Neither document states a passing score.

## What happens after you are licensed?

The flowchart's last steps are "Submit completed application to state of Michigan.", "Take and pass appropriate test."
and "Complete required continuing education annually." It states: "Completion of continuing education is a condition of
renewal of a plumbing journeyperson license." Neither document gives the renewal date, the number of continuing education
hours, or the courses that count; LARA's license renewal pages are the place to confirm those.

## Where to check before you act

These are the requirements as shown in LARA's flowchart and application form on the date above. LARA's Bureau of
Construction Codes is the authority, so confirm the current form, exam scheduling, fees and renewal steps with LARA before
you apply. These rules describe Michigan only; other states set their own. For other plumbing topics on goraedu, see our
[plumbing page](/plumbing/).
