Respirable crystalline silica is covered in general industry by 29 CFR 1910.1053. This explainer answers one practical
question: which workers must be offered medical exams under that standard, and what do the exams and reports involve? The
answer is in paragraph (i), "Medical surveillance," read in the eCFR (text current to November 24, 2026). It is an
explainer, not a rule change.

## Does 1910.1053 apply to the workplace?

Under 1910.1053(a)(1), the section "applies to all occupational exposures to respirable crystalline silica," except construction work as defined in 29 CFR 1910.12(b) (covered by 29 CFR 1926.1153), agricultural operations covered under 29 CFR part 1928, and "Exposures
that result from the processing of sorptive clays." Under (a)(2), it "does not apply where the employer has objective data
demonstrating that employee exposure to respirable crystalline silica will remain below 25 micrograms per cubic meter of
air" as an 8-hour time-weighted average "under any foreseeable conditions." Paragraph (a)(3) adds a further exception where the employer complies with 29 CFR 1926.1153, the task is indistinguishable from a construction task listed on Table 1 in 1926.1153(c), and the task will not be performed regularly in the same environment and conditions.

The standard's action level is 25 µg/m³ calculated as an 8-hour TWA (1910.1053(b)). "Employee exposure" is defined as the
exposure "that would occur if the employee were not using a respirator."

## Who must be offered medical surveillance?

Section 1910.1053(i)(1)(i) states: "The employer shall make medical surveillance available at no cost to the employee, and
at a reasonable time and place, for each employee who will be occupationally exposed to respirable crystalline silica at
or above the action level for 30 or more days per year."

The exams must be performed by a PLHCP ("Physician or other licensed health care professional," as defined in paragraph (b)) (1910.1053(i)(1)(ii)).

## When is the first exam, and what does it include?

Under 1910.1053(i)(2), the employer "shall make available an initial (baseline) medical examination within 30 days after
initial assignment, unless the employee has received a medical examination that meets the requirements of this section
within the last three years." The initial exam consists of:

- a medical and work history, with emphasis on silica, dust and other exposures affecting the respiratory system,
  respiratory symptoms, "history of tuberculosis; and smoking status and history";
- "A physical examination with special emphasis on the respiratory system";
- a chest X-ray "interpreted and classified according to the International Labour Office (ILO) International
  Classification of Radiographs of Pneumoconioses by a NIOSH-certified B Reader";
- a pulmonary function test including FVC, FEV1 and the FEV1/FVC ratio, "administered by a spirometry technician with a
  current certificate from a NIOSH-approved spirometry course";
- "Testing for latent tuberculosis infection"; and
- "Any other tests deemed appropriate by the PLHCP."

## How often are follow-up exams?

Section 1910.1053(i)(3) requires periodic exams with the same procedures, except the latent tuberculosis test, "at least
every three years, or more frequently if recommended by the PLHCP."

## What does the doctor get from the employer?

Under 1910.1053(i)(4), the employer must ensure the PLHCP "has a copy of this standard" and must provide a description of
the employee's former, current and anticipated duties and exposure levels, a description of personal protective
equipment used or to be used, and information from earlier employment-related medical exam records within the employer's
control.

## Who gets the results, and when?

- **The employee:** the PLHCP explains the results and gives the employee "a written medical report within 30 days of
  each medical examination performed" (1910.1053(i)(5)). The report covers results, any recommended limits on respirator
  use or silica exposure, and a statement about a specialist referral where the chest X-ray "is classified as 1/0 or
  higher by the B Reader" or where the PLHCP otherwise thinks referral appropriate.
- **The employer:** the employer obtains a written medical opinion within 30 days that "shall contain only" the exam
  date, a statement that the exam met the section's requirements, and any recommended limits on respirator use
  (1910.1053(i)(6)(i)). Limits on silica exposure and the specialist statement are added only "If the employee provides
  written authorization" (1910.1053(i)(6)(ii)). The employee must receive a copy of the opinion within 30 days of each
  exam (1910.1053(i)(6)(iii)).

## When is a specialist exam required?

Under 1910.1053(i)(7)(i), if the PLHCP's written opinion indicates the employee should see a specialist, "the employer
shall make available a medical examination by a specialist within 30 days after receiving the PLHCP's written opinion."
The specialist receives the same information as the PLHCP, gives the employee a written report within 30 days, and the
employer obtains the specialist's written opinion within 30 days of the exam.
