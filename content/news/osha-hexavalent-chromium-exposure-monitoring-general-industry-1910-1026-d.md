Hexavalent chromium, written chromium (VI) or Cr(VI), is covered in general industry by 29 CFR 1910.1026. This
explainer answers one practical question from the eCFR text: when does an employer have to measure or otherwise
determine workers' chromium (VI) exposure, and how often? The answer is in paragraph (d), "Exposure determination." It
is an explainer, not a rule change.

## Does 1910.1026 apply?

Section 1910.1026(a)(1) states that the standard "applies to occupational exposures to chromium (VI) in all forms and
compounds in general industry," except:

- "Exposures that occur in the application of pesticides regulated by the Environmental Protection Agency or another
  Federal government agency (e.g., the treatment of wood with preservatives)" ((a)(2));
- "Exposures to portland cement" ((a)(3)); or
- where the employer has objective data showing that a chromium-containing material or a specific process, operation or
  activity "cannot release dusts, fumes, or mists of chromium (VI) in concentrations at or above 0.5 µgm/m 3 as an 8-hour
  time-weighted average (TWA) under any expected conditions of use" ((a)(4)).

## What are the two exposure numbers?

- **Permissible exposure limit (PEL):** under 1910.1026(c), "The employer shall ensure that no employee is exposed to an
  airborne concentration of chromium (VI) in excess of 5 micrograms per cubic meter of air (5 µgm/m 3 ), calculated as an
  8-hour time-weighted average (TWA)."
- **Action level:** paragraph (b) defines it as "a concentration of airborne chromium (VI) of 2.5 micrograms per cubic
  meter of air (2.5 µgm/m 3 ) calculated as an 8-hour time-weighted average (TWA)."

## Who must have an exposure determination?

Section 1910.1026(d)(1) states: "Each employer who has a workplace or work operation covered by this section shall
determine the 8-hour TWA exposure for each employee exposed to chromium (VI)." The employer uses one of two options:
scheduled monitoring under (d)(2) or the performance-oriented option under (d)(3).

## How does the scheduled monitoring option work?

- **Initial monitoring** ((d)(2)(i)): based on "a sufficient number of personal breathing zone air samples to accurately
  characterize full shift exposure on each shift, for each job classification, in each work area." If the employer
  samples only some employees, it "shall sample the employee(s) expected to have the highest chromium (VI) exposures."
- **Initial monitoring below the action level** ((d)(2)(ii)): the employer "may discontinue monitoring for those employees whose exposures
  are represented by such monitoring."
- **At or above the action level** ((d)(2)(iii)): periodic monitoring "at least every six months."
- **Above the PEL** ((d)(2)(iv)): periodic monitoring "at least every three months."
- **Stopping periodic monitoring** ((d)(2)(v)): allowed when periodic monitoring shows exposures below the action level
  "and the result is confirmed by the result of another monitoring taken at least seven days later."
- **Additional monitoring** ((d)(2)(vi)): required "when there has been any change in the production process, raw
  materials, equipment, personnel, work practices, or control methods that may result in new or additional exposures to
  chromium (VI), or when the employer has any reason to believe that new or additional exposures have occurred."

## What is the performance-oriented option?

Under 1910.1026(d)(3), the employer determines each employee's 8-hour TWA exposure "on the basis of any combination of
air monitoring data, historical monitoring data, or objective data sufficient to accurately characterize employee
exposure to chromium (VI)."

## When do employees learn the results?

Section 1910.1026(d)(4)(i) requires that, "Within 15 work days after making an exposure determination," the employer
"shall individually notify each affected employee in writing of the results of that determination or post the results in
an appropriate location accessible to all affected employees." When exposure is above the PEL, (d)(4)(ii) requires the
written notification to describe "the corrective action being taken to reduce employee exposure to or below the PEL."

## What about the sampling method and observers?

Where air monitoring is done to comply with the section, the employer must use a method that "can measure chromium (VI) to within an accuracy of plus or minus 25 percent
(±25%)" and produce accurate measurements "within a statistical confidence level of 95 percent for airborne
concentrations at or above the action level" ((d)(5)). Affected employees or their designated representatives must be given "an
opportunity to observe any monitoring of employee exposure to chromium (VI)" ((d)(6)(i)).

Where exposure is, "or can reasonably be expected to be, in excess of the PEL," paragraph (e)(1) also requires a
regulated area.
