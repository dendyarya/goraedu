Apart from any welder certification, OSHA's general industry rules set workplace duties for setting up and using arc welding equipment. They are in 29 CFR 1910.254, "Arc welding and
cutting," in Subpart Q (Welding, Cutting and Brazing). This explainer reads paragraphs (a), (c) and (d) of that section
as they stand in the eCFR (text current to November 20, 2026). It is an explainer, not a rule change: the eCFR lists the
section's most recent amendment at 70 FR 53929 (Sept. 13, 2005). It covers general industry workplaces under Part 1910,
not construction work under Part 1926. OSHA rules set duties at the workplace; they are not a welder certification
test.

## Does OSHA require arc welders to be instructed?

Yes. 1910.254(a)(3) states: "Workmen designated to operate arc welding equipment shall have been properly instructed and
qualified to operate such equipment as specified in paragraph (d) of this section."

Paragraph (d)(1) sets what they must know: "Workers assigned to operate or maintain arc welding equipment shall be
acquainted with the requirements of this section and with 1910.252 (a), (b), and (c) of this part." Those three
paragraphs of 1910.252 cover fire prevention, protection of personnel, and health protection and ventilation.

The section does not name a specific welder certification or test.

## What must be checked when setting up?

1910.254(d)(2) to (d)(5) list the hook-up checks; only (d)(2) says "Before starting operations":

- **Connections:** "Before starting operations all connections to the machine shall be checked to make certain they are
  properly made."
- **Work lead:** "The work lead shall be firmly attached to the work; magnetic work clamps shall be freed from adherent
  metal particles of spatter on contact surfaces."
- **Coiled cable:** "Coiled welding cable shall be spread out before use to avoid serious overheating and damage to
  insulation."
- **Grounding:** "Grounding of the welding machine frame shall be checked. Special attention shall be given to safety
  ground connections of portable machines."
- **Leaks:** "There shall be no leaks of cooling water, shielding gas or engine fuel."
- **Switches:** "It shall be determined that proper switching equipment for shutting down the machine is provided."

Under (d)(6), "Printed rules and instructions covering operation of equipment supplied by the manufacturers shall be
strictly followed."

## What may not carry welding current?

1910.254(c)(2) sets limits on the work-lead (return) circuit:

- "Conduits containing electrical conductors shall not be used for completing a work-lead circuit."
- "Pipelines shall not be used as a permanent part of a work-lead circuit," but they may be used during construction,
  extension or repair if current is not carried through threaded joints, flanged bolted joints or caulked joints and
  special precautions are used to avoid sparking at connection of the work-lead cable.
- "Chains, wire ropes, cranes, hoists, and elevators shall not be used to carry welding current."
- Where a structure, conveyor or fixture is regularly used as a welding current return circuit, "joints shall be bonded
  or provided with adequate current collecting devices."
- "All ground connections shall be checked to determine that they are mechanically strong and electrically adequate for
  the required current."

## What about several welders on one structure?

1910.254(c)(3)(iv) explains that when several welders work on one structure, no-load voltages between electrode holders can reach up to 2 times normal. It then requires: "All d.c. machines shall be connected with the same polarity" and "All a.c.
machines shall be connected to the same phase of the supply circuit and with the same instantaneous polarity."

## Where can an electrode holder be left?

1910.254(d)(7) states: "Electrode holders when not in use shall be so placed that they cannot make electrical contact
with persons, conducting objects, fuel or compressed gas tanks."

Paragraph (d)(8) adds that "The welder should not coil or loop welding electrode cable around parts of his body." Note
the word "should" in that sentence; the cable splice rule in the same paragraph uses "shall."

## What happens when equipment is damaged or wet?

1910.254(d)(9) covers maintenance:

- "The operator should report any equipment defect or safety hazard to his supervisor and the use of the equipment shall
  be discontinued until its safety has been assured. Repairs shall be made only by qualified personnel."
- "Machines which have become wet shall be thoroughly dried and tested before being used."
- "Cables with damaged insulation or exposed bare conductors shall be replaced." Joining lengths of work and electrode
  cables must be done with connecting means "specifically intended for the purpose," with insulation adequate for the
  service conditions.

## What this means for study

From 1910.254(c) and (d), the points to know are: operators and maintainers must know the section and 1910.252(a) to
(c); connections are checked before starting, and the work lead, frame grounding, leaks and switching are checked as part of set-up; coiled cable is
spread out; chains, wire ropes, cranes, hoists and elevators never carry welding current, and conduits containing electrical conductors are never used to complete a work-lead circuit; electrode
holders not in use are placed so they cannot touch people, conducting objects or gas tanks; and damaged cables are
replaced and wet machines dried and tested.
