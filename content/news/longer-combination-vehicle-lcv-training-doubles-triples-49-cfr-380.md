A Class A commercial driver's license (CDL) with a double/triple trailer endorsement is not, on its own, enough to
drive, in interstate commerce, a truck-tractor with two or more trailers weighing more than 80,000 pounds on the
National System of Interstate and Defense Highways. Federal rules in 49 CFR Part 380 add a separate
training requirement for longer combination vehicles (LCVs). This explainer reads Subparts A, B and D of Part 380 as
they stand in the eCFR (current to November 12, 2026). Nothing here describes a rule change.

## What counts as a longer combination vehicle?

380.105(b) defines it: "Longer combination vehicle (LCV) means any combination of a truck-tractor and two or more
trailers or semi-trailers, which operate on the National System of Interstate and Defense Highways with a gross
vehicle weight (GVW) greater than 36,288 kilograms (80,000 pounds)."

The same section names two types. An "LCV Double" is "a truck-tractor in combination with two trailers and/or
semi-trailers," and an "LCV Triple" is "a truck-tractor in combination with three trailers and/or semi-trailers."

Who the rules cover is set in 380.103: "The rules in this part apply to all operators of LCVs in interstate commerce,
employers of such persons, and LCV driver-instructors."

## Is training required before driving one?

Yes, apart from the closed grandfather option described below. 380.107(a) states that "a driver who wishes to operate
an LCV shall first take and successfully complete an LCV driver-training program that provides the knowledge and
skills necessary to operate an LCV." The topics the program must teach are listed in Appendix F to Part 380.

The employer side is in 380.113(a)(1). No motor carrier shall "Allow, require, permit or authorize an individual to
operate an LCV unless he/she meets the requirements in §§ 380.203 or 380.205 and has been issued the LCV
driver-training certificate described in § 380.401."

## Who can sign up for LCV training?

The entry conditions look back over "the 6 months immediately preceding application for training." For LCV Doubles,
380.203(a) requires, during that period:

- "A valid Class A CDL with a double/triple trailer endorsement";
- driving experience in a Group A vehicle, shown by "an employer's written statement that the driver has, for at
  least 6 months immediately preceding application, operated a Group A vehicle while under his/her employ";
- "No more than one driver's license";
- "No suspension, revocation, or cancellation of his/her CDL";
- no convictions for a major offense or a railroad-highway grade crossing offense while operating a CMV, and no
  convictions for violating an out-of-service order (as defined in 383.51(b), (d) and (e));
- "No more than one conviction for a serious traffic violation, as defined in § 383.5 of this subchapter, while
  operating a CMV"; and
- no convictions for a violation of State or local traffic control law "arising in connection with any traffic crash
  while operating a CMV."

For LCV Triples, 380.205(a) lists the same license and record conditions. The experience must be in either a "Group A
truck-tractor/semi-trailer combination" or a "Group A truck-tractor/semi-trailer/trailer combination that operates at
a gross vehicle weight of 80,000 pounds or less," again shown by an employer's written statement.

Before training starts, 380.107(b) has the driver "present evidence to the LCV driver-instructor" that these
conditions are met, and the instructor "shall verify" it.

## How is the training tested?

380.201(a) says the program "requires training using an LCV Double or LCV Triple" and covers five categories:
orientation, basic operation, safe operating practices, advanced operations and nondriving activities.

Under 380.109, the driver-student must pass knowledge and skills tests. To be certified, the student must "Answer
correctly at least 80 percent of the questions on each knowledge test" and "Demonstrate that he/she can successfully
perform all of the skills" practiced in the behind-the-wheel unit. The skills tests "must be administered by a
qualified LCV skills instructor." 380.109(c) adds: "Failure to obey traffic laws or involvement in a preventable crash
during the skills portion of the test will result in automatic failure."

## What paperwork follows?

A student who successfully completes the training is issued a Driver-Training Certificate. 380.401(b) states: "An LCV driver must
provide a copy of the Driver-Training Certificate to his/her employer to be filed in the Driver Qualification File."

## Can experienced drivers skip the training?

Not as a new applicant. 380.111 had a grandfather clause for drivers with a qualifying record and experience, but 380.111(e)
states: "An applicant may be grandfathered under this section only during the year following June 1, 2004." Drivers
who qualified then are still exempt from the training under 380.113(a)(1).

## What this means for CDL study

Part 380 builds on Part 383: its definitions apply (380.105(a)), and the double/triple trailer endorsement is a condition for LCV
training, not a substitute for it. Our [CDL standards study guide](/cdl/cdl-standards/study-guide/) covers the
vehicle groups (383.91) and endorsements (383.93) in Part 383, and the
[CDL standards practice test](/cdl/cdl-standards/practice-test/) lets you check them. Part 380 is not one of our
practice-test topics, and the LCV rules above do not say whether a state's CDL knowledge test asks about them. Our
questions are our own, written from the regulations.
