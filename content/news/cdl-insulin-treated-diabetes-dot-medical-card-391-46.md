Drivers working toward a Commercial Driver's License (CDL) often ask whether insulin-treated diabetes rules them out
of the DOT medical certificate. The federal rule that answers this is 49 CFR 391.46. This explainer walks through what
it requires, quoting the eCFR text (up to date as of November 17, 2026). Nothing here describes a rule change, and it
is not medical advice: the decision belongs to the medical examiner.

## Can a driver who uses insulin be physically qualified?

Yes, if the conditions in 391.46 are met. Paragraph (a) states that "An individual with diabetes mellitus treated with
insulin for control is physically qualified to operate a commercial motor vehicle provided" two things are true:

- "The individual otherwise meets the physical qualification standards in § 391.41 or has an exemption or skill
  performance evaluation certificate, if required; and"
- "The individual has the evaluation required by paragraph (b) and the medical examination required by paragraph (c)
  of this section."

## Step 1: the treating clinician and Form MCSA-5870

Before the medical examination, or before the medical examiner's certificate expires, the driver "must be evaluated by
his or her 'treating clinician.'" The rule defines that person as "a healthcare professional who manages, and
prescribes insulin for, the treatment of the individual's diabetes mellitus as authorized by the healthcare
professional's State licensing authority."

During that evaluation, "the treating clinician must complete the Insulin-Treated Diabetes Mellitus Assessment Form,
MCSA-5870," then sign and date it and give "his or her full name, office address, and telephone number on the Form"
(391.46(b)(1) and (2)).

## Step 2: the medical examiner, and the 45-day window

Paragraph (c) sets the timing: "At least annually, but no later than 45 days after the treating clinician signs and
dates the Insulin-Treated Diabetes Mellitus Assessment Form, MCSA-5870", the driver "must be medically examined and
certified by a medical examiner as physically qualified in accordance with § 391.43 and as free of complications from
diabetes mellitus that might impair his or her ability to operate a commercial motor vehicle safely." The examiner
"must receive a completed" MCSA-5870 "for each required examination", and the form is "treated and retained as part of the Medical Examination Report Form, MCSA-5875."

The examiner considers the form and uses "independent medical judgment". The rule then lists when a driver is not
qualified. Under 391.46(c)(2)(i), the individual is not physically qualified "if he or she is not maintaining a stable
insulin regimen and not properly controlling his or her diabetes mellitus." Under (c)(2)(ii), the individual is "not
physically qualified on a permanent basis" with "either severe non-proliferative diabetic retinopathy or proliferative
diabetic retinopathy."

## How long is the certificate valid?

The usual maximum is 12 months. 49 CFR 391.45(e) requires a new examination for "Any driver who has diabetes mellitus
treated with insulin for control and who has obtained a medical examiner's certificate under the standards in §
391.46, if such driver's most recent medical examination and certification as qualified to drive did not occur during
the preceding 12 months".

The glucose records decide whether a driver can get the full period. A driver is not qualified "up to the maximum
12-month period under § 391.45(e) until he or she provides the treating clinician with at least the preceding 3
months of electronic blood glucose self-monitoring records" (391.46(c)(2)(iii)). A driver who does not provide those 3
months of records "is not physically qualified to operate a commercial motor vehicle for more than 3 months"
(391.46(c)(2)(iv)). Once 3 months of compliant records are provided and the clinician completes a new MCSA-5870, the
examiner "may issue a medical examiner's certificate that is valid for up to the maximum 12-month period allowed by §
391.45(e)".

## What records must the driver keep?

Paragraph (d) requires drivers to "self-monitor blood glucose in accordance with the specific treatment plan
prescribed by the treating clinician." The records must be "measured with an electronic glucometer that stores all
readings, that records the date and time of readings, and from which data can be electronically downloaded." "A
printout of the electronic blood glucose records or the glucometer must be provided to the treating clinician at the
time of any of the evaluations required by this section."

## What happens after a severe hypoglycemic episode?

The rule defines one as an episode "that requires the assistance of others, or results in loss of consciousness,
seizure, or coma." A certified driver who has one "is prohibited from operating a commercial motor vehicle, and must
report such occurrence to and be evaluated by a treating clinician as soon as is reasonably practicable"
(391.46(e)(1)).

The prohibition lasts until a treating clinician has determined "that the cause of the severe hypoglycemic episode has
been addressed", has determined that the individual "is maintaining a stable insulin regimen and proper control of
his or her diabetes mellitus", and "Completes a new Insulin-Treated Diabetes Mellitus Assessment Form, MCSA-5870." The
driver "must retain the Form and provide it to the medical examiner at the individual's next medical examination"
(391.46(e)(2)).
