This explainer covers **Connecticut only**, where plumbing licenses are issued by the Department of Consumer Protection
(DCP). It answers one practical question: how do you qualify for Connecticut's P-2 unlimited journeyperson plumber
license, and how is it kept current? It draws on DCP's plumbing licensing and continuing education pages and on
chapter 393 of the Connecticut General Statutes (the 2025 edition and the 2026 supplement, published by the Connecticut
General Assembly), all read on 2026-11-26. This is an explainer; it does not report a new rule change, and nothing here
applies to any other state.

## What does the P-2 license allow?

DCP's "Plumbing Licenses and Scope of Work" page lists the license as "P-2 UNLIMITED JOURNEYPERSON PLUMBER". It says
"The holder of this license may do all plumbing and piping work as so defined" and "only while in the employ of a
contractor licensed for such work." So a P-2 journeyperson may do this work only while employed by a contractor licensed for such work.

## What are the two routes to the exam?

The same page states: "The requirements to qualify for this license examination shall be the completion of a registered
apprenticeship program or equivalent experience and training."

The statute describes the apprenticeship route. Section 20-334a(a)(2)(A) says "An unlimited journeyman's license may be
issued to any person who has completed a bona fide apprenticeship program," "including not less than four years'
experience in the trade for which such person seeks a license," and who shows competency "by successfully completing the
applicable state licensure examination." DCP's "Plumbing and Pipefitting Licensing" page sends this group to the
standard application: "If you completed an apprenticeship with the Connecticut Department of Labor, apply here:".

Section 20-333(b), as amended by Public Act 25-111 effective June 24, 2025, covers the other route. The department "shall allow any
applicant, who has not participated in a registered apprenticeship program," but who either presents a recommendation
for review under section 31-22u or "demonstrates to the department, in consultation with the applicable board,
equivalent experience and training," "to sit for any such examination."

## How many hours does the equivalent experience route need?

DCP's "Plumbing Journeyperson - Equivalent Experience and Training" page says applicants "must be able to prove that you
completed the number of classroom hours AND on-the-job training hours for the license type you are applying for." Its
table lists, for P-2 (Unlimited Plumbing and Piping), "8,000" on-the-job training hours and "576" classroom hours.

On the classroom part, the page states: "Classroom hours are required." and "On the job training and work experience
will not be considered in lieu of the classroom requirement." It adds that "Continuing education certificates and
manufacturer certificates do not meet these criteria and will not be considered." The page asks for transcripts and
notarized employer letters.

## What about the exam itself?

DCP's licensing page states: "Every individual applicant must take an exam(s)". Under section 20-333(b), a person who
completes the required apprentice training program for a journeyman's license under section 20-334a must, "not later than thirty days after completing such program, apply for a licensure
examination". Applicants who fail receive "information on how to retake the examination and a report describing the
applicant's strengths and weaknesses". When applying for the initial license, the passed exam "shall occur not more than
two years prior to the date of the relevant licensure application, unless the appropriate board grants a hardship
extension of such two-year period."

DCP also notes: "The Department does not pre-approve or review applications before submission." On licenses from
elsewhere, the page states: "Connecticut does not have reciprocal agreements with any other jurisdiction." DCP has a
separate application page for applicants with an equivalent out-of-state license; this post does not cover it.

## How often is the license renewed?

Section 20-335 states: "All such licenses shall expire annually." DCP's licensing page gives the date: "All plumbing and
pipefitting licenses expire annually on October 31st."

## What continuing education is required?

Section 20-334d(c)(1) directs the commissioner, with the advice of the Plumbing and Piping Work Board, to adopt
regulations that require for plumbers "not more than a total of seven hours of accredited continuing professional
education every two years," except that after significant plumbing-related building code changes approved by the
International Code Council the commissioner may require more. DCP's "Plumber - Continuing Education" page lists
categories rather than license codes; its unlimited journeyperson line, which corresponds to P-2 (titled "P-2
UNLIMITED JOURNEYPERSON PLUMBER" on the scope page), reads: "Plumbing and Piping unlimited journeyperson - 5 credit hours every two years".

For journeypersons, the page says CE must be completed "between August 1 of an ODD-numbered year and July 31 of the
ODD-numbered year two years later," and adds: "This CE completion deadline is 3 months before the renewal deadline." It
also states: "CE is not required to be completed before the first renewal." Holders "must attest to the completion of
their required continuing education (CE) as part of the renewal process." A hardship exception may be considered "Upon
appropriate showing of a bona fide health or other individual hardship".

## Where to check before you act

These are the requirements as shown on DCP's pages and in the statutes on the date above. DCP and the Connecticut
Plumbing and Piping Work Examining Board are the authority, so confirm current forms, exam scheduling, fees and
continuing education courses with them before you apply. These rules describe Connecticut only; other states set their
own. For other plumbing topics on goraedu, see our [plumbing page](/plumbing/).
