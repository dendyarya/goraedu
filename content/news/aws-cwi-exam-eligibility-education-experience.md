This explainer covers the American Welding Society's own rules for its Certified Welding Inspector (CWI) program, as
AWS published them on the date checked. AWS is a private organization, not a government agency, and the CWI is AWS's
credential, not a government license. goraedu is independent and is not affiliated with or endorsed by AWS. The
question here is a practical one: before you apply, what education, experience and vision checks does AWS ask for?
Nothing here describes a rule change, and renewal after you earn a CWI is covered in our earlier news post on that topic.

## What are the basic conditions to qualify?

There are two. On its CWI page, AWS states that "candidates must pass a vision test and have a combination of
qualifying education and work experience, with supporting documentation."

Experience is not optional. Every education level on the page still calls for some years of "welding based work
experience." More schooling lowers the minimum, but it never removes it.

## How many years of experience do you need?

AWS's page sets a minimum for each education level. In plain terms:

| Your highest education (as AWS describes it)                                                                              | Minimum welding-based work experience |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Bachelor's or higher degree in welding engineering or welding technology                                                   | 1 year                                |
| Associate's or higher degree in welding or non-welding related engineering technology, engineering, or a physical science  | 2 years                               |
| Engineering/technical courses applicable to a Bachelor or higher degree in Welding                                    | 3 years                               |
| Trade or vocational courses                                                                                                | 4 years                               |
| High school diploma or approved high school equivalency diploma                                                            | 5 years                               |
| 8th grade level of schooling                                                                                               | 9 years                               |
| Less than 8th grade                                                                                                        | 12 years                              |

AWS words each row in terms of substitution: education counts in place of some years of work, up to a cap. The
welding degree row reads "four (4) years maximum substitution," the associate's degree row "three (3) years maximum
substitution," the engineering and technical courses row "two (2) years maximum substitution," and the trade courses
row "one (1) year maximum substitution for successfully completed courses."

The trade and vocational row says the substitution is for "successfully completed courses." The page counts only
successfully completed courses toward this substitution.

## What counts as welding-based work experience?

The CWI page itself does not define it. It names the program standards instead, including "B5.1:2025 - Specification
for the Qualification of Welding Inspectors" and "QC1:2016-AMD1 - Specification for AWS Certification of Welding
Inspectors." If you are unsure whether a past job counts, those AWS documents, or AWS itself, are the place to check.
We do not guess.

## Which forms prove it?

AWS asks applicants to download, review and complete several required documents as part of the application. The page lists an
"Employment Verification Form," a "Visual Acuity Exam Form," an "Additional Qualifying Work Experience Form" and
"Photo ID Requirements."

On ID, the page's FAQ adds: "The name on your government-issued ID needs to exactly match the name you provide on your
application."

The vision test is part of the paperwork. The page says candidates "must pass a vision test" and lists the visual
acuity form as a required document. It does not give a vision standard; that detail is not on the page we read.

## What happens after AWS approves the application?

AWS's confirmation comes before you book Parts A and C with Prometric. AWS states: "A candidate must receive a confirmation letter from AWS before
scheduling exams with Prometric." The FAQ adds that "eligibility codes for Parts A and C are created and emailed
directly to the candidate" once an application is processed.

The CWI exam has three parts (A, B and C), and the page states that "Candidates must achieve a minimum score of 72% on
each exam part to obtain the CWI credential." On order, the FAQ says: "All candidates are required to take the Part B
Practical portion of the CWI Exam before Parts A and C." Holders of International Institute of Welding (IIW) diplomas
are an exception for one part: the page says they "can bypass the CWI Part A (Fundamental) exam."

## What the page does not answer

The CWI page we read does not say how long you have to pass or retake a part, and it does not define qualifying work.
Fees are on AWS's page but not repeated here, because they can change.

## Where federal safety rules fit

The CWI is a private credential. OSHA's welding rules are a separate federal matter, covered in our
[OSHA general industry](/osha-general-industry/) and [OSHA construction](/osha/) study materials. More welding topics
are collected on our [welding page](/welding/).

## Before you apply

These are AWS's program rules as shown on its page on the date above. AWS can change its requirements, forms and
standards, so confirm the current rules with AWS before you apply.
