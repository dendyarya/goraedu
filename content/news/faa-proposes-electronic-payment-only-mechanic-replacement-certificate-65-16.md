The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) published a **proposed rule** (notice of proposed rulemaking) titled
"Modernizing to Electronic Payments" in the Federal Register on November 17, 2026 (Docket No. FAA-2026-3538). FAA
"proposes to modernize its payment systems to require electronic payments and eliminate the option to pay by paper
payment methods such as cash, money orders, and paper checks." Comments are due **on or before January 4, 2027**.
It is a proposal: until FAA publishes a final rule, the current text of the regulations stays in force.

The proposal touches 14 CFR parts 13, 47, 49, 61, 63, 65, 187 and 406. This post covers the part that matters to
aviation maintenance technicians: Part 65, the rules for mechanic certificates.

## What changes for Part 65 airmen

Section 65.16 covers a change of name and the replacement of a lost or destroyed certificate. FAA's document notes
that, under 65.1, the airmen in Part 65 "include air-traffic control-tower operators, aircraft dispatchers,
mechanics, repairmen, and parachute riggers."

- **Replacement airman certificate, 65.16(b)(2).** Today the request letter must "Be accompanied by a check or money
  order for $2, payable to the Federal Aviation Administration." The proposed text reads: "Be accompanied by an
  electronic payment of $2 to the Federal Aviation Administration, unless another payment method is otherwise
  authorized by law."
- **Replacement medical certificate, 65.16(c).** Today the letter goes to the Aerospace Medical Certification Division
  at Post Office Box 26200, "accompanied by a check or money order for $2.00." The proposal would require "an
  electronic payment of $2" (with the same "unless another payment method is otherwise authorized by law" wording)
  and change the box number. FAA writes that it proposes to change the P.O. Box number in 65.16(c) from 26200 to
  25082 "because the address has changed."

The fee itself stays at $2 in the proposed text. Paragraphs (a) and (d) of 65.16 are not among the amended
paragraphs.

FAA states that the regulatory text differs from its current practice: it accepts electronic payments for the fees in 65.16
(and in 47.17, 49.15, 61.29, 63.16 and 406.9) "even though the regulatory language for each section does not mention
the ability to pay by electronic methods." In a footnote, FAA says its web pages and the Registry's
Airmen Services website route people "to Pay.gov to pay the appropriate fee."

## Civil penalty payments

The proposal would also change how civil penalties are paid under 14 CFR 13.18, the section for "individuals acting
as pilots, flight engineers, mechanics, or repairmen." FAA proposes to remove the paper payment option there and
require electronic payment "unless another payment method is otherwise authorized by law." The amounts of penalties
are not part of this change.

## Why FAA proposes it

FAA states that the proposal complies with Executive Order 14247, "Modernizing Payments To and From America's Bank
Account," and that the amendments "would standardize FAA's regulations with current practice and guidance." FAA
expects the costs to customers "to be de minimis to transition from paper to electronic payments."

## What this means for exam study

The document does not mention the mechanic knowledge, oral or practical tests, and it does not change who may hold a
mechanic certificate or what its ratings allow. It concerns only how fees and penalties are paid. Because it is a
proposal, the current 65.16 text (check or money order) is still the regulation as of November 17, 2026. If FAA
publishes a final rule, we will cover it as a new post. For the Part 65 rules on who can get a mechanic certificate,
see our [eligibility and ratings study guide](/faa-amt/amt-eligibility-ratings/study-guide/).
