A question for people studying for a Commercial Driver's License (CDL) who also drive on a farm: does a farm truck
driver need the DOT medical card (the medical examiner's certificate under Part 391 Subpart E) and the other driver qualification steps in 49 CFR Part 391? The federal rules
have several farm exceptions, and each one depends on exact definitions. This explainer sets out what 49 CFR 391.2,
391.67 and 390.39 say. Nothing here describes a rule change, and it does not cover any State's own rules.

## Exception 1: "farm vehicle drivers" (391.2(c))

49 CFR 391.2(c) states: "The rules in this part, except for § 391.15(e) and (f), do not apply to a farm vehicle driver
except a farm vehicle driver who drives an articulated (combination) commercial motor vehicle, as defined in § 390.5
of this chapter."

"Farm vehicle driver" is defined in 49 CFR 390.5 as "a person who drives only a commercial motor vehicle that is—" all
of the following:

1. "Controlled and operated by a farmer as a private motor carrier of property;"
2. "Being used to transport either— (i) Agricultural products, or (ii) Farm machinery, farm supplies, or both, to or
   from a farm;"
3. "Not being used in the operation of a for-hire motor carrier;"
4. "Not carrying hazardous materials of a type or quantity that requires the commercial motor vehicle to be placarded
   in accordance with § 177.823 of this subtitle; and"
5. "Being used within 150 air-miles of the farmer's farm."

The two sections kept by the exception, 391.15(e) and (f), are the disqualifications for texting while driving and
for using a hand-held mobile telephone while driving.

## Drivers of articulated (combination) farm vehicles (391.67)

A farm vehicle driver of an articulated vehicle is not covered by 391.2(c), but has a narrower exception in 49 CFR
391.67. For a farm vehicle driver "who is 18 years of age or older and who drives an articulated commercial motor
vehicle," these rules do not apply: "Section 391.11(b)(1) and (7)" (in 391.11, those are the minimum age of 21 and the
road test), "Subpart C" (background and driving record inquiries), "Subpart D (relating to road tests)" and "Subpart F
(relating to maintenance of files and records)."

Subpart E, the physical qualification and medical examination rules, is not on the 391.67 list.

## Exception 2: "covered farm vehicles" (391.2(d) and 390.39)

49 CFR 391.2(d) states: "The rules in part 391, Subpart E—Physical Qualifications and Examinations—do not apply to
drivers of 'covered farm vehicles,' as defined in 49 CFR 390.5."

49 CFR 390.39(a) gives the full list: a covered farm vehicle, "including the individual operating that vehicle," is
exempt from "Any requirement relating to commercial driver's licenses in 49 CFR Part 383 or controlled substances and
alcohol use and testing in 49 CFR Part 382," from Part 391 Subpart E, from Part 395 (hours of service) and from Part 396
(inspection, repair, and maintenance).

Under 390.5, a covered farm vehicle is "a straight truck or articulated vehicle" that is:

- "Registered in a State with a license plate or other designation issued by the State of registration that allows
  law enforcement officials to identify it as a farm vehicle;"
- "Operated by the owner or operator of a farm or ranch, or an employee or family member of an owner or operator of a
  farm or ranch;"
- "Used to transport agricultural commodities, livestock, machinery or supplies to or from a farm or ranch; and"
- "Not used in for-hire motor carrier operations," with a stated carve-out for a tenant, under a crop share farm lease
  agreement, using a vehicle that meets the first three conditions to haul the landlord's portion of the crops.

Weight sets where the exemption can be used. A covered farm vehicle whose gross vehicle weight rating, gross
combination weight rating, gross vehicle weight or gross combination weight, whichever is greater, is 26,001 pounds or
less "may utilize the exemptions in §
390.39 anywhere in the United States." Above 26,001 pounds, it may use them "anywhere in the State of registration or
across State lines within 150 air miles of the farm or ranch with respect to which the vehicle is being operated."

## Placarded hazardous materials and State rules

Section 390.39(b) says federal transportation funding to a State may not be "terminated, limited, or otherwise
interfered with" because the State exempts a covered farm vehicle and its driver from the requirements in 390.39(a) or
from any other State minimum standard on operating that vehicle, but 390.39(b)(2) states that this "does not apply with respect to
a covered farm vehicle transporting hazardous materials that require a placard." This post does not cover what any
State requires.

## Using more than one exception

Section 390.39(c) says the covered farm vehicle exemptions "are in addition to, not in place of," other agricultural
exceptions, including 391.2(a), (b) and (c) and 391.67, and that "Motor carriers and drivers may utilize any
combination of these exemptions and exceptions, providing they comply fully with each separate exemption and
exception." The same paragraph also lists the farm exceptions in 383.3 and 395.1, which this post does not cover.
