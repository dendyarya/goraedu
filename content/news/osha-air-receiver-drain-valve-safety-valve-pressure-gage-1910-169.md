An air receiver is a tank that holds compressed air. OSHA's general industry rules
cover these tanks in 29 CFR 1910.169, "Air receivers," in Subpart M (Compressed Gas and Compressed Air Equipment). This
explainer answers one practical question: what must an air receiver have, and what must be done to keep it in working
order? It reads the section in the eCFR (text current to November 24, 2026). It is an explainer, not a rule change.

## Which equipment does the section cover?

Under 1910.169(a)(1), the section "applies to compressed air receivers, and other equipment used in providing and
utilizing compressed air for performing operations such as cleaning, drilling, hoisting, and chipping."

The same paragraph sets three limits. The section "does not deal with the special problems created by using compressed
air to convey materials nor the problems created when men work in compressed air as in tunnels and caissons." It also
"is not intended to apply to compressed air machinery and equipment used on transportation vehicles such as steam
railroad cars, electric railway cars, and automotive equipment."

## How must a receiver be built?

1910.169(a)(2)(i) states that "All new air receivers installed after the effective date of these regulations shall be
constructed in accordance with the 1968 edition of the A.S.M.E. Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code Section VIII," which is
incorporated by reference in 1910.6. Under (a)(2)(ii), "All safety valves used shall be constructed, installed, and
maintained in accordance with the A.S.M.E. Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code, Section VIII Edition 1968."

## Where can a receiver be installed?

1910.169(b)(1) requires that receivers "be so installed that all drains, handholes, and manholes therein are easily
accessible." It adds: "Under no circumstances shall an air receiver be buried underground or located in an inaccessible
place."

## Does the tank need a drain, and how often is it drained?

Yes. 1910.169(b)(2) states: "A drain pipe and valve shall be installed at the lowest point of every air receiver to
provide for the removal of accumulated oil and water." Automatic traps are allowed but do not replace the valve:
"Adequate automatic traps may be installed in addition to drain valves."

The rule does not set a fixed number of days. It states: "The drain valve on the air receiver shall be opened and the
receiver completely drained frequently and at such intervals as to prevent the accumulation of excessive amounts of
liquid in the receiver."

## What gage and safety valves are required?

1910.169(b)(3)(i) states: "Every air receiver shall be equipped with an indicating pressure gage (so located as to be
readily visible) and with one or more spring-loaded safety valves." The same paragraph sets how much the valves must
relieve: their "total relieving capacity" must "prevent pressure in the receiver from exceeding the maximum allowable
working pressure of the receiver by more than 10 percent."

Three more rules protect those valves:

- **Nothing in between:** "No valve of any type shall be placed between the air receiver and its safety valve or
  valves" (1910.169(b)(3)(ii)).
- **Hard to defeat:** "Safety appliances, such as safety valves, indicating devices and controlling devices, shall be
  constructed, located, and installed so that they cannot be readily rendered inoperative by any means, including the
  elements" (1910.169(b)(3)(iii)).
- **Testing:** "All safety valves shall be tested frequently and at regular intervals to determine whether they are in
  good operating condition" (1910.169(b)(3)(iv)). As with draining, the section does not give a fixed interval.

## Quick list

- Drains, handholes and manholes easily accessible; never buried or in an inaccessible place.
- Drain pipe and valve at the lowest point; drained completely, frequently enough to prevent excessive liquid.
- Readily visible indicating pressure gage.
- One or more spring-loaded safety valves sized so pressure cannot exceed the maximum allowable working pressure by more
  than 10 percent.
- No valve of any type between the receiver and its safety valves.
- Safety valves tested frequently and at regular intervals.
