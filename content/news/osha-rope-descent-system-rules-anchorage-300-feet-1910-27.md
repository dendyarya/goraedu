OSHA's general industry rule on rope descent systems is 29 CFR 1910.27(b), in Subpart D (Walking-Working Surfaces). This
explainer asks one question: what must be in place before a worker uses a rope descent system, and what rules apply
during the descent? It describes the regulation as published in the eCFR on the date checked (November 21, 2026);
nothing here describes a rule change.

## What counts as a rope descent system?

Section 1910.21(b) defines it as "a suspension system that allows an employee to descend in a controlled manner and, as
needed, stop at any point during the descent." It "usually consists of a roof anchorage, support rope, a descent
device, carabiner(s) or shackle(s), and a chair (seatboard)." The same definition states: "Rope descent systems do not include industrial rope access systems."

Scaffolds are separate: under 1910.27(a), they "must meet the requirements in 29 CFR part 1926, subpart L
(Scaffolds)."

## What has to happen before anyone is attached?

The building owner has to put the anchorage information in writing. Section 1910.27(b)(1)(i) states: "Before any rope
descent system is used, the building owner must inform the employer, in writing that the building owner has
identified, tested, certified, and maintained each anchorage so it is capable of supporting at least 5,000 pounds
(2,268 kg), in any direction, for each employee attached."

The same paragraph sets the schedule: the information "must be based on an annual inspection by a qualified person and
certification of each anchorage by a qualified person, as necessary, and at least every 10 years."

The employer then holds the paperwork. Under 1910.27(b)(1)(ii), "The employer must ensure that no employee uses any
anchorage before the employer has obtained written information from the building owner that each anchorage meets the requirements of paragraph (b)(1)(i) of this section," and "The employer must keep
the information for the duration of the job."

## Is there a height limit?

Yes, with a stated exception. Section 1910.27(b)(2)(i) requires that "No rope descent system is used for heights
greater than 300 feet (91 m) above grade unless the employer demonstrates that it is not feasible to access such
heights by any other means or that those means pose a greater hazard than using a rope descent system."

A second height trigger is lower: under 1910.27(b)(2)(x), "Stabilization is provided at the specific work location
when descents are greater than 130 feet (39.6 m)."

## Does the worker need a separate fall arrest system?

Yes. Section 1910.27(b)(2)(vi) requires that "Each employee uses a separate, independent personal fall arrest system
that meets the requirements of subpart I of this part." Section 1910.28(b)(12)(ii) states the same duty from the fall
protection side: "Each employee using a rope descent system 4 feet (1.2 m) or more above a lower level is protected
from falling by a personal fall arrest system."

Section 1910.27(b)(2)(viii) adds: "Prompt rescue of each employee is provided in the event of a fall."

## How strong must the equipment be, and how often is it inspected?

Under 1910.27(b)(2)(vii), "All components of each rope descent system, except seat boards, are capable of sustaining a
minimum rated load of 5,000 pounds (22.2 kN). Seat boards must be capable of supporting a live load of 300 pounds (136
kg)."

Under 1910.27(b)(2)(iv), the system "is inspected at the start of each workshift that it is to be used," and the
employer "must ensure damaged or defective equipment is removed from service immediately and replaced." It must be
used "in accordance with instructions, warnings, and design limitations set by the manufacturer or under the direction
of a qualified person" ((b)(2)(ii)).

## What other conditions apply during the work?

The rest of 1910.27(b)(2) requires:

- **Rigging:** "proper rigging, including anchorages and tiebacks, with particular emphasis on providing tiebacks when
  counterweights, cornice hooks, or similar non-permanent anchorages are used" ((b)(2)(v)).
- **Rope protection at edges:** ropes "effectively padded or otherwise protected, where they can contact edges of the
  building, anchorage, obstructions, or other surfaces" ((b)(2)(ix)).
- **Weather:** "No employee uses a rope descent system when hazardous weather conditions, such as storms or gusty or
  excessive wind, are present" ((b)(2)(xi)).
- **Dropped objects:** "Equipment, such as tools, squeegees, or buckets, is secured by a tool lanyard or similar
  method to prevent it from falling" ((b)(2)(xii)).
- **Heat and chemicals:** ropes "protected from exposure to open flames, hot work, corrosive chemicals, and other
  destructive conditions" ((b)(2)(xiii)).

## What training is required?

Section 1910.27(b)(2)(iii) requires that "Each employee who uses the rope descent system is trained in accordance with §
1910.30." That section states, in 1910.30(b)(3): "The employer must train each employee who uses a rope descent system
in proper rigging and use of the equipment in accordance with § 1910.27." Under 1910.30(a)(2), "each employee is trained
by a qualified person."
