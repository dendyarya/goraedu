Electrician licensing is set by each state (and in some places by a city). This explainer covers **Washington State
only**, where electricians and electrical trainees are certified by the Department of Labor & Industries (L&I). It
answers one practical question from L&I's own pages: when you work as an electrical trainee, which hours count toward
the electrician exam, and which do not? Nothing here describes a rule change, and nothing here applies to any other
state.

## Do you need a certificate to work as an electrical trainee?

Yes. L&I's Electrician page states: "To work in the electrical construction trade in Washington, you must have a valid
electrician certificate issued by L&I." For people who are not yet electricians, the Electrical Trainee page states:
"Electrical trainees and apprentices must have a training certificate" and "work under the supervision of a certified
electrician."

Under "To get started as an electrical trainee," L&I lists: "Trainees must be 16 years or older."

## What supervision does L&I require?

The Electrical Trainee page states: "You and the electrician supervising you must be on the same jobsite." It gives
the ratios this way: "In general, supervision ratios are:"

- "1 trainee to 1 general journey level electrician when doing journey level type work, or"
- "2 trainees to 1 specialty electrician when doing specialty type work."

For the specialties that need 2,000 hours of experience, L&I's Electrician page states: "Trainees must be
supervised by a specialty electrician certified to do the kind of work being done or by a journey level electrician and
be supervised 100% of the time until they pass examination."

## Which hours do not count?

L&I's Electrical Trainee page answers this under "What hours do not count?" It starts: "Experience gained by
violating the electrical laws does not count toward qualifying for an electrician exam." The items it lists include:

- "Working without a training certificate, or working with an expired one."
- "Working for an unlicensed electrical contractor or one that has an expired, suspended, or revoked license."
- "When not properly supervised by a certified electrician."
- "Working out of ratio."

## How long does the trainee certificate last?

Two years. L&I states that trainees "must renew their certificate every 2 years until they pass an exam." Under
"Trainee responsibilities," the page says: "Renewal requires 48 hours of Basic Classroom Instruction."

## When are affidavits of experience due?

Hours are reported on affidavits of experience, and L&I sets a deadline: "L&I must receive your affidavit(s) for the
previous two years no later than 180 days after each expiration date printed on your training certificate." L&I tells
trainees to "request affidavits of experience from your employers," and says: "They have 20 days to provide them to
you." L&I notes that hours covered by Washington or recognized Oregon apprenticeship programs are not subject to
the 180-day deadline.

The page puts its warning in capitals: "YOU WILL LOSE CREDIT FOR YOUR HOURS IF YOUR AFFIDAVIT IS LATE."

## How many hours lead to the journey level exam?

For in-state applicants, L&I's Electrician page lists for a journey level (01) exam: "Hours of education: Basic
Classroom Instruction– 96 for (01) journey level exams, 48 for 4,000 hour specialties." For work experience: "For a 01
journey level exam– 8,000 hours, no more than 4,000 of which can be worked in 4,000 hour specialties."

Once certified, the page states: "You must renew your electrician certificate every 3 years. It expires on your
birthdate." Renewal needs "24 hours of approved continuing education credit including 8 hours of code update, 4 hours
of RCW/WAC," and L&I adds: "Not renewable if expired more than 90 days; retest is your only option."

## How this relates to OSHA's "qualified person"

A state electrician certificate is not the same thing as OSHA's federal "qualified person" training rules for
workplace electrical safety. Our earlier explainer covers that difference:
[OSHA qualified person versus an electrician license](/news/osha-qualified-person-electrical-work-vs-electrician-license/).
For OSHA's construction electrical rules, see the [OSHA construction safety](/osha/) study material.
