Before some small surface water systems move their point of disinfection or switch disinfectants, federal rules require
groundwork. For these systems, 40 CFR Part 141, Subpart T ("Enhanced Filtration and Disinfection—Systems Serving
Fewer Than 10,000 People") first asks for a year-long record of disinfection, called a
profile, and then a benchmark drawn from it. This explainer reads sections 141.501 and 141.530 to 141.544 as they stand
in the eCFR, current to November 19, 2026. It does not describe a rule change.

## Which systems does Subpart T cover?

Section 141.501 says a system is subject to Subpart T if it "(a) Is a public water system; (b) Uses surface water or
GWUDI as a source; and (c) Serves fewer than 10,000 persons."

## What is a disinfection profile, and who must develop one?

Section 141.530 defines it: "A disinfection profile is a graphical representation of your system's level of Giardia
lamblia or virus inactivation measured during the course of a year." The profiling duty is narrower. Under 141.530, "If you are a subpart H community or non-transient non-community water system which serves
fewer than 10,000 persons, your system must develop a disinfection profile unless your State determines that your
system's profile is unnecessary."

## When can the State say a profile is unnecessary?

Only on disinfection byproduct results. Section 141.531 states: "States may only determine that a system's profile is
unnecessary if a system's TTHM and HAA5 levels are below 0.064 mg/L and 0.048 mg/L, respectively." The samples count
only if "collected after January 1, 1998, during the month with the warmest water temperature, and at the point of
maximum residence time in your distribution system." The State may approve a more representative TTHM and HAA5 data set.

## When did data collection have to start?

Section 141.532(a) requires 12 months of plant data and states the start dates by size: "If your system serves between
500 and 9,999 persons you must begin to collect data no later than July 1, 2003. If your system serves fewer than 500
persons you must begin to collect data no later than January 1, 2004." These are dates the rule states, not upcoming
deadlines.

## What data goes into the profile?

Section 141.533 requires monitoring "once per week on the same calendar day, over 12 consecutive months," using the
methods in 141.74(a), of water temperature, pH (if the system uses chlorine), disinfectant contact time (“T”) and residual disinfectant
concentration (“C”), all during peak hourly flow.

Under 141.534, the system uses the CT99.9 tables in 141.74(b)(3)(v) to calculate a total inactivation ratio and must
"multiply the value by 3.0 to determine log inactivation of Giardia lamblia." If the system uses chloramines, ozone or chlorine dioxide
for primary disinfection, 141.535 requires it to "develop an additional disinfection profile for viruses using methods
approved by the State."

The result is 52 weekly values. Section 141.536 states that the system "must retain the Disinfection Profile data in
graphic form, such as a spreadsheet, which must be available for review by the State as part of a sanitary survey."

## How is the benchmark calculated?

Section 141.543 sets two steps. First, average the Giardia lamblia inactivation values for each calendar month. Then,
in the rule's words: "Determine the lowest monthly average value out of the twelve values. This value becomes the
disinfection benchmark." Under 141.544, systems using chloramines, ozone or chlorine dioxide for primary disinfection
also calculate a viral benchmark "in the same manner."

## What counts as a significant change to disinfection practice?

Section 141.541 lists four: "(a) Changes to the point of disinfection; (b) Changes to the disinfectant(s) used in the
treatment plant; (c) Changes to the disinfection process; or (d) Any other modification identified by the State."

## What must the system do before making the change?

Section 141.540 applies to a system "required to develop a disinfection profile under §§ 141.530 through 141.536." It
states that the system "must develop a Disinfection Benchmark if you decide to make a significant change to your
disinfection practice" and "must consult with the State for approval before you can implement a significant
disinfection practice change."

Section 141.542 lists what goes to the State as part of that consultation:

1. "A description of the proposed change;"
2. "The disinfection profile for Giardia lamblia (and, if necessary, viruses) and disinfection benchmark;"
3. "An analysis of how the proposed change will affect the current levels of disinfection; and"
4. "Any additional information requested by the State."

## What this means for operator exam study

The sources do not mention operator certification exams, so check your State's requirements as well. Points that
come straight from the rule: who must profile (subpart H community and non-transient non-community systems under 10,000 people), the TTHM and
HAA5 levels that let a State decide a profile is unnecessary, weekly sampling during peak hourly flow for 12 months,
the benchmark as the lowest monthly average, and the four kinds of significant change.
