When a water source is over a maximum contaminant level (MCL), two shortcuts come up: deliver bottled water, or put a
treatment unit in each building instead of treating at the plant. For people preparing for drinking water operator
certification, the federal rules answer both in a few short sections of 40 CFR Parts 141 and 142. This explainer
quotes them from the eCFR (up to date as of November 17, 2026). Nothing here describes a rule change. Your State drinking water
program may have its own requirements, so check them too.

## Can bottled water be used to comply with an MCL?

No. 40 CFR 141.101 is two sentences long: "Public water systems shall not use bottled water to achieve compliance
with an MCL. Bottled water may be used on a temporary basis to avoid unreasonable risk to health."

So bottled water can protect people for a time, but it does not make the system compliant with the MCL.

## What are point-of-entry and point-of-use devices?

40 CFR 141.2 defines both:

- "Point-of-entry treatment device (POE) is a treatment device applied to the drinking water entering a house or
  building for the purpose of reducing contaminants in the drinking water distributed throughout the house or
  building."
- "Point-of-use treatment device or point of use device (POU) is a water treatment device physically installed or
  connected to a single fixture, outlet, or tap to reduce or remove contaminants in drinking water."

## Can point-of-entry devices be used to meet an MCL?

Yes, but only under 40 CFR 141.100: "Public water systems may use point-of-entry devices to comply with maximum
contaminant levels only if they meet the requirements of this section." The conditions:

- The system runs them: "It is the responsibility of the public water system to operate and maintain the
  point-of-entry treatment system" (141.100(b)).
- A State-approved monitoring plan comes first: the system "must develop and obtain State approval for a monitoring
  plan before point-of-entry devices are installed for compliance," and the devices "must provide health protection
  equivalent to central water treatment" (141.100(c)). Monitoring must include "physical measurements and observations
  such as total flow treated and mechanical condition of the treatment equipment."
- "Effective technology must be properly applied under a plan approved by the State and the microbiological safety of
  the water must be maintained" (141.100(d)). The State "must require adequate certification of performance, field
  testing, and, if not included in the certification process, a rigorous engineering design review" ((d)(1)).
- The rule warns about "the tendency for increase in heterotrophic bacteria concentrations in water treated with
  activated carbon" and says "frequent backwashing, post-contactor disinfection, and Heterotrophic Plate Count
  monitoring" may be necessary ((d)(2)).
- Every building is covered: "All consumers shall be protected. Every building connected to the system must have a
  point-of-entry device installed, maintained, and adequately monitored" (141.100(e)). The State must also be assured
  "that the rights and responsibilities of the public water system customer convey with title upon sale of property."

## When can a State require bottled water or POU/POE devices?

Part 142 covers State-issued variances and exemptions. Under 40 CFR 142.57(a), "A State may require a public water
system to use bottled water, point-of-use devices, or point-of-entry devices as a condition of granting an exemption
from the requirements of §§ 141.61 (a) and (c), and 141.62 of this chapter." Under 40 CFR 142.62(f), the State may also require bottled water, point-of-use devices,
point-of-entry devices or other means as a condition of granting a variance or an exemption from §§ 141.61(a) and (c)
and 141.62, to avoid an unreasonable risk to health.

When bottled water is used as a condition of a variance or exemption, 40 CFR 142.62(g) adds requirements. The system
either runs a monitoring program approved by the Administrator or primacy State, sampling "a representative sample of
the bottled water" during "the first three-month period that it supplies the bottled water to the public, and annually
thereafter" ((g)(1)), or gets a certification from the bottled water company under the 21 CFR bottled water rules the section names
(21 CFR 129.3(a), 129.80(g), 165.110, and parts 110 and 129) ((g)(2)). In both cases, "The public water system is fully responsible for the provision of sufficient quantities of
bottled water to every person supplied by the public water system via door-to-door bottled water delivery" ((g)(3)).

For POU or POE devices used as a condition of a variance or exemption, 142.62(h) repeats the core conditions: the
system operates and maintains them, a monitoring plan is approved "Before point-of-use or point-of-entry devices are
installed," and "The microbiological safety of the water must be maintained at all times." Where a POE device is a
condition of an exemption from the lead and copper treatment requirements under § 141.83 or § 141.84, the State "must be assured that use of the device will not cause
increased corrosion of lead and copper bearing materials located between the device and the tap" ((h)(7)).
