This explainer covers **South Carolina only**, where home inspectors are licensed by the South Carolina Residential
Builders Commission, part of the Department of Labor, Licensing and Regulation (LLR). It answers one question from the
commission's own pages and Chapter 106 of the state's regulations: what does South Carolina ask for to get a home
inspector license? Nothing here describes a rule change, and nothing here applies to any other state.

## What are the ways to get licensed?

The commission's home inspector page describes three paths: the SC Home Inspector exam, the NHIE examination, and
endorsement: "Individuals currently licensed in other states by examination may apply for licensure by endorsement."

The same page sets limits on endorsement: "Grandfather licenses do not qualify for licensure by endorsement/without
technical examination."

## What education or experience do I need before the exam?

Regulation 106-4 is headed "Qualifications for Home Inspectors." For licensure by examination, the commission's
instructions page quotes two ways to qualify. One is experience: the applicant "Has a minimum of one year of experience
as a home inspector under the direction and direct supervision of a licensed home inspector, residential builder,
general contractor, engineer, or architect;". The other is education: the applicant "Has successfully completed formal
course of training or study in home inspection approved by the Commission."

On the course, the instructions page states: "The commission has approved a course with a minimum of 120 hours" and adds
"(one 120 hour course, not multiple courses that add up to 120 hours)." It also states: "Course completion certificates
are valid for three (3) years."

The commission's FAQ adds a time limit on experience: "The experience must have been gained within the preceding five
(5) years of application."

## What does the exam look like?

The exam page states that applicants "must take a two-part examination for licensure in South Carolina." The parts are
the "Home Inspector: Technical Examination (100 multiple-choice questions)" and the "Business Management and Law
Examination (50 multiple-choice questions)". On scores, it states: "Applicants must obtain a passing score of 70% on the
Technical Examination." It also states: "Applicants must obtain a scale score of 68 to pass the Business Management and
Law Examination."

Answering a question about applicants licensed in another state, the FAQ says the commission accepts the national exam
as well as the state technical exam: "We do accept the National Home Inspector exam as well as the SC Home Inspector
exam". The same answer adds that "you are still required to take our SC Business Management and Law exam."

The exam page states: "Examinations cannot be scheduled prior to obtaining an Examination Eligibility Letter from the
Commission". It adds: "All examination fees are paid directly to PSI." The FAQ adds: "Your Examination Eligibility is
valid for one year and you are allowed only three (3) attempts to pass" within a twelve-month period.

## What if I am already licensed or certified somewhere else?

Regulation 106-4(A), on licensure without examination, asks the applicant to be "currently certified or licensed as a
building or home inspector after written examination" by an organization the commission approves. As quoted on the
instructions page, the applicant must also show either one year of supervised experience or that they have "performed a
minimum of fifty residential inspections, as evidenced by the name, address, telephone number, and summary page of each
inspection being submitted for review."

## How does the application work?

The home inspector page states: "This electronic application is only available for first-time applicants." Once an
application is submitted, "you have one (1) year to complete and submit all required information." The fee amounts are
stated on that page.

## How often is the license renewed?

The FAQ states: "The Residential Home Inspectors License is renewable every two (2) years, before June 30, of each even
year". The FAQ warns: "Your license will lapse on June 30 if you do not renew it."

On the home inspector page: "If you have a license that has been lapsed or inactive for less than three years, you may
apply for reinstatement." After more than three years, "you will need to apply for a license as a new applicant, which
requires reexamination."

## What the pages do not state

The pages used here do not state a continuing education requirement for home inspectors, any insurance or bond
requirement, or which topics the 120-hour course must cover. Check those points with the commission directly.

of the state's regulations on the date above. The commission is the authority, so confirm the current rules, forms and
fees with it before you enroll in a course, book an exam or apply. These requirements describe South Carolina only;
