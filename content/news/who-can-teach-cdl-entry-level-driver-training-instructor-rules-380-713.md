Before an entry-level driver takes certain commercial driver's license (CDL) tests (the skills test for a first or
upgraded Class A or B CDL or a first passenger or school bus endorsement, or the knowledge test for a first hazardous
materials endorsement), they must complete entry-level driver training from a provider listed on FMCSA's Training Provider Registry (TPR). This explainer answers
one practical question for drivers thinking about teaching that training: who is allowed to be the instructor? The
answer is in 49 CFR 380.713 and the instructor definitions in 49 CFR 380.605, read in the eCFR (text up to date as of
November 25, 2026). It is an explainer, not a rule change.

## What does 380.713 require?

Section 380.713 is short. It has two paragraphs:

- 380.713(a): "Theory training providers must utilize instructors who are theory instructors as defined in § 380.605."
- 380.713(b): "BTW training providers must utilize instructors who are BTW instructors as defined in § 380.605."

BTW means behind-the-wheel. The instructor rule is also one of the conditions for being listed on the registry: under
380.703(a)(4), to be eligible for listing an entity must "Utilize driver training instructors that meet the criteria set
forth in § 380.713". So the real detail is in the two definitions.

## Who counts as a theory instructor?

Section 380.605 defines a theory instructor as "an individual who provides knowledge instruction on the operation of a
CMV" and who meets one of two qualifications. Both start the same way: the person "Holds a CDL of the same (or higher)
class and with all endorsements necessary to operate the CMV for which training is to be provided". Then:

1. the person "has at least 2 years of experience driving a CMV requiring a CDL of the same (or higher) class and/or the
   same endorsement and meets all applicable State qualification requirements for CMV instructors"; or
2. the person "has at least 2 years of experience as a BTW CMV instructor and meets all applicable State qualification
   requirements for CMV instructors."

The definition lists two exceptions for theory instructors:

- **A former CDL holder.** "An instructor is not required to hold a CDL of the same (or higher) class and with all
  endorsements necessary to operate the CMV for which training is to be provided, if the instructor previously held a
  CDL of the same (or higher) class and complies with the other requirements set forth in paragraphs (1) or (2) of this
  definition."
- **Online-only providers.** "Training providers offering online content exclusively are not required to meet State
  qualification requirements for theory instructors." Section 380.703(a)(5)(ii) says the same for listing: "State
  qualification requirements otherwise applicable to theory instruction do not apply to providers offering such
  instruction only online."

## Who counts as a behind-the-wheel instructor?

The same section defines a BTW instructor as "an individual who provides BTW training involving the actual operation of
a CMV by an entry-level driver on a range or a public road". The two qualification paths are worded the same way as for
theory instructors: a CDL of the same (or higher) class with all necessary endorsements, plus either at least 2 years of
experience driving a CMV requiring a CDL of the same or higher class and/or the same endorsement, or at least 2 years
of experience as a BTW CMV instructor, and in both cases meeting "all applicable State qualification requirements for
CMV instructors."

The BTW exception is narrower. It applies only to range training: "A BTW instructor who provides training solely on a
range which is not a public road is not required to hold a CDL of the same (or higher) class and with all endorsements
necessary to operate the CMV for which training is to be provided, as long as the instructor previously held a CDL of
the same (or higher) class and with all endorsements necessary to operate the CMV for which training is to be
provided, and complies with the other requirements set forth in paragraphs (1) or (2) of this definition." An
instructor teaching on a public road has no such exception in the definition.

## What if the instructor's own CDL was disqualified?

Both definitions have a third paragraph that bars some people for a time. For theory instruction: "If an instructor's
CDL has been cancelled, suspended, or revoked due to any of the disqualifying offenses identified in § 383.51 of this
subchapter, the instructor is prohibited from engaging in theory instruction for 2 years following the date his or her
CDL is reinstated." The BTW definition says the same, with "BTW instruction" in place of "theory instruction."

Note that the 2-year clock in that paragraph starts on the date the CDL is reinstated, not on the date of the offense.

## Does the training provider itself need State approval?

The federal rule also points to the States for the provider. Under 380.703(a)(5)(i), to be listed an entity must "Be
licensed, certified, registered, or authorized to provide training in accordance with the applicable laws and
regulations of any State where in-person training is conducted." The eCFR text does not list what each State requires,
so a prospective instructor or school also has to check the State's own rules.

## What this means for CDL study

Instructor qualifications are a rule for training providers, and these sections do not mention the knowledge or skills tests' content. For the parts of the CDL rules that drivers study,

## Where to check before you act

These are the requirements as shown in the eCFR on the date checked. FMCSA is the authority, and Subpart G of Part 380
also sets rules on curriculum, facilities, equipment and continued listing that this post does not explain, so read the
full sections, and your State's instructor and school rules, before relying on them.
