OSHA's general industry standard 29 CFR 1910.120, "Hazardous waste operations and emergency response", is called
HAZWOPER here, from the initials of its title. This explainer answers one question from that text: who needs the
training, and how many hours does the rule set? It describes the current eCFR text, not a rule change.

## Which work does 1910.120 cover?

Section 1910.120(a)(1) lists five kinds of operations, and it covers them "unless the employer can demonstrate that the
operation does not involve employee exposure or the reasonable possibility for employee exposure to safety or health
hazards":

- (i) clean-up operations "required by a governmental body" at "uncontrolled hazardous waste sites";
- (ii) "Corrective actions involving clean-up operations" at sites covered by the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act
  (RCRA);
- (iii) "Voluntary clean-up operations" at sites a governmental body recognizes as uncontrolled hazardous waste sites;
- (iv) hazardous waste operations at "treatment, storage, and disposal (TSD) facilities" regulated under RCRA;
- (v) "Emergency response operations for releases of, or substantial threats of releases of, hazardous substances
  without regard to the location of the hazard."

Under 1910.120(a)(2)(ii), clean-up operations in (a)(1)(i) to (iii) "must comply with all paragraphs of this section
except paragraphs (p) and (q)", so their training is in paragraph (e). Under (a)(2)(iii), TSD operations in (a)(1)(iv)
"must comply only with the requirements of paragraph (p)". Paragraph (q) "covers employers whose employees are engaged
in emergency response no matter where it occurs", except the operations in (a)(1)(i) to (iv); emergencies at clean-up
sites are under (e)(7).

## Who must be trained at a clean-up site?

Section 1910.120(e)(1)(i) covers "All employees working on site (such as but not limited to equipment operators, general
laborers and others) exposed to hazardous substances, health hazards, or safety hazards and their supervisors and
management responsible for the site". They must be trained "before they are permitted to engage in hazardous waste
operations that could expose them to hazardous substances, safety, or health hazards".

## How many hours of initial training?

Paragraph (e)(3) sets three levels:

- General site workers "shall receive a minimum of 40 hours of instruction off the site, and a minimum of three days
  actual field experience under the direct supervision of a trained, experienced supervisor" (e)(3)(i).
- Workers "on site only occasionally for a specific limited task" who are "unlikely to be exposed over permissible
  exposure limits and published exposure limits" get "a minimum of 24 hours of instruction off the site, and the minimum
  of one day actual field experience" (e)(3)(ii).
- Workers regularly on site in areas monitored and "fully characterized" as under those limits, "where respirators are
  not necessary", with no health hazards or possibility of an emergency, also get 24 hours and one day (e)(3)(iii).

If a 24-hour worker becomes a general site worker or must wear a respirator, (e)(3)(iv) requires "the additional 16
hours and two days of training".

## What about supervisors and managers?

Under 1910.120(e)(4), on-site management and supervisors "shall receive 40 hours initial training, and three days of
supervised field experience", reduced to 24 hours and one day if they supervise only the 24-hour groups, "and at least
eight additional hours of specialized training at the time of job assignment".

## How often is refresher training required?

Section 1910.120(e)(8) states that covered employees, managers and supervisors "shall receive eight hours of refresher
training annually".

## Who can teach it, and is there a certificate?

Under (e)(5), trainers must have completed "a training program for teaching the subjects they are expected to teach",
or have the academic credentials and instructional experience for those subjects. Under (e)(6), their instructor or the
head instructor and trained supervisor certifies those who complete the training and field experience, and "A written
certificate shall be given to each person so certified." Anyone not so certified, or not meeting (e)(9), "shall be
prohibited from engaging in hazardous waste operations." Paragraph (e)(9) covers equivalent training, but "certified employees or employees with equivalent training
new to a site shall receive appropriate, site specific training before site entry and have appropriate supervised field
experience at the new site".

## Is TSD facility training different?

Yes. Section 1910.120(p)(7)(i) states: "The initial training shall be for 24 hours and refresher training shall be for
eight hours annually." Note (B) under (a)(2)(iii) excludes employers that need no permit or interim status because they
are conditionally exempt small quantity generators or qualify for generator exemptions under 40 CFR 262.34 from (p)(1)
to (p)(7).

## What about emergency responders?

Paragraph (q)(6) sets training by duty. Awareness level responders need "sufficient training" or experience to show
competency. Operations level responders need "at least eight hours of training" or demonstrated competency. Hazardous
materials technicians, specialists and on-scene incident commanders need "at least 24 hours of training" plus listed
competencies. Under (q)(8)(i), they receive "annual refresher training of sufficient content and duration to maintain
their competencies, or shall demonstrate competency in those areas at least yearly."
