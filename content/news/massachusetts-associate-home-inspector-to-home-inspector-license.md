Home inspector licensing is set by each state that regulates the work. This explainer covers **Massachusetts only**,
where home inspectors are licensed by the Board of Registration of Home Inspectors. It answers one practical question from the state's own pages: how do you go from associate
home inspector to home inspector? Nothing here describes a rule change, and nothing here applies to any other state.

## Who needs a license?

Massachusetts General Laws chapter 112, section 222(a) begins: "No person shall present, call or represent himself as
authorized to provide a home inspection for compensation unless" that person holds a license from the Board under
that section and sections 223 to 226.

The Board's regulation, 266 CMR 3.00, sets out the Associate Home Inspector license and the Home Inspector license, plus a path for holders of an equivalent license from another jurisdiction that this post does not cover. We read
the 266 CMR 3.00 and 4.00 versions marked Mass. Register #1567, dated February 13, 2026.

## What does the associate license require?

The associate list in 266 CMR 3.01(2)(a) starts with two personal requirements: the applicant "Is of good moral
character" and "Has successfully completed high school or its equivalent."

It then names a supervisor. The applicant "Has an identified Supervisor(s) who is a licensed Home Inspector in good
standing in the Commonwealth."

The other key items are:

- **Training program.** The applicant "Has documented the successful completion of the Associate Home Inspectors
  Training Program" in 266 CMR 4.00. That regulation states: "Training requirements include the successful completion
  of 75 hours of Board approved Educational Training Hours."
- **Supervised inspections.** The applicant "Has performed and documented the successful completion of not less than
  25 feepaid Home Inspections under the Direct Supervision of a licensed Home Inspector who is in good standing."
- **Exam.** The applicant "Has documented passing the written or electronic examination approved by the Board."
- **Ethics.** The applicant "Agrees to uphold the standards of ethics and professional conduct set forth in" 266 CMR
  8.00.

Mass.gov's associate how-to page turns these into documents, including an "Experience Log showing 25 inspections
completed under direct supervision" and "Documentation of passing score on the National Home Inspectors Exam."

## Can some of the 25 inspections be mock inspections?

Some can. 266 CMR 3.01(2)(a) states: "A total of ten Mock Inspections of the 25 required will be allowed if conducted with and provided by an approved provider in accord with 266 CMR 5.05". Neither 266 CMR 3.00 nor 4.00 defines a mock inspection further. 266 CMR 4.06(5) adds: "In the case of Mock Inspections, a Supervisor may supervise no more
than three Trainees at one time."

## What does "direct supervision" mean during training?

266 CMR 4.00 states: "The Supervisor is responsible for full time, direct, on site supervision of a Trainee's home
inspections." The supervisor's role includes paperwork: "The Supervisor(s) shall certify the statements of the
applicant pertaining to training and inspections."

## Is insurance required?

Yes. The associate how-to page lists "Proof of errors and omissions insurance policy, which shall be a minimum of
$250,000 in the aggregate." The home inspector list in 266 CMR 3.01(2)(b) also requires that the applicant "Has filed
satisfactory proof of the mandatory insurance coverage."

## What does the home inspector license add?

The statute, M.G.L. c. 112, § 222(d)(iii), says an applicant shall "have been engaged as a licensed associate home
inspector for not less than one year and have performed not less than 100 home inspections" under the supervision of
a licensed home inspector.

266 CMR 3.01(2)(b) repeats both points. The applicant "Has been engaged as a licensed Associate Home Inspector for not
less than one year" and "Has performed and documented the successful completion of not less than an additional 100
home inspections under the indirect or direct supervision of a licensed Home Inspector who is in good standing."

Two details in that wording matter. The 100 inspections are "additional," so they come on top of the 25 from the
associate stage. And at this stage the supervision may be "indirect or direct," while the associate's 25 inspections
must be under "Direct Supervision." 266 CMR 4.06(7), headed "Indirect Supervision," states: "Indirect supervision shall only be permitted for an Associate Home Inspector." 266 CMR 3.01 also states: "The computation of the one year period referred to in M.G.L. c. 112, § 222(d)(iii) shall begin upon issuance of the Associate Home Inspector license."

The same list also requires documentation that the applicant "has met the Continuing Education requirements of" 266
CMR 5.01.

## Is there an exam at the home inspector step?

The statute's eligibility list for a home inspector, § 222(d)(iv), includes having "passed a written or electronic
competency examination offered or approved by the board." In 266 CMR 3.01, the exam item appears in the associate
list, not in the home inspector list. If you are unsure how this applies to you, ask the Board.
