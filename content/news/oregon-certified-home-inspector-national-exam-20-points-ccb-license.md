This explainer covers **Oregon only**, where home inspectors are certified by the Oregon Construction Contractors
Board (CCB). It answers one question from the CCB's page, its certification application and its rules in Oregon
Administrative Rules (OAR) chapter 812, division 8: what exam, qualifying points and business license does Oregon ask
for before someone can work as an Oregon certified home inspector? Nothing here describes a rule change, and nothing
here applies to any other state.

## Who needs an Oregon certification?

The CCB's Specialty Licenses page defines the work: "A home inspector, for a fee, inspects and provides a written
report on the overall physical condition of a residential structure." It then states: "Any individual who advertises,
bids, or performs residential home inspections of more than one structural component needs to be certified."

The page lists exceptions, such as a person who "inspects only one component such as roof inspectors." OAR
812-008-0030(4) adds that no person, including those exempt under section (3), "shall use the title Oregon certified
home inspector without receiving such certification from the agency."

## Which exam does Oregon use?

The CCB's page states: "Candidates for certification as an Oregon home inspector must pass the National Home Inspector
Examination." OAR 812-008-0040(1)(e) asks for "Evidence of successful passage of the National Home Inspector
Examination".

Step 1 of the CCB's application (form dated 06/2026) describes the format: "There are 200 multiple choice questions,"
and "It is a four-hour exam". Among the documents to send, the application lists "Proof of passing the National Home
Inspector Exam (Report from PSI)". The exam fee and the CCB's own fees are listed on the CCB's page and application;
check the amounts there.

## What are the 20 qualifying points?

OAR 812-008-0040(1)(d) asks for "Proof of minimum of 20 education points". Section (3) lists the education choices:

- "Ten points for a completed, 3-credit hour minimum class with a passing grade in home inspection at an accredited
  college or university."
- "One point for each completed 3-hour minimum class with a passing grade in construction, remodeling, engineering,
  architecture, building design, building technology, or real estate at an accredited college or university."
- "One point for each completed “ride-along” inspection performed under the direct supervision of an Oregon certified
  home inspector."
- One point for each completed 3-hour minimum class with a passing grade in approved subject areas "by approved
  education providers under 812-008-0074(2) that are not colleges or universities."

The application states: "You must submit a minimum of 20 qualifying points to apply. Copies of documentation
substantiating the qualifying points must be submitted with your application." For ride-alongs, it asks you to "Submit
Home Inspector Continuing Education Ride-Along Credit form."

## Can work experience count instead?

Yes. OAR 812-008-0040(4) says the applicant "may substitute the following experiences for all or part of the education
requirements":

- "Four points for each completed 12 months legally working as a home inspector in Oregon or another state or
  country."
- "Two points for each completed 12 months working or teaching at an accredited college or university, trade school or
  private business for monetary compensation in construction, remodeling, engineering, architecture, building design,
  building technology, real estate, or building inspections."
- "One-half point for each letter of recommendation from an Oregon-certified home inspector."
- "One point for each building codes certification issued by a government agency."

## Is the certificate enough to start working?

No. "Except as provided in section (3) of this rule," OAR 812-008-0030(1) says no individual shall do work as an
Oregon certified home inspector "without first receiving certification to do same from the agency and without being an
owner or employee of a business that is licensed with the agency." Section (3) exempts some persons, such as those
performing an energy audit or a forensic evaluation. Section (2) says the business must be licensed "as a residential
general contractor, a residential specialty contractor, or a home inspection services contractor". The application
states: "You’re not legal to work as a home inspector, until you have been associated with an Active CCB License."

For inspection-only businesses, the CCB's page states: "If you only do home inspections - and do not perform
construction work - you may want the home inspector services contractor endorsement." It adds: "You do not have to
take pre-license education and pass a test to become a contractor." It also says: "Your bond and insurance
requirements are less than those of the residential general or specialty contractor endorsements." Confirm the amounts
with the CCB.

## What happens at renewal?

The application states: "Certified Home Inspectors must complete 30 hours of continuing education from approved
providers to renew their certification every two years." OAR 812-008-0030(6) says certificates are renewable "upon
meeting all requirements, including continuing education."

These are the requirements as shown on the CCB's Specialty Licenses page, its 06/2026 certification application and
OAR 812-008-0030 and 812-008-0040 on the date above. The Oregon Construction Contractors Board is the authority, so
confirm the current forms, fees, approved education providers and exam details with the CCB before you apply. For the
federal lead disclosure and radon topics that home inspectors meet, see our [home inspector topics](/home-inspector/),
