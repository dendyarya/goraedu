Backing vehicles are a hazard on any construction site. Is a backup alarm always required? OSHA's construction
standards answer this in Subpart O, "Motor Vehicles, Mechanized Equipment, and Marine Operations": 29 CFR 1926.601
for motor vehicles, and 1926.602, "Material handling equipment," whose paragraph (a) covers earthmoving equipment.
This explainer sets out what those two sections require. Nothing here describes a rule change.

## Which rule applies to your vehicle?

- **29 CFR 1926.601 (motor vehicles).** Its coverage paragraph states: "Motor vehicles as covered by this part are
  those vehicles that operate within an off-highway jobsite, not open to public traffic. The requirements of this
  section do not apply to equipment for which rules are prescribed in § 1926.602."
- **29 CFR 1926.602(a) (earthmoving equipment).** Its rules "apply to the following types of earthmoving equipment:
  scrapers, loaders, crawler or wheel tractors, bulldozers, off-highway trucks, graders, agricultural and industrial
  tractors, and similar equipment." The same paragraph adds: "The promulgation of specific rules for compactors and
  rubber-tired 'skid-steer' equipment is reserved pending consideration of standards currently being developed."
  The reverse rule in paragraph (a)(9)(ii) nonetheless names "earthmoving or compacting equipment."

## The reverse rule for jobsite vehicles

29 CFR 1926.601(b)(4) states: "No employer shall use any motor vehicle equipment having an obstructed view to the rear
unless:

(i) The vehicle has a reverse signal alarm audible above the surrounding noise level or:

(ii) The vehicle is backed up only when an observer signals that it is safe to do so."

## The reverse rule for earthmoving and compacting equipment

29 CFR 1926.602(a)(9)(ii) states: "No employer shall permit earthmoving or compacting equipment which has an
obstructed view to the rear to be used in reverse gear unless the equipment has in operation a reverse signal alarm
distinguishable from the surrounding noise level or an employee signals that it is safe to do so."

## So is an alarm always required?

Not under these two paragraphs. Both share the same structure:

1. **The trigger is an obstructed view to the rear.** The paragraphs apply to vehicles and equipment "having an
   obstructed view to the rear" (1926.601) or "which has an obstructed view to the rear" (1926.602), and in
   1926.602 to equipment "used in reverse gear."
2. **Either of two options satisfies the rule.** A reverse signal alarm (one the vehicle "has" in 1926.601; one
   "in operation" in 1926.602), or a person's signal: the vehicle "is backed up only when an observer signals that
   it is safe" (1926.601), or "an employee signals that it is safe" (1926.602).
3. **The alarm must stand out from the surrounding noise.** It must be "audible above the surrounding noise level"
   (1926.601) or "distinguishable from the surrounding noise level" (1926.602).

Neither paragraph defines "obstructed view to the rear," and neither sets a decibel level.

## Horns are a separate requirement

Apart from the reverse rule, both sections require a horn or warning device:

- Jobsite vehicles: "All vehicles shall be equipped with an adequate audible warning device at the operator's
  station and in an operable condition" (1926.601(b)(3)).
- Bidirectional machines: "All bidirectional machines, such as rollers, compacters, front-end loaders, bulldozers,
  and similar equipment, shall be equipped with a horn, distinguishable from the surrounding noise level, which shall
  be operated as needed when the machine is moving in either direction. The horn shall be maintained in an operative
  condition" (1926.602(a)(9)(i)).

## Checking the vehicle each shift

For jobsite motor vehicles, 1926.601(b)(14) requires that "All vehicles in use shall be checked at the beginning of
each shift." The list of parts to check includes service brakes, the parking system, the emergency stopping system,
tires, "horn," steering, coupling devices, seat belts, operating controls and "safety devices," and "All defects shall
be corrected before the vehicle is placed in service." It adds: "These requirements also apply to equipment such as
lights, reflectors, windshield wipers, defrosters, fire extinguishers, etc., where such equipment is necessary."

## Quick summary

| Equipment                                                                                          | Rule               | When it applies                                   | What satisfies it                                                                       |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Motor vehicles on an off-highway jobsite not open to public traffic (not equipment under 1926.602) | 1926.601(b)(4)     | Obstructed view to the rear                       | Alarm audible above the surrounding noise, or backing only on an observer's signal      |
| Earthmoving or compacting equipment                                                                | 1926.602(a)(9)(ii) | Obstructed view to the rear, used in reverse gear | Alarm in operation, distinguishable from the surrounding noise, or an employee's signal |
