This explainer covers **Connecticut only**, where electrical licenses are issued by the Department of Consumer
Protection (DCP). It answers one practical question: how do you qualify for Connecticut's E-2 unlimited electrical
journeyperson license, and what continuing education keeps it current? It draws on four DCP pages, section 20-334a of
the Connecticut General Statutes (2025 edition, published by the Connecticut General Assembly) and section 6 of Public
Act 25-111, all read on 2026-11-26. This is an explainer; it does not report a new rule change, and nothing here applies
to any other state.

## What does the E-2 license allow?

DCP's "Electrical License Types and Scope of Work" page lists the license as "E-2 UNLIMITED ELECTRICAL JOURNEYPERSON".
It says "The holder of this license shall be permitted to do all electrical work as defined in section 20-330 of the
Connecticut General Statutes" and "only while in the employment of a properly licensed contractor."

DCP's consumer page explains the numbering: "Licenses with even numbers are journeymen/employees, such as: E-2, L-2,
L-6." It adds: "Only contractors can obtain permits and sign contracts," and "Journeymen and apprentices cannot work
directly for consumers, unless employed by, or employing a licensed contractor of record."

## What are the two routes to the exam?

The scope page states: "The requirement to qualify for this license exam shall be the completion of a registered
apprenticeship program" "or at least four (4) years of equivalent experience and training."

The statute describes the apprenticeship route. Section 20-334a(a)(2)(A) says "An unlimited journeyman's license may be
issued to any person who has completed a bona fide apprenticeship program," "including not less than four years'
experience in the trade for which such person seeks a license," and who has shown competency "by successfully completing
the applicable state licensure examination."

The equivalent route is in section 20-333(b) as amended by section 6 of Public Act 25-111. The department must allow an
applicant who has not participated in a registered apprenticeship program (as set forth in section 31-22r) but either presents a recommendation for review
under section 31-22u "or demonstrates to the department, in consultation with the applicable board," "equivalent
experience and training, to sit for any such examination."

## How many hours does the equivalent route need?

DCP's "Electrical Journeyperson - Equivalent Experience and Training" page says applicants "must be able to prove that
you completed the number of classroom hours AND on the job training hours" for the license type. It gives the E-2 case
directly: "If you are applying for the E-2 license, proof of 720 classroom hours and 8,000 hours of the job training is
required." Its chart lists E-2 at "8,000" hours of "Apprentice On-the-Job Training (OJT)" and "720" "Classroom hours".

The page is strict about the classroom part: "Classroom hours MUST be equivalent to classes listed in Exhibit A."
"Classroom hours are required." "On the job training and work experience will not be considered in lieu of the
classroom requirement." It also states that "Continuing education certificates and manufacturer certificates do not
meet these criteria and will not be considered."

For proof, the page asks for "Transcripts, degrees, and diplomas" and "Notarized letters from current or previous
employers," and it warns: "Payroll records, W-2's, or tax returns will not be accepted." and "Letters MUST be notarized.
Letters that are not notarized will not be accepted."

## What does the statute say about the exam?

Under section 20-333(b) as amended by Public Act 25-111, a person who completes the required apprentice training program
for a journeyman's license under section 20-334a must, "not later than thirty days after completing such program, apply
for a licensure examination given by the department or a person authorized by the department to give such examination." When applying for an initial license, the applicant submits
evidence of passing the final licensure exam, "which successful completion shall occur not more than two years prior to
the date of the relevant licensure application," "unless the appropriate board grants a hardship extension of such
two-year period."

## What continuing education is required?

DCP's "Electrician - Continuing Education" page states: "4 credit hours per year." and "All continuing education (CE)
hours must be completed by June 30 of each year." It adds: "This CE completion deadline is 3 months before the renewal
deadline." and "CE is not required to be completed before the first renewal." "Courses must be approved by the CT
Electrical Board."

"All credential holders must attest to the completion of their required continuing education (CE)" "as part of the
renewal process," and proof "should be retained by the credential holder for at least four (4) years" in case DCP
audits. A hardship exception may be considered "Upon appropriate showing of a bona fide health or other individual
hardship," but "A loss of income resulting from cancellation of a license is not a bona fide hardship."

## Where to check before you act

These are the requirements as shown on DCP's pages and in the statute and public act on the date above. DCP and the
CT Electrical Board (as DCP's CE page names it) are the authority, so confirm current forms, exam scheduling, renewal dates,
fees and approved courses with them before you apply. These rules describe Connecticut only; other states set their
own. For federal electrical safety rules that apply on the job, see our
[OSHA general industry electrical study guide](/osha-general-industry/electrical/study-guide/), and for other electrician
topics, our [electrician page](/electrician/).
