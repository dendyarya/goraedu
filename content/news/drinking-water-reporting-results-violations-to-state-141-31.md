EPA's general reporting rule for public water systems is 40 CFR 141.31, "Reporting requirements." This explainer asks
one question: once a required sample has been analyzed, or something has gone wrong, how soon must the water system
tell the State? It describes the regulation as published in the eCFR on the date checked (November 21, 2026); nothing
here describes a rule change.

## Who reports, and to whom?

Section 141.31 places the duty on the "supplier of water," which 40 CFR 141.2 defines as "any person who owns or
operates a public water system." Reports go to the "State," defined in 141.2 as "the agency of the State or Tribal
government which has jurisdiction over public water systems." When a State or Tribal government does not have primary
enforcement responsibility, 141.2 says "the term 'State' means the Regional Administrator, U.S. Environmental
Protection Agency."

## How soon are routine test results due?

Section 141.31(a) sets the default deadline: "Except where a shorter period is specified in this part, the supplier of
water shall report to the State the results of any test measurement or analysis required by this part within (1) The
first ten days following the month in which the result is received, or (2) the first ten days following the end of
the required monitoring period as stipulated by the State, whichever of these is shortest."

In plain terms, there are two possible ten-day windows, and the shortest one, the one that ends first, applies.

## What if the State's own lab ran the test?

Section 141.31(c) states: "The supplier of water is not required to report analytical results to the State in cases
where a State laboratory performs the analysis and reports the results to the State office which would normally receive
such notification from the supplier."

## How soon must a violation be reported?

Section 141.31(b) states: "Except where a different reporting period is specified in this part, the supplier of water
must report to the State within 48 hours the failure to comply with any national primary drinking water regulation
(including failure to comply with monitoring requirements) set forth in this part."

## Which sections set a different period?

The coliform rule is one example. Under 40 CFR 141.861(a)(1)(i), "A system must notify the State by the end of the day
when the system learns of an E. coli MCL violation," with a next-business-day allowance only when the State office is
closed and has no after-hours line or alternative procedure. Under 141.861(a)(4), a system "that has failed to comply
with a coliform monitoring requirement must report the monitoring violation to the State within 10 days after the
system discovers the violation."

## What must be sent after public notice is given?

Section 141.31(d)(1) states that the system, "within 10 days of completing the public notification requirements under
subpart Q of this part for the initial public notice and any repeat notices, must submit to the primary agency a
certification that it has fully complied with the public notification regulations under subpart Q." The system "must
include with this certification a representative copy of each type of notice distributed, published, posted, and made
available to the persons served by the system and to the media."

Under 40 CFR 141.33(e), those certifications "must be kept for three years after issuance."

Section 141.31(d)(2) adds a lead-specific step: "For Tier 1 notices for a lead action level exceedance, public water
systems must provide a copy of the Tier 1 notice to the Administrator and the head of the primacy agency as soon as
practicable, but not later than 24 hours after the public water system learns of the exceedance." Note the timing set
in 40 CFR 141.80(a)(4)(i): between October 30, 2024, and November 1, 2027, community and non-transient non-community
systems comply with 141.31(d) "as codified on July 1, 2020," and also with "40 CFR 141.31(d)(2), as codified on July
1, 2024," along with other listed provisions. That July 1, 2024 text reads slightly differently: systems "must provide
a copy of any Tier 1 notice" no later than 24 hours "after the public water system learns of the violation or
exceedance." The quotes above are the current eCFR wording; the July 1, 2020 version of 141.31(d) covers the
certification step with the same 10-day period.

## Can the State ask for records at other times?

Yes. Section 141.31(e) states that the system "shall submit to the State within the time stated in the request copies
of any records required to be maintained under § 141.33" or "copies of any documents then in existence which the State
or the Administrator is entitled to inspect" under section 1445 of the Safe Drinking Water Act or equivalent State law.
