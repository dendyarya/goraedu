Under the Ground Water Rule (Subpart S of 40 CFR Part 141), 141.400(c) puts a ground water system on one of two
monitoring paths for viruses. A system that does not treat all of its ground water to at least 4-log treatment of
viruses before or at the first customer is subject to the source water monitoring requirements of 141.402 (our
[earlier explainer](/news/ground-water-rule-triggered-source-water-monitoring-141-402/)
covers the triggered monitoring path). A system that does provide 4-log treatment is instead covered by the compliance monitoring rules in
141.403(b). This explainer answers the operator's question on that second path: what has to be monitored, and how
often? It reads 141.400, 141.403(b) to (d) and 141.404(c) as they stand in the eCFR (text current to November 23,
2026). It is an explainer, not a rule change.

## Which systems does this apply to?

Subpart S applies to "all public water systems that use ground water," except systems that "combine all of their ground
water with surface water or with ground water under the direct influence of surface water prior to treatment under
subpart H" (141.400(b)). 141.400(c)(4) states that ground water systems that provide at least 4-log treatment of viruses
"before or at the first customer are required to conduct compliance monitoring to demonstrate treatment effectiveness,
as described in § 141.403(b)."

In 141.400(c)(2), 4-log is spelled out as "99.99 percent (4-log) treatment of viruses (using inactivation, removal, or
a State-approved combination of 4-log virus inactivation and removal)."

141.403(b)(3) applies the monitoring to a system subject to "paragraphs (a), (b)(1) or (b)(2) of this section," so it
applies to systems subject to the treatment technique requirements in 141.403(a), such as a system that provides 4-log
treatment as its corrective action under 141.403(a)(6)(iv).

## What does a system have to tell the State first?

- **Existing sources, with 4-log treatment in place before December 1, 2009** (141.403(b)(1)): the system "must notify the State in writing that it
  provides at least 4-log treatment of viruses" for the specified source and begin compliance monitoring "by December
  1, 2009."
- **New sources placed in service after November 30, 2009 that rely on 4-log treatment instead of source water
  monitoring** (141.403(b)(2)): the system must notify the State in writing
  and "conduct compliance monitoring as required under § 141.403(b)(3) of this subpart within 30 days of placing the
  source in service."

In both cases the notice "must include engineering, operational, or other information that the State requests to
evaluate the submission."

## How often must the disinfectant residual be checked?

For chemical disinfection, the answer depends on the population served (141.403(b)(3)(i)). Both sizes use the
analytical methods in 141.74(a)(2), at a location approved by the State, and both "must maintain the State-determined
residual disinfectant concentration every day" water from the source is served to the public.

**More than 3,300 people.** The system "must continuously monitor the residual disinfectant concentration" and "must
record the lowest residual disinfectant concentration each day that water from the ground water source is served to the
public." If the continuous equipment fails, the system "must conduct grab sampling every four hours until the
continuous monitoring equipment is returned to service," and it "must resume continuous residual disinfectant
monitoring within 14 days."

**3,300 or fewer people.** The system "must take a daily grab sample during the hour of peak flow or at another time
specified by the State" and record the residual each day water from the source is served. "If any daily grab sample
measurement falls below the State-determined residual disinfectant concentration, the ground water system must take
follow-up samples every four hours until the residual disinfectant concentration is restored to the State-determined
level." A small system may also choose to monitor continuously and meet the larger-system rules instead.

141.403(b)(3) does not set a single residual number: the level to maintain is the "State-determined" one.

## What if the system uses membrane filtration or another treatment?

- **Membrane filtration** (141.403(b)(3)(ii)): the system monitors and operates the membrane process under all
  State-specified requirements. It meets the 4-log virus removal requirement when the membrane's absolute molecular
  weight cut-off (MWCO) (or an alternate parameter) "can reliably achieve at least 4-log removal of viruses," the process is
  operated under State-specified compliance requirements, and "The integrity of the membrane is intact."
- **State-approved alternative treatment** (141.403(b)(3)(iii)): the system monitors and operates it under "all
  compliance requirements that the State determines to be necessary to achieve at least 4-log treatment of viruses."

## What happens if treatment drops below 4-log?

141.404(c) states that a system subject to 141.403(b)(3) "that fails to maintain at least 4-log treatment of viruses ...
before or at the first customer for a ground water source is in violation of the treatment technique requirement if the
failure is not corrected within four hours of determining the system is not maintaining at least 4-log treatment of
viruses before or at the first customer." Under 141.404(d), that violation requires public notification under 141.203
(Tier 2).

Missing the monitoring itself is a separate matter: 141.403(d) states that failure to meet the monitoring requirements
of paragraph (b) "is a monitoring violation and requires the ground water system to provide public notification under §
141.204" (Tier 3).

## Can a system stop 4-log treatment?

Only with the State's written decision. Under 141.403(c), a system may discontinue 4-log treatment for a source "if the
State determines and documents in writing that 4-log treatment of viruses is no longer necessary for that ground water
source." A system that discontinues it is then subject to the source water monitoring and analytical methods
requirements of 141.402. Paragraphs (b)(1) and (b)(2)(iii) say the same: once 4-log treatment stops, source monitoring
under 141.402 applies.

## What this means for operator exam study

The points to know from 141.403(b) are: written notice to the State; monitoring within 30 days of placing a new source
in service; continuous residual monitoring above 3,300 people, with the lowest value recorded daily, grab samples every
four hours if the equipment fails and continuous monitoring back within 14 days; a daily grab sample during the hour of peak flow (or at another time the State specifies) for
3,300 or fewer, with follow-up samples every four hours after a low reading; and the four-hour correction window in
141.404(c). The rule does not mention any certification exam.
