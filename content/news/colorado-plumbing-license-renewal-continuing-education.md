Plumbing licensing is set by each state. This explainer covers **Colorado only**, where
plumbing licenses are issued by the State Plumbing Board through the Division of Professions and Occupations (DPO). It
answers one practical question from the board's own pages: when does a Colorado plumbing license expire, and how much
continuing education (CE) do you need to renew it? It explains the rules as published on the date checked; it does not
describe a rule change, and nothing here applies to any other state.

## When do Colorado plumbing licenses expire?

All at the same time, on a fixed date. Under "License Renewal Information" on its Applications and Forms page, the
board states: "All Plumbing Licenses, Water Conditioning Installer/Principal Registrations, Plumbing Contractor
Registrations and Water Conditioning Contractor Registrations expire on the final day of February of odd-numbered years
and must be renewed to continue practicing."

The same page says renewal is done online through DPO's Online Services system, where you can view your own expiration
date, and that "renewals open approximately 4-5 weeks prior to the expiration date." The page also lists online options
to reinstate an expired license and forms to change a plumbing license to inactive status or reactivate it. Check the
board's forms for any fee that applies.

## Who has to complete continuing education?

Licensed plumbers at three levels. The board's Continuing Education page states: "On or after May 1, 2021, all licensed
Residential, Journeyworker, and Master Plumbers must complete Continuing Education (CE) in order to renew a license."

There is one exception, for the first renewal of a license issued after passing the licensing exam: "This requirement does not apply to the first renewal or
reinstatement of a license for which, as a condition of issuance, the applicant successfully completed a licensing
examination."

## How many hours, and when?

Eight hours a year, counted by license year. The CE page states: "At least eight (8) hours of CE must be completed
during EACH year (March 1 through February 28) of the two-year license period." It adds that "CE hour completion
requirements are based on licensure year; NOT calendar year."

So the hours are tracked from March 1 to February 28, the same cycle that ends when licenses expire at the end of
February. Two years of eight hours each is 16 hours per two-year license period (our calculation from the board's
figures).

## Do some hours have to be about the code?

Yes. The CE page states that "within every twenty-four month period, a minimum of four (4) CE hours must be related to
codes as defined in Board Rule." It also says: "All four (4) code hours may be completed in a single year or divided
between the two (2) years of licensure."

Safety courses count, but only up to a limit: "No more than two (2) hours will be accepted in CE courses related to
safety training."

The board adds a separate step at renewal: "Licensees must attest upon renewal that they have read the current Colorado
Plumbing Code."

## What topics count?

The CE page says the hours "must be selected from among the Core Competencies below in any combination". Its list
includes, among others, "Cross Connection and Backflow Prevention", "Sanitary Drainage", "Storm Drainage", "Vents - DVM",
"Water Heaters", "Water Supply and Distribution", "Special Piping (Medical Gas)" and "Safety Related to the Plumbing
Industry". Read the CE page for the full list of 16.

## What if you hold more than one plumbing license?

You meet the CE rules once, for your highest license. The board states: "Licensees holding multiple plumbing licenses
issued by the Board must complete CE requirements for the most advanced license held." and "Completion of CE
requirements for the most advanced license shall satisfy the requirements for all lesser licenses."

## Where can you take CE, and what records do you keep?

Only from providers on the board's list. The CE page states: "ALL CE COURSES must be taken through a CE vendor or
provider that has attested to the Board that their courses and instructors meet the continuing education requirements
of Board Rule." It also says "Licensees are responsible for ensuring the CE providers they choose meet the CE
requirements in Board Rule."

Keep your CE records. The board states: "Documentation must be retained for a minimum of six years." and "The Board
may conduct audits of continuing education for verification of compliance with these requirements at any time."

## Where to check before you act

These are the State Plumbing Board's requirements as shown on its pages on the date above. The board's pages and its
Board Rules are the authority, and requirements can change, so confirm the current rules with the board before you
renew or sign up for a course. For more on this topic, see [plumbing](/plumbing/) and
[all certifications](/certifications/).
