OSHA's construction standards cover work with explosives in Subpart U of 29 CFR Part 1926, "Blasting and the Use of Explosives". This explainer answers one practical
question: under those rules, who is allowed to act as the blaster? The answer is in 29 CFR 1926.901, "Blaster
qualifications", read in the eCFR (text up to date as of November 25, 2026), with the subpart's definition and general
provisions. It is an explainer, not a rule change.

## Who is "the blaster" in Subpart U?

Section 1926.914 defines the term: "Blaster—The person or persons authorized to use explosives for blasting purposes
and meeting the qualifications contained in § 1926.901." So the definition has two parts: the person is authorized, and
the person meets the qualifications.

The employer's side is in 1926.900(a): "The employer shall permit only authorized and qualified persons to handle and
use explosives."

## What qualifications does 1926.901 list?

The section has five paragraphs:

- **(a) Orders.** "A blaster shall be able to understand and give written and oral orders."
- **(b) Fitness.** "A blaster shall be in good physical condition and not be addicted to narcotics, intoxicants, or
  similar types of drugs."
- **(c) Training, knowledge or experience, and the law.** "A blaster shall be qualified, by reason of training,
  knowledge, or experience, in the field of transporting, storing, handling, and use of explosives, and have a working
  knowledge of State and local laws and regulations which pertain to explosives."
- **(d) Evidence of competency.** "Blasters shall be required to furnish satisfactory evidence of competency in handling
  explosives and performing in a safe manner the type of blasting that will be required."
- **(e) Each method.** "The blaster shall be knowledgeable and competent in the use of each type of blasting method
  used."

Paragraph (c) uses "or": training, knowledge or experience. Paragraph (d) asks for "satisfactory evidence" but does not
say what form that evidence takes, and the section does not describe an OSHA-issued blaster license, card or exam.
Paragraph (c) also points to State and local laws on explosives, which 1926.901 does not list, so those have to be
checked where the work is done.

Separately from the qualifications, 1926.900(c) states: "No person shall be allowed to handle or use explosives while
under the influence of intoxicating liquors, narcotics, or other dangerous drugs."

## What does the blaster have to do on the job?

Later sections of Subpart U give the blaster specific duties. A few examples:

- **Timing and warning.** Under 1926.909(d), "It shall be the duty of the blaster to fix the time of blasting." Under
  1926.909(b), "Before a blast is fired, a loud warning signal shall be given by the blaster in charge, who has made
  certain that all surplus explosives are in a safe place and all employees, vehicles, and equipment are at a safe
  distance, or under sufficient cover."
- **Underground blasts.** In 1926.909(e), which covers underground blasts, "The blaster shall make sure that all employees are out of the blast area
  before firing a blast."
- **Inspection after the blast.** Under 1926.910(b), "An inspection of the area and the surrounding rubble shall be made
  by the blaster to determine if all charges have been exploded before employees are allowed to return to the
  operation, and in tunnels, after the muck pile has been wetted down."
- **Misfires.** Under 1926.911(a), "If a misfire is found, the blaster shall provide proper safeguards for excluding all
  employees from the danger zone."
- **Congested areas.** Under 1926.900(h), when blasting is done "in congested areas or in proximity to a structure,
  railway, or highway, or any other installation that may be damaged, the blaster shall take special precautions in the
  loading, delaying, initiation, and confinement of each blast with mats or other methods so as to control the throw of
  fragments, and thus prevent bodily injury to employees."

## Where to check before you act

These are the requirements as shown in the eCFR on the date checked. OSHA is the authority, and Subpart U also covers
transportation, storage, loading, electric blasting, safety fuse, detonating cord and underwater blasting, which this
post does not explain, so read the full subpart, and the State and local explosives laws that 1926.901(c) refers to,
before relying on it.
