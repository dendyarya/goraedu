The Senior Certified Welding Inspector (SCWI) credential is run by the American Welding Society (AWS). AWS is a private
certifying body, not a government agency, and its certifications are not government licenses. This explainer uses only
AWS's own SCWI program page for facts about its own program. It answers one practical question for welding
certification: what does AWS ask of someone who wants to move from Certified Welding Inspector (CWI) to SCWI? Nothing
here describes a rule change. Whether an employer, code or contract requires an SCWI is outside this post.

## What does an SCWI do, according to AWS?

AWS's page states: "A Senior Certified Welding Inspector (SCWI) is responsible for performing inspections, managing
Certified Welding Inspectors (CWI) and Certified Associate Welding Inspectors (CAWI)". The same sentence goes on to list devising inspection procedures, interpreting joining
methods, auditing suppliers and service providers, and making sure work and records comply with standards and contract
obligations.

## Is there still a single SCWI exam?

No. AWS's page states: "As of April 1, 2025, the path to earning your Senior Certified Welding Inspector (SCWI)
credential has been updated". It adds: "The single SCWI exam has been retired, and the new pathway is now in effect."

In its place, the page states: "Instead of a single exam, candidates are now required to achieve four specific CWI
endorsements". The requirements section names them as:

- "Welder Performance Qualifier"
- "Welding Procedures Qualifier"
- "Welding Coordination and QA"
- "Nondestructive Examination Coordination"

Other parts of the page use slightly longer names for some of these, such as "Welding Coordination and Quality
Assurance". On order, the page states: "You can take these endorsements in any order". It describes the endorsement
exams as computer-based, at Prometric test centers, offered year-round. The page does not state a limit on attempts or
a deadline for finishing all four.

## What experience does AWS ask for?

Two things, both about experience. First, the page states: "You must have held a CWI certification for at least six of
the past eight years" and asks that you "have relevant professional experience during that time." So the SCWI builds on
the CWI; it is not a first credential.

Second, the page asks for "a minimum of 15 years work experience in at least three of the following areas":

- "Procedure development"
- "Control and application of base metals"
- "Experience as a welder or welding operator"
- "Detection and measurement of weld discontinuities"
- "Planning and application of repair welding"
- "Preparation of written procedures"
- "Qualification of welders or welding procedures"
- "Application of welding related codes and standards"
- "Operational techniques fulfilling quality control requirements"
- "Teaching welding or related subjects."

## Is there an education requirement?

The requirements section states: "There are no education requirements as these are implied requirements of the previous
certification (CWI)." The page's FAQ, answering "What industry experience and educational requirements do I need to
qualify for the SCWI?", describes the experience as "typically at least 15 years, with education potentially
substituting for part of that time." The page does not explain how those two statements fit together, so ask AWS how
it will count your education before you apply.

## What documents does the application need?

The page states: "Make sure to have the following documents ready before starting the application process:" and lists
an "Employment Verification Form," a "Qualifying Work Experience Form," a "Photo ID," and a "VAR Form".

## How long does an SCWI last?

The page states: "The SCWI Certification lasts 9 years, with renewals required at years 3 and 6." For those renewals it
states: "Renewal requires continuous employment in the relevant activities" with "documented work experience stated in
the requirements section."

At nine years, the page states: "There are several options available to SCWIs wishing to achieve recertification after
nine years." The options it lists are a committee-approved endorsement taken "during the previous nine years of
certification", "sufficient professional development hours (PDHs)" in that period, "the 9-year AWS Recertification
Seminar prior to the certification expiration date", or "achieving a previous Certified Radiographic Certification."

For people who already hold the credential, the page states: "Current SCWIs will continue in their certification status
and may recertify in accordance with AWS QC1."
