40 CFR Part 141 includes a special monitoring requirement for sodium. The rule is 40 CFR 141.41, "Special monitoring for sodium," in Subpart E (Special
Regulations, Including Monitoring) of Part 141. This explainer answers one practical question: which systems must sample for
sodium, how often, and who must be told the results? It reads the section in the eCFR (text current to November 24,
2026). It is an explainer, not a rule change.

## Which systems must sample?

141.41(a) applies to "Suppliers of water for community public water systems." Paragraph (a) names only community public water
systems; it does not mention other kinds of public water systems.

## Where and how often?

The system "shall collect and analyze one sample per plant at the entry point of the distribution system for the
determination of sodium concentration levels" (141.41(a)). The frequency depends on the source:

- **Surface water, in whole or in part:** samples "must be collected and analyzed annually."
- **Ground water only:** "at least every three years."

The minimum number of samples "shall be based on the number of treatment plants used by the system, except that
multiple wells drawing raw water from a single aquifer may, with the State approval, be considered one treatment plant
for determining the minimum number of samples." The State can ask for more: the supplier "may be required by the State
to collect and analyze water samples for sodium more frequently in locations where the sodium content is variable."

Under 141.41(d), "Analyses for sodium shall be conducted as directed in § 141.23(k)(1)."

## When are results reported, and to whom?

141.41(b) requires the supplier to "report to EPA and/or the State the results of the analyses for sodium within the
first 10 days of the month following the month in which the sample results were received or within the first 10 days
following the end of the required monitoring period as stipulated by the State, whichever of these is first."

If sampling is more than annual, "the supplier shall report the average sodium concentration within 10 days of the month
following the month in which the analytical results of the last sample used for the annual average was received."

Whether EPA gets the results depends on the State: "The supplier of water shall not be required to report the results to
EPA where the State has adopted this regulation and results are reported to the State. The supplier shall report the
results to EPA where the State has not adopted this regulation."

## Who has to be notified?

141.41(c) states: "The supplier of water shall notify appropriate local and State public health officials of the sodium
levels by written notice by direct mail within three months." A copy of each notice "shall be sent to EPA and/or the
State within 10 days of its issuance."

There is one exception: the supplier "is not required to notify appropriate local and State public health officials of
the sodium levels where the State provides such notices in lieu of the supplier."

## Is there a related special monitoring rule?

Yes. The next section, 40 CFR 141.42, "Special monitoring for corrosivity characteristics," has paragraphs (a) through
(c) marked "[Reserved]". Its paragraph (d) states that "Community water supply systems shall identify whether the
following construction materials are present in their distribution system and report to the State." The list covers
lead (from "piping, solder, caulking, interior lining of distribution mains, alloys and home plumbing"), copper,
galvanized piping, "Ferrous piping materials such as cast iron and steel," and "Asbestos cement pipe." States "may
require identification and reporting of other materials of construction present in distribution systems that may contribute contaminants to the drinking water," with vinyl lined asbestos cement pipe and coal tar lined pipes
and tanks given as examples.

## Quick list

- Who: community public water systems.
- Where: one sample per plant, at the entry point to the distribution system.
- How often: yearly if any surface water is used; at least every three years if only ground water; more often if the
  State requires it in locations where the sodium content is variable.
- Report: to EPA and/or the State within the 10-day windows in 141.41(b).
- Notice: written notice by direct mail to local and State public health officials within three months, unless the
  State sends it instead; copy to EPA and/or the State within 10 days.
