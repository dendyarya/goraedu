OSHA's general industry rule for this work is 29 CFR 1910.253, "Oxygen-fuel gas welding and cutting," in Subpart Q.
Paragraph (e) of that section, "Protective equipment, hose, and regulators," sets out what the hose, the hose connections
and the regulators must meet. This explainer answers one practical question: what does 1910.253(e) require for hose and
regulators? It reads the section in the eCFR (text current to November 24, 2026). It is an explainer, not a rule
change, and it does not cover the construction rule in 29 CFR 1926.350.

## What is the general rule for this equipment?

1910.253(e)(1), headed "General," starts with one sentence: "Equipment shall be
installed and used only in the service for which it is approved and as recommended by the manufacturer."

## What standard must the hose meet?

1910.253(e)(5)(i) ties the hose itself to a named consensus standard: "Hose for oxy-fuel gas service shall comply with
the Specification for Rubber Welding Hose, 1958, Compressed Gas Association and Rubber Manufacturers Association, which
is incorporated by reference as specified in § 1910.6." Hose connections under (e)(5)(iii) "shall comply with the
Standard Hose Connection Specifications, 1957, Compressed Gas Association." This post only names those standards; it
does not summarize them.

## How strong must hose connections be, and how are they tested?

1910.253(e)(5)(iv) sets the strength and test requirement: "Hose connections shall be clamped or otherwise securely
fastened in a manner that will withstand, without leakage, twice the pressure to which they are normally subjected in
service, but in no case less than a pressure of 300 psi (2.04 MPa). Oil-free air or an oil-free inert gas shall be used
for the test."

So the strength target is the higher of two figures: twice the normal service pressure, or 300 psi. The test gas must
be oil-free air or an oil-free inert gas.

## Can oxygen and acetylene hose be taped together?

Yes, within a limit. 1910.253(e)(5)(ii) states: "When parallel lengths of oxygen and acetylene hose are taped together
for convenience and to prevent tangling, not more than 4 inches (10.2 cm) out of 12 inches (30.5 cm) shall be covered
by tape."

## What happens to a damaged hose?

1910.253(e)(5)(v): "Hose showing leaks, burns, worn places, or other defects rendering it unfit for service shall be
repaired or replaced." The paragraph gives both options, repair or replacement.

## What does the rule say about pressure-reducing regulators?

1910.253(e)(6) has four requirements:

- Use: "Pressure-reducing regulators shall be used only for the gas and pressures for which they are intended." The
  same paragraph adds that "The regulator inlet connections shall comply with Regulator Connection Standards, 1958,
  Compressed Gas Association." ((e)(6)(i))
- Repair: "When regulators or parts of regulators, including gages, need repair, the work shall be performed by skilled
  mechanics who have been properly instructed." ((e)(6)(ii))
- Marking: "Gages on oxygen regulators shall be marked “USE NO OIL.”" ((e)(6)(iii))
- Inspection before use: "Union nuts and connections on regulators shall be inspected before use to detect faulty seats
  which may cause leakage of gas when the regulators are attached to the cylinder valves." ((e)(6)(iv))

## What else is in paragraph (e)?

The rest of 1910.253(e) deals with service piping systems and portable outlet headers rather than cylinder hose: pressure relief devices
((e)(2)), piping protective equipment against backflow, flashback and excessive back pressure ((e)(3)), and station
outlet equipment ((e)(4)). One example from (e)(4)(vii): "Station outlets shall be equipped with a detachable outlet
seal cap secured in place." Those piping rules are not covered in detail here.
