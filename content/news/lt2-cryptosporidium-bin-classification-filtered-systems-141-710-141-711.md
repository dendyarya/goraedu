Operators at surface water plants hear that their plant is "in Bin 1" or "in Bin 2." The term comes from EPA's rules titled "Enhanced Treatment for Cryptosporidium," in 40 CFR Part 141, Subpart W. This explainer answers what a bin is,
how a filtered system gets one and what each bin requires, from the eCFR text current to November 20, 2026. It is an
explainer, not a rule change, and it covers only the federal text.

## Which systems does Subpart W apply to?

Under 141.700(b), Subpart W applies to "all subpart H systems, which are public water systems supplied by a surface water
source and public water systems supplied by a ground water source under the direct influence of surface water"
(GWUDI). Under 141.700(a), its requirements are "treatment technique requirements in lieu of maximum contaminant levels
for Cryptosporidium," in addition to the filtration and disinfection rules in subparts H, P and T.

Bins apply to **filtered** systems. Under 141.700(c)(3), "All unfiltered systems must provide treatment for
Cryptosporidium as described in § 141.712," which uses a different approach not covered here.

## Where do the bin numbers come from?

They come from source water monitoring under 141.701. In the initial round, for example, filtered systems serving at
least 10,000 people "must sample their source water for Cryptosporidium, E. coli, and turbidity at least monthly for 24
months" (141.701(a)(1)). Filtered systems serving fewer than 10,000 people first monitor E. coli (unless they notify the State that they will monitor for Cryptosporidium instead, 141.701(a)(3)(ii)) and must monitor for Cryptosporidium if they meet one of the conditions in 141.701(a)(4), such as an annual mean E. coli
concentration above 10 E. coli/100 mL for lake or reservoir sources or above 50 E. coli/100 mL for flowing stream
sources.

Under 141.701(d)(1), a filtered system does not have to do source water monitoring at all "if the system will provide a
total of at least 5.5-log of treatment for Cryptosporidium, equivalent to meeting the treatment requirements of Bin 4 in § 141.711,"
after notifying the State in writing as 141.701(d)(3) describes.

## How is the bin concentration calculated?

After the initial round of monitoring, 141.710(a) requires filtered systems to "calculate an initial Cryptosporidium bin
concentration for each plant for which monitoring was required." The method in 141.710(b) depends on how many samples the
system collected:

- **At least 48 samples:** "the bin concentration is equal to the arithmetic mean of all sample concentrations."
- **24 to 47 samples:** it is "the highest arithmetic mean of all sample concentrations in any 12 consecutive months
  during which Cryptosporidium samples were collected."
- **Systems serving fewer than 10,000 people that monitor for one year (24 samples in 12 months):** the arithmetic mean
  of all sample concentrations.
- **Plants operating only part of the year that monitor fewer than 12 months per year (141.701(e)):** "the highest arithmetic mean of all sample concentrations during any year
  of Cryptosporidium monitoring."

If the monthly sampling frequency varies, 141.710(b)(5) says systems first calculate a monthly average for each month and
use those averages instead of individual samples.

## What are the four bins?

The bin table in 141.710(c) for systems required to monitor for Cryptosporidium under 141.701 is:

| Cryptosporidium bin concentration      | Bin   |
| -------------------------------------- | ----- |
| below 0.075 oocysts/L                  | Bin 1 |
| 0.075 to below 1.0 oocysts/L           | Bin 2 |
| 1.0 to below 3.0 oocysts/L             | Bin 3 |
| 3.0 oocysts/L or more                  | Bin 4 |

A system serving fewer than 10,000 people that is "NOT required to monitor for Cryptosporidium under § 141.701(a)(4)"
is placed in Bin 1.

## How much extra treatment does each bin need?

141.711(a) sets the additional Cryptosporidium treatment by bin and by the filtration the system uses "in full
compliance with subparts H, P, and T":

| Bin   | Conventional filtration (including softening) | Direct filtration       | Slow sand or diatomaceous earth filtration |
| ----- | --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Bin 1 | No additional treatment                       | No additional treatment | No additional treatment                    |
| Bin 2 | 1-log treatment                               | 1.5-log treatment       | 1-log treatment                            |
| Bin 3 | 2-log treatment                               | 2.5-log treatment       | 2-log treatment                            |
| Bin 4 | 2.5-log treatment                             | 3-log treatment         | 2.5-log treatment                          |

For alternative filtration technologies, Bin 1 needs no additional treatment; for Bins 2 to 4 the State sets the additional treatment so that total Cryptosporidium removal
and inactivation is at least 4.0-log (Bin 2), 5.0-log (Bin 3) or 5.5-log (Bin 4).

Under 141.711(b)(1), systems meet the additional treatment with options from 141.715, "termed the microbial toolbox."
For Bins 3 and 4, 141.711(b)(2) adds that at least 1-log of the additional treatment must come from one or a combination
of "bag filters, bank filtration, cartridge filters, chlorine dioxide, membranes, ozone, or UV."

## When is the bin reported, and can it change?

Under 141.710(e)(1), the initial bin classification goes to the State for approval "no later than 6 months after the
system is required to complete initial source water monitoring." The report must include "a summary of source water
monitoring data and the calculation procedure used to determine bin classification" (141.710(e)(3)).

The bin is not permanent. After the second round of monitoring under 141.701(b), 141.710(d) requires systems to
recalculate the bin concentration and "redetermine their bin classification." And under 141.711(d), if the State finds
during a sanitary survey or an equivalent source water assessment that, after the system completed its 141.701(a) or (b) monitoring, significant changes in the watershed could lead to increased Cryptosporidium contamination, the system "must take actions specified by the State."

Missing the bin report (141.710(f)) or failing in any month to earn the required treatment credit (141.711(c)) is a
violation of the treatment technique requirement.

## What this means for operator study

The key points: bins apply to filtered subpart H systems; the bin comes from the Cryptosporidium bin concentration
calculated from source water monitoring; the cut points are 0.075, 1.0 and 3.0 oocysts/L; Bin 1 needs no additional
treatment; and the extra log treatment for Bins 2 to 4 depends on the filtration type, with Bins 3 and 4 needing at least
1-log from the specific toolbox options listed.
