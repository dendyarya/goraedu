Next to its solar PV certifications, the North American Board of Certified Energy Practitioners (NABCEP) has a board
certification for battery work: the Energy Storage Installation Professional (ESIP). NABCEP is a private certifying
body, not a government agency. goraedu is independent of it. This explainer answers one practical question from NABCEP's own ESIP page and its Certification
Handbook (the edition marked "2025 Update"): who can take the ESIP exam, and how the certification is kept. Nothing
here describes a rule change.

## Who is the ESIP certification for?

NABCEP's ESIP page says the credential was built by NABCEP with CREATE Energy Center and the Midwest Renewable Energy Association (MREA), with support from a National Science Foundation
grant, "to address the need for a commonly accepted standard of education and training for technicians working with
battery energy storage systems technology." The page also states: "The NABCEP ESIP exam is now available in both
English and Spanish languages!" (The handbook, Section 15.1, still says NABCEP's certification examinations are written and administered in English.)

## What are the basic requirements?

Section 8.1 of the handbook lists, among other items, that the applicant must "Be at least 18 years of age" and "Meet
prerequisites of related experience and education as outlined below." Section 8.3 then sets out what qualifies an
applicant for the exam:

- "Completion of a minimum of OSHA 30 Outreach Training Program for the Construction Industry training"
- "Completion of at least 58 hours of advanced training as defined in Section 8.5.2 below"
- "Completion of installations equaling at least 6 Project Credits"

Note that the safety course is the 30-hour construction course. Section 8.5.1 asks for proof of "30 hours of the
Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) Outreach Training Program for the Construction Industry, or
government equivalent."

## Which installations count?

Section 8.2 says the applicant "shall have performed in a decision-making role (DMR), which had a material impact on the quality and serviceability of the Energy Storage installation." It adds two limits: "Installations must have
been completed within the two (2) calendar years prior to the application submission date" and "All installations
must be complete and have a permit and a final approved inspection."

Project Credits depend on system size. Section 8.3 lists:

- "System size of 1 – 80kWh = 1 Project Credit"
- "System size of 81kWh – 999kWh = 2 Project Credits"
- "System size of 1MWh and up = 3 Project Credits"

## How is each installation documented?

Under Section 8.4, for each completed energy storage (ES) system the applicant must submit documentation "that
summarizes system information." Plans are not a substitute for the real approvals: "NABCEP cannot accept site plans, drawings
and/or permit applications in lieu of the actual approved permits." The handbook also states that "Inspections may not
be completed by the Applicant, Installation Contractor or customer."

## What counts toward the 58 training hours?

Section 8.5.2 states that "Forty (40) hours must have been taken with an accredited institution" and that "Eighteen
(18) hours may be obtained from non-accredited, non-certified sources such as:" courses on "electrical, fire or
building codes relevant to the installation of ES systems," "Additional OSHA or equivalent workplace safety courses
above and beyond the required OSHA 30-hour course," and "Having an active NABCEP PV Associate (PVA) credential."
For people who already hold the PV Installation Professional certification, the ESIP page states: "Current active PV
Installation Professionals (PVIP) now receive 18 non-accredited hours towards the 58 required hours!!" The handbook's
list in Section 8.5.2 names an active PV Associate (PVA) credential but not the PVIP, so check with NABCEP how the PVIP credit is applied before you count on it.

## What is the exam like?

The handbook's ESIP section (Section 8) does not describe the exam itself; it only lists "Pass a written examination" among the requirements. Section 15, which covers NABCEP's certification exams in general and does not name ESIP, states: "Each
certification examination (except System Inspector exams) consists of seventy (70) equally weighted multiple-choice
questions, with four (4) choices per question." It adds: "The time limit for the examination is four (4) hours." ESIP is not listed in the handbook's exam-format table (Section 16), and the ESIP page gives no question count or time limit, so confirm the ESIP exam format with NABCEP before you plan around it.

## How long does ESIP last, and how is it renewed?

Section 2.4 states: "Certification is valid for three (3) years from the date of issuance." Section 8.8 says
"Certificants may submit their Recertification Application during the third (3rd) year." Both the ESIP page and
Section 8.8.1 ask for 30 hours of continuing education; the page breaks them down as "6 hours specific to Electric
(NEC) Codes," "12 hours specific to the ESIP JTA" and "12 hours specific to Renewable Energy of which 2 hours must be
Building and/or Fire Codes."

Section 8.8.2 adds industry activity: "Industry activity must meet one (1) of the minimum requirements," such as a
decision-making role in "at least 3 completed installs within the applicant's three (3) year certification period," or
teaching "at least 120 hours of an advanced ES course(s) or a Registered NABCEP PV Associate course" in that period.
The proof is a signed letter, and "Emails will not be accepted in lieu of a physically signed letter."

## Where federal safety rules fit

NABCEP's certification is separate from federal OSHA electrical rules for employers (29 CFR 1910 Subpart S
in general industry and 29 CFR 1926 Subpart K in construction). Our study guides quote them:
[general industry electrical](/osha-general-industry/electrical/study-guide/) and
[construction electrical](/osha/electrical/study-guide/). goraedu's [solar PV page](/solar-pv/) covers the OSHA
construction safety rules for installation work; it does not cover NABCEP exams.
