For solar PV certification, the North American Board of Certified Energy Practitioners (NABCEP) offers a PV
Commissioning & Maintenance Specialist (PVCMS) board certification. NABCEP is not a government agency: its handbook
calls it "a non-profit credentialing organization" and states that "NABCEP credentials are not professional licenses
issued by a government agency". goraedu is independent of NABCEP. This explainer answers one practical question from
NABCEP's own Certification Handbook (the edition marked "2025 Update") and its Apply Now page: who can take the PVCMS
exam, and how the certification is kept. Nothing here describes a rule change.

## What does the PVCMS certification cover?

Section 7.2 of the handbook states: "The PVCMS Certification combines operations, maintenance and commissioning tasks
into one job specialty."

## Who can apply?

Section 7.1 says the applicant must be "at least 18 years of age," must "Meet prerequisites of related experience
and/or education," must "Sign and agree to uphold the NABCEP Code of Ethics," and must pass a written examination.
The prerequisites in Section 7.3 have three parts.

**Safety training.** "Completion of a minimum of 30 hours of OSHA Outreach Training Program for the Construction
Industry training". Section 7.8.1 also accepts a "government equivalent."

**Advanced training.** "Completion of 24 hours of advanced training as defined in Section 7.8.2". That section requires
"18 hours of advanced PV commissioning and maintenance principles and practices," taken at an accredited institution,
plus 6 hours on the National Electrical Code (NEC). "All training must have been completed in the five (5) calendar
years prior to the submission of the application." "Courses offered by private training organizations or businesses
that are not accredited" are not accepted.

**Experience.** Applicants need "at least 3 Project Credits in a decision-making role related to commissioning or
maintenance," and "12 Project Credits in total must be submitted" (Section 7.2). The work must fall within "the two (2)
calendar years prior to the application submission date," and "All systems must have a minimum rating of 1 kW DC
(STC)." NABCEP's Apply Now page counts 2 project credits for a system of 1 to 999 kW and 3 for a system of 1 MW or more.
Sections 7.4 to 7.7 list the proof, including "a physically signed letter on letterhead from their supervisor/manager."

Section 7.10 describes a separate pathway for veterans, with OSHA 30 training, at least 24 hours of classroom training,
"Completion of commissioning and/or maintenance on solar PV systems for at least Twelve (12) Project Credits," and a
DD-214 with a letter from senior management.

## What is the exam like?

Section 15 states that the exam "consists of seventy (70) equally weighted multiple-choice questions." "Sixty (60) of
the questions are scored and ten (10) of the questions are un-scored pilot questions." "The time limit for the
examination is four (4) hours." Section 17.2 lists the "2017 National Electrical Code, NFPA 70" as provided at the
exam for PVCMS.

A candidate who does not pass may retake the exam "fourteen (14) days after their failed exam" (Section 19.8).

## How long do I have to take the exam?

Section 14.6 says an approved applicant is "eligible to take the exam for a period of One (1) year from the date of
application approval." NABCEP's Apply Now page currently says "you are eligible to take the exam for two years." The
two NABCEP sources do not agree, so check your own approval notice or ask NABCEP.

## How long is PVCMS valid, and how is it renewed?

Section 2.4: "Certification is valid for three (3) years from the date of issuance." Under Section 7.11,
"Certificants may submit their Recertification Application during the third (3rd) year of their current
certification period."

Recertification needs two things:

- **Continuing education:** "30 hours of continuing education" (Section 7.11.1), split into 6 hours on the NEC, 12
  hours on the PVCMS job task analysis and 12 hours on renewable energy, of which 2 hours must cover building and/or
  fire codes. Section 21.1.2 says "A contact hour equals one sixty (60) minute clock hour" and allows passing the
  current exam "As an alternative to meeting CE requirements for any certification", before the current certification
period ends.
- **Industry activity (Section 7.11.2):** a signed employer letter on company letterhead ("Emails will not be accepted
  in lieu of a physically signed letter") showing, for example, that the certificant "had a decision-making role in the
  installation(s) of at least 3 completed installs" in the cycle. The section lists other ways to meet it, such as
  teaching or committee service.

Section 21.1.4 adds that certificants "may renew their certification at any time within one (1) year following their
certification expiration date". They are not certified during that year, and a late fee applies.

This post does not give fee amounts.

## Where federal safety rules fit

The OSHA 30 prerequisite refers to OSHA's construction outreach training. The federal electrical safety rules for
construction work are in 29 CFR 1926 Subpart K, which our
[construction electrical study guide](/osha/electrical/study-guide/) quotes. goraedu's [solar PV page](/solar-pv/)
covers the OSHA construction safety rules for installation work; it does not cover NABCEP exams.
