Employers of CDL drivers keep records of their DOT drug and alcohol testing. Who may see those records, and can the
driver get a copy? FMCSA's testing rule answers this in 49 CFR 382.405,
"Access to facilities and records," with a related limit for medical review officers and third-party administrators in
49 CFR 382.409. This explainer describes both sections as published in the eCFR on the date checked (November 21,
2026); nothing here describes a rule change.

## Who does this apply to?

Section 382.103(a) says Part 382 applies "to service agents and to every person and to all employers of such persons
who operate a commercial motor vehicle in commerce in any State" and are subject to the CDL requirements of Part 383
(or the Mexican or Canadian equivalents named there). Section 382.103(d) lists exceptions, such as employers and
drivers covered only by the Federal Transit Administration's testing rules in Part 655.

## What is the starting rule?

The default is that the records stay closed. Section 382.405(a) says: "Except as required by law or expressly
authorized or required in this section, no employer shall release driver information that is contained in records
required to be maintained under § 382.401."

Section 382.401(a) requires employers to keep those testing-program records "in a secure location with controlled
access," and 382.401(b) sets how long each kind is kept.

## Can a driver get copies of their own records?

Yes. Under 382.405(b), "A driver is entitled, upon written request, to obtain copies of any records pertaining to the
driver's use of alcohol or controlled substances, including any records pertaining to his or her alcohol or controlled
substances tests." The same paragraph says "The employer shall promptly provide the records requested by the driver."

On cost, it adds: "Access to a driver's records shall not be contingent upon payment for records other than those
specifically requested."

## Can a new employer see them?

Only if the driver asks in writing. Section 382.405(f) says: "Records shall be made available to a subsequent employer
upon receipt of a written request from a driver. Disclosure by the subsequent employer is permitted only as expressly
authorized by the terms of the driver's request."

Separately, 382.405(h) says an employer "shall release information regarding a driver's records as directed by the
specific written consent of the driver authorizing release of the information to an identified person." The person who
receives the information may pass it on only within the terms of that consent; the paragraph cross-references 49 CFR
40.321(b) for those terms.

## Who else can see them without the driver's request?

Section 382.405 names several others:

- **DOT and regulators.** Under 382.405(d), each employer, "and each service agent who maintains records for an
  employer," must make available copies of all DOT test results and other program information "when requested by the
  Secretary of Transportation, any DOT agency, or any State or local officials with regulatory authority over the
  employer or any of its drivers."
- **The NTSB after a crash.** Under 382.405(e), when the National Transportation Safety Board asks as part of a crash
  investigation, employers "must disclose information related to the employer's administration of a post-accident
  alcohol and/or a controlled substances test administered following the crash under investigation," and FMCSA "will
  provide access to information in the Clearinghouse concerning drivers who are involved with the crash under
  investigation."
- **Certain proceedings.** Under 382.405(g), an employer "may disclose" a driver's Part 382 information "to the decision
  maker in a lawsuit, grievance, or administrative proceeding initiated by or on behalf of the individual, and arising
  from a positive DOT drug or alcohol test or a refusal to test," with adulterated or substituted results named as
  examples. Covered proceedings include "a worker's compensation, unemployment compensation, or other proceeding
  relating to a benefit sought by the driver." The paragraph also allows disclosure "in criminal or civil actions in
  accordance with § 40.323(a)(2) of this title."

## What about the medical review officer or third-party administrator?

Section 382.409 also covers drug test results kept by a medical review officer (MRO) or a consortium/third-party
administrator (C/TPA). Section 382.409(c) says: "No person may obtain the individual controlled substances test results
retained by a medical review officer (MRO as defined in § 40.3 of this title) or a consortium/third-party administrator
(C/TPA as defined in § 382.107), and no MRO or C/TPA may release the individual controlled substances test results of
any driver to any person, without first obtaining a specific, written authorization from the tested driver."

That paragraph does not stop an MRO or C/TPA from releasing to "the employer, the Clearinghouse, or to the Secretary of
Transportation, any DOT agency, or any State or local officials with regulatory authority over the controlled
substances and alcohol testing program under this part" the information listed in Part 40, subpart G.
