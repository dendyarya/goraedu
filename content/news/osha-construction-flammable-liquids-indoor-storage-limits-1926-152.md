OSHA's construction standard on storing flammable liquids is 29 CFR 1926.152, "Flammable liquids," in Subpart F (Fire Protection and Prevention). This explainer asks one question:
how much flammable liquid can be stored indoors, and in what? It describes the regulation as published in the eCFR on
the date checked (November 21, 2026); nothing here describes a rule change.

## Which liquids does 1926.152 cover?

Section 1926.152(h) states: "This section applies to the handling, storage, and use of flammable liquids with a
flashpoint at or below 199.4 °F (93 °C)." It "does not apply to" "Bulk transportation of flammable liquids" and
"Storage, handling, and use of fuel oil tanks and containers connected with oil burning equipment."

Section 1926.155(h) defines a flammable liquid as "any liquid having a vapor pressure not exceeding 40 pounds per
square inch (absolute) at 100 °F (37.8 °C) and having a flashpoint at or below 199.4 °F (93 °C)" and divides them into
four categories. Category 4, which has a higher cabinet limit below, "shall include liquids having flashpoints above
140 °F (60 °C) and at or below 199.4 °F (93 °C)"; Categories 1 to 3 have lower flashpoints.

## What containers are allowed?

Section 1926.152(a)(1) states: "Only approved containers and portable tanks shall be used for storage and handling of
flammable liquids." For 5 gallons or less, "Approved safety cans or Department of Transportation approved containers
shall be used for the handling and use of flammable liquids," with an exception for "highly viscid (extremely hard to
pour)" materials, which "may be used and handled in original shipping containers." For "quantities of one gallon or
less, the original container may be used, for storage, use and handling of flammable liquids."

Section 1926.155(l) defines a safety can as "an approved closed container, of not more than 5 gallons capacity, having
a flash-arresting screen, spring-closing lid and spout cover and so designed that it will safely relieve internal
pressure when subjected to fire exposure." "Approved," under 1926.155(a), means listed or approved by "a nationally
recognized testing laboratory" or by federal agencies that issue such approvals, such as the U.S. Coast Guard.

## Where can the liquids not be stored?

Section 1926.152(a)(2) states: "Flammable liquids shall not be stored in areas used for exits, stairways, or normally
used for the safe passage of people."
## How much can sit in a room outside a cabinet?

Twenty-five gallons. Section 1926.152(b)(1) states: "No more than 25 gallons of flammable liquids shall be stored in a
room outside of an approved storage cabinet." The same paragraph points to 1926.153 for liquefied petroleum gas, which
is a separate section.

Section 1926.152(b)(2) adds that "Quantities of flammable liquid in excess of 25 gallons shall be stored in an
acceptable or approved cabinet." Under (b)(2)(ii), "Approved metal storage cabinets will be acceptable." Wooden
cabinets are acceptable if built as (b)(2)(i) describes "or equivalent," starting with plywood "at least 1 inch in
thickness." Under (b)(2)(iii), cabinets must be "labeled in conspicuous lettering" with the words Flammable-Keep Away
from Open Flames.

## How much can go in each cabinet, and how many cabinets?

Section 1926.152(b)(3) sets both limits: "Not more than 60 gallons of Category 1, 2 and/or 3 flammable liquids or 120
gallons of Category 4 flammable liquids shall be stored in any one storage cabinet. Not more than three such cabinets
may be located in a single storage area."

## What if there is more than that?

The same paragraph continues: "Quantities in excess of this shall be stored in an inside storage room." Under
1926.152(b)(4), such a room needs, among other things, "approved self-closing fire doors," wiring "approved for Class
I, Division 1, Hazardous Locations," and an exhaust system giving "a complete change of air within the room at least 6
times per hour." Table F-2 in (b)(4)(iv) limits room size and gallons per square foot of floor area, depending on fire
protection and a 1- or 2-hour fire resistance rating.

Beyond what an inside storage room may hold, 1926.152(b)(4)(viii) states: "Flammable liquids in excess of that
permitted in inside storage rooms shall be stored outside of buildings in accordance with paragraph (c) of this
section." Paragraph (c) sets the outdoor rules, such as no more than 1,100 gallons "in any one pile or area" for
containers of not more than 60 gallons each.

## Is a fire extinguisher required at the storage room?

Yes, once the room holds more than 60 gallons. Section 1926.152(d)(1) states: "At least one portable fire extinguisher,
having a rating of not less than 20-B units, shall be located outside of, but not more than 10 feet from, the door
opening into any room used for storage of more than 60 gallons of flammable liquids."
