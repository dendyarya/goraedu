For OSHA construction safety, the federal rule on slings, chains, wire rope, shackles and hooks used to hoist material
is 29 CFR 1926.251 ("Rigging equipment for material handling") in Subpart H (Materials Handling, Storage, Use, and
Disposal) of the construction standards. This explainer reads the section as it stands in the eCFR (current to
November 19, 2026) and answers one question: when must a sling stop being used? Nothing here describes a rule change,
and it does not cover general industry workplaces under Part 1910.

## Which slings does 1926.251 cover?

1926.251(a)(5) sets the scope: "This section applies to slings used in conjunction with other material handling
equipment for the movement of material by hoisting, in employments covered by this part." It names the types: "those
made from alloy steel chain, wire rope, metal mesh, natural or synthetic fiber rope (conventional three strand
construction), and synthetic web (nylon, polyester, and polypropylene)."

So the section is about slings used to hoist material in construction work covered by Part 1926.

## How often must a sling be inspected, and by whom?

Two paragraphs set inspection duties. For rigging equipment in general, 1926.251(a)(1) states that it "shall be
inspected prior to use on each shift and as necessary during its use to ensure that it is safe. Defective rigging
equipment shall be removed from service."

For slings, 1926.251(a)(6) adds who does it: "Each day before being used, the sling and all fastenings and attachments
shall be inspected for damage or defects by a competent person designated by the employer. Additional inspections shall
be performed during sling use, where service conditions warrant. Damaged or defective slings shall be immediately
removed from service."

Alloy steel chain slings also need a periodic check. Under 1926.251(b)(6)(i), "a thorough periodic inspection" is made
"on a regular basis" determined on the basis of (A) frequency of sling use, (B) severity of service conditions, (C) nature of lifts being made, and (D) experience gained on the service life of slings used in similar circumstances, and "Such inspections shall in no
event be at intervals greater than once every 12 months." Under (b)(6)(ii), the employer keeps "a record of the most recent month in which each alloy steel chain sling was thoroughly inspected" and makes it available for examination.

## Can a sling be used if its tag is missing?

No. 1926.251(a)(2) says employers must ensure that rigging equipment has "permanently affixed and legible identification markings as prescribed by the manufacturer that indicate the recommended safe working load" and, under (a)(2)(ii), is not loaded in excess of that recommended safe working load. Paragraph (a)(2)(iii) adds that it must "Not be used without affixed, legible
identification markings, required by paragraph (a)(2)(i) of this section."

## When must a chain sling be removed?

1926.251(b)(5) states: "Whenever wear at any point of any chain link exceeds that shown in Table H-1, the assembly shall
be removed from service." Table H-1 gives a maximum allowable wear for each chain size; for example, 1/2-inch chain is
listed at 7/64 inch and 1-inch chain at 3/16 inch.

## When is wire rope too damaged to use?

1926.251(c)(4)(iv) states: "Wire rope shall not be used if, in any length of eight diameters, the total number of
visible broken wires exceeds 10 percent of the total number of wires, or if the rope shows other signs of excessive wear,
corrosion, or defect."

Heat matters too. Under 1926.251(c)(14), "Fiber core wire rope slings of all grades shall be permanently removed from
service if they are exposed to temperatures in excess of 200 °F (93.33 °C)." For nonfiber core wire rope slings used
above 400 °F or below minus 60 °F, the sling manufacturer's recommendations for that temperature must be followed.

## What damage takes a fiber rope sling out of service?

1926.251(d)(6) says natural and synthetic fiber rope slings "shall be immediately removed from service if any of the
following conditions are present":

- "Abnormal wear."
- "Powdered fiber between strands."
- "Broken or cut fibers."
- "Variations in the size or roundness of strands."
- "Discoloration or rotting."
- "Distortion of hardware in the sling."

## What damage takes a synthetic web sling out of service?

1926.251(e)(8) says synthetic web slings "shall be immediately removed from service if any of the following conditions
are present":

- "Acid or caustic burns;"
- "Melting or charring of any part of the sling surface;"
- "Snags, punctures, tears or cuts;"
- "Broken or worn stitches; or"
- "Distortion of fittings."

## What happens to rigging that is not in use?

1926.251(a)(3) states that rigging equipment "when not in use, shall be removed from the immediate work area so as not
to present a hazard to employees."

## What this means for OSHA study

The points to know from 1926.251 are the daily inspection before use by a competent person designated by the employer,
immediate removal of damaged or defective slings, chain wear limits in Table H-1, the 10 percent broken-wire limit in
any length of eight diameters, and the 200 °F limit for fiber core wire rope slings. The section also covers splices,
sling lengths, end attachments, shackles and hooks, which this post does not summarize.
