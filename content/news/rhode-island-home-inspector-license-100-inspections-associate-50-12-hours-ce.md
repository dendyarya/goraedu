This explainer covers **Rhode Island only**, where home inspectors are licensed by the Contractors' Registration and
Licensing Board (CRLB). It answers one practical question from the Board's own license pages and its rule, 440-RICR-10-00-5:
what does it take to get, and keep, a Rhode Island home inspector or associate home inspector license? Nothing here
describes a rule change, and nothing here applies to any other state.

## Who needs a license?

The Board's license page states: "Anyone who performs paid inspections of residential buildings in Rhode Island must be
licensed." Its FAQ adds: "Effective January 1, 2020, all Home Inspectors/Associate Home Inspectors must be licensed."
The FAQ also says the license covers the home inspection itself; radon and septic work need their own licensing or
certification from the agencies that regulate them. It lists exemptions, among them government employees acting within
their duties, architects, engineers, electrical contractors and master plumbers working within their own practice, and
people inspecting under a licensed home inspector's supervision to meet the requirement for associate licensure.

## What is the difference between the two licenses?

The license page describes the associate license this way: "An Associate Home Inspector is an entry-level license holder
who performs home inspections under the supervision" of a licensed home inspector. The page says it is meant for people
gaining the experience and training needed for full licensure.

## What does a first-time applicant submit?

The Board's apply page lists, for each license:

- **Home inspector:** "Submit proof of completing a PSI exam." and "Provide one hundred (100) home inspections."
- **Associate home inspector:** "Submit proof of completing a PSI exam." and "Provide fifty (50) home inspections."

For both, the page also asks for certificates of general liability and errors and omissions insurance naming the Board
as certificate holder, and workers' compensation coverage only if the applicant has employees. It adds that contractors
"are required to maintain the applicable insurance coverage as a condition of registration and licensing." The page does not say
how the inspections are to be documented, and it states no pre-licensing course hours. Both licenses are issued for two
years, and an incomplete application is rejected after three months.

## How does an associate move up?

Under section 5.7(B) of the rule, an associate may apply for the home inspector license while still licensed as an
associate, if they meet the home inspector requirements. The rule says: "No fee shall be charged." It also says: "The
effective date of the home inspector license shall be the date of issuance and shall expire on the same date as the
previously issued associate home inspector license." Once an associate license has expired, the holder may still apply
by meeting the requirements for licensure for home inspector. The rule does not set its own inspection count for this
step; the apply page's home inspector requirements are the 100 inspections, the PSI exam and the insurance.

## How many continuing education hours are required?

Section 5.8(B)(1) states: "As a condition for renewal of licensure, a home inspector or associate home inspector shall
complete twelve (12) credit hours of continuing education courses during each two (2) year licensing period." Section
5.8(B)(3) adds: "At least one (1) credit hour per renewal cycle shall be dedicated to professional ethics and one (1)
credit hour on standards of practice." The renew page asks for proof of the state-approved 12 hours and current
insurance certificates at each renewal.

## What records must be kept?

Section 5.24.3(C) states: "A written contract and home inspection report is required for all home inspections", and the
original copy of the contract and the report is delivered only to the client, unless the client directs otherwise. The rule
lists what every report must include, such as the licensee's license number, a notice that claims may be directed to the
Board with the Board's address and telephone number, and, where it applies, a disclosure that an associate home inspector
performed the inspection. Section 5.24.11(A) states: "Home inspectors and associate home inspectors shall maintain a copy
of all reports prepared by them, or under their supervision, for a period of not less than three (3) years from the
completion date of the written report."
