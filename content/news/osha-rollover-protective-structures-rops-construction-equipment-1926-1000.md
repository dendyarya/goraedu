A rollover protective structure, or ROPS, is the frame that protects an equipment operator if the machine tips over.
OSHA's construction standards set the ROPS rules in 29 CFR 1926 Subpart W (sections 1926.1000 to 1926.1003). This
explainer answers one practical question from that text: which construction equipment needs a ROPS, and what goes with
it? It describes the regulation as published in the eCFR on the date above; nothing here describes a rule change.

## Which equipment is covered?

Section 1926.1000(a) states that the subpart applies to "All rubber-tired, self-propelled scrapers, rubber-tired
front-end loaders, rubber-tired dozers, wheel-type agricultural and industrial tractors, crawler tractors, crawler-type
loaders, and motor graders, with or without attachments, that are used in construction work."

The same paragraph adds a second group with a date limit: "This subpart also applies to compactors and rubber-tired
skid-steer equipment, with or without attachments, manufactured after July 15, 2019, that are used in construction
work."

## Is any equipment excluded?

Yes. Section 1926.1000(a) ends: "This subpart does not apply to sideboom pipelaying tractors."

Section 1926.602(a), which covers earthmoving equipment, also states at 1926.602(a)(7): "The promulgation of standards
for rollover protective structures for off-highway trucks is reserved pending further study and development."

## Does the manufacture date matter?

Yes, for which test standard the ROPS must meet. Under 1926.1000(b), covered equipment (excluding compactors and
rubber-tired skid-steer equipment) "manufactured before July 15, 2019, shall be equipped with rollover protective
structures that meet the minimum performance standards prescribed in § 1926.1001(b), as applicable." Under 1926.1000(c),
equipment "manufactured on or after July 15, 2019, shall be equipped with rollover protective structures that meet the
minimum performance standards prescribed in § 1926.1001(c)."

Section 1926.1001(b) lists SAE recommended practices for the older equipment, "or comply with the consensus standard
(ISO 3471:2008)". Section 1926.1001(c) requires newer equipment to meet "ISO 3471:2008". For wheel-type agricultural
and industrial tractors, 1926.1002(b) and (c) set the parallel split, with "ISO 5700:2013" or "ISO 3471:2008" for
tractors made on or after July 15, 2019.

## What counts as an agricultural or industrial tractor?

Section 1926.1002(e)(1) defines "Agricultural tractor" as "a wheel-type vehicle of more than 20 engine horsepower, used
in construction work, that is designed to furnish the power to pull, propel, or drive implements." Section 1926.1002(e)(2)
defines "Industrial tractor" as wheel-type tractors "of more than 20 engine horsepower (other than rubber-tired loaders
and dozers described in 29 CFR 1926.1001), used in operations such as landscaping, construction services, loading,
digging, grounds keeping, and highway maintenance."

## Is overhead protection required on tractors?

Section 1926.1003 applies "When overhead protection is provided" on wheel-type agricultural and industrial tractors: it
then must meet the standard for the tractor's manufacture date (SAE J167 or ISO 27850:2013 before July 15, 2019; ISO
27850:2013 on or after). Under 1926.1003(d), where the site clearing rule in 1926.604 also applies, "the overhead
protection may be either the type of protection provided in § 1926.604, or the type of protection provided by this
section."

## What if a ROPS is removed, and how is it labeled?

Section 1926.1000(d) states: "ROPS removed for any reason, shall be remounted with equal quality, or better, bolts or
welding as required for the original mounting." Under 1926.1000(e), each ROPS must have permanently affixed the
"Manufacturer or fabricator's name and address", the "ROPS model number, if any", and the "Machine make, model, or
series number that the structure is designed to fit."

Section 1926.1000(f) also deems a machine in use with ROPS in compliance "if it meets the rollover protective structure
requirements of the State of California, the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, or the Bureau of Reclamation of the U.S.
Department of the Interior in effect on April 5, 1972."

## What about seat belts?

Seat belts are in 1926.602, not Subpart W. Section 1926.602(a)(2)(i) states: "Seat belts shall be provided on all
equipment covered by this section". That section covers "scrapers, loaders, crawler or wheel tractors, bulldozers,
off-highway trucks, graders, agricultural and industrial tractors, and similar equipment" (1926.602(a)(1)). Two
exceptions follow: seat belts "need not be provided for equipment which is designed only for standup operation"
(1926.602(a)(2)(ii)) and "need not be provided for equipment which does not have roll-over protective structure (ROPS)
or adequate canopy protection" (1926.602(a)(2)(iii)).
