The Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations list physical qualifications that a commercial motor vehicle driver must
meet, and two of them concern arms, hands, legs and feet. A driver who does not meet those two, and is otherwise qualified, can still
drive with a Skill Performance Evaluation (SPE) certificate granted by the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA). This
explainer answers one practical question: can a person who has lost, or has an impairment of, a limb drive a commercial
motor vehicle, and how does the SPE certificate work? It reads 49 CFR 391.41 and 391.49 in the eCFR (text current to
November 24, 2026). It is an explainer, not a rule change.

## Which physical standards does the SPE certificate replace?

49 CFR 391.41(b) states when "A person is physically qualified to drive a commercial motor vehicle". Its first two
items are:

- (b)(1): "Has no loss of a foot, a leg, a hand, or an arm, or has been granted a skill performance evaluation
  certificate pursuant to § 391.49;"
- (b)(2): "Has no impairment of: (i) A hand or finger which interferes with prehension or power grasping; or (ii) An
  arm, foot, or leg which interferes with the ability to perform normal tasks associated with operating a commercial
  motor vehicle; or any other significant limb defect or limitation which interferes with the ability to perform normal
  tasks associated with operating a commercial motor vehicle; or has been granted a skill performance evaluation
  certificate pursuant to § 391.49;"

391.49(a) then says that a person "who is not physically qualified to drive under § 391.41(b)(1) or (2) and who is
otherwise qualified to drive a commercial motor vehicle, may drive a commercial motor vehicle if FMCSA has granted a
Skill Performance Evaluation (SPE) Certificate to that person." The certificate covers only those two standards; the
driver must still be "otherwise qualified."

## Who applies, and where?

Under 391.49(b)(1), the letter of application "may be submitted jointly by the person (driver applicant) who seeks an
SPE certificate and by the motor carrier that will employ the driver applicant". A joint application goes to the SPE
Certificate Program at the FMCSA service center for the State where the carrier's principal place of business is
located ((b)(2)). Under the exception in (b)(3), a letter "may be submitted unilaterally by a driver applicant", and it
goes to the field service center for the State where the driver has legal residence. A unilateral applicant meets
all of paragraph (c) except the motor carrier's name and address and its U.S. DOT number ((c)(1)(i) and (iii)), and
answers the operation questions in (c)(2)(i) to (v) "if the information is known."

## What goes with the application?

391.49(c) lists what the letter must contain: identification of the applicants, a description of the limb impairment,
the type of driving operation, the vehicle (including any modifications, with photographs where applicable), a
certification that the driver is otherwise qualified, and signatures. Under 391.49(d), it must be accompanied by,
among other items:

- copies of the Medical Examination Report Form, MCSA-5875, and the Medical Examiner's Certificate, Form MCSA-5876;
- "A medical evaluation summary completed by either a board qualified or board certified physiatrist (doctor of
  physical medicine) or orthopedic surgeon";
- a description of any prosthetic or orthotic device worn;
- a road test (from the co-applicant carrier, or, for a unilateral applicant, one administered "by a motor carrier or a
  person who is competent to administer the test and evaluate its results");
- the employment application under 391.21 (for a unilateral applicant, the application from the last commercial driving
  position held, or a statement that there was none), and the driver's State driving record "for the past 3 years from each State in which a
  motor vehicle driver's license or permit has been obtained."

For a driver applicant with loss of a hand or arm, 391.49(d)(3)(i)(B) adds: "Prior to applying for an SPE certificate,
an applicant with loss of a hand or arm must be fitted and proficient with a proper prosthesis that enables the
applicant to demonstrate precision prehension and power grasp prehension with each hand separately." Paragraph
(d)(3)(ii)(C) sets a parallel rule for upper limb impairment, with a prosthesis or orthotic device where needed. Both requirements exclude an individual who was granted a waiver,
absent the device, prior to January 6, 1986, "the effective date of the requirement."

## How long is the certificate valid?

391.49(h): FMCSA "may deny the application for SPE certificate or may grant it totally or in part" with terms,
conditions and limitations. "The SPE certificate is valid for a period not to exceed 2 years from date of issue, and
may be renewed 30 days prior to the expiration date." The renewal application in 391.49(i) includes total miles driven
and accidents under the current certificate, a current MCSA-5875, and a current State driving record. It goes to the service center for the driver's State of
legal residence for a unilateral certificate, or for the co-applicant carrier's State of principal place of business
for a joint one.

## What must the driver and the motor carrier do?

- The driver "shall supply each employing motor carrier with a copy of the SPE certificate" (391.49(f)) and "shall
  have the SPE certificate (or a legible copy) in his/her possession whenever on duty" (391.49(j)(1)). 391.41(a)(1)(ii) likewise requires a driver qualified through "a skill performance
  evaluation certificate" to have "on his or her person a copy of the variance documentation when on-duty."
- An employing carrier agrees under 391.49(e) to file promptly ("within 30 days of the involved incident") the documents
  and information required about the driver's driving activities, accidents, arrests, license suspensions, revocations
  or withdrawals, and convictions, whether the certificate is unilateral or joint; to road test the driver with the trailer it intends the
  driver to transport (or accept a similar trailer road test, as the paragraph allows); to evaluate the driver for the
  nondriving safety related job tasks tied to the trailer type(s) used and any tasks unique to its operations
  ((e)(3), which the SPE itself does not evaluate); and to use the driver only in
  compliance with the certificate's conditions and limitations.
- The carrier keeps a copy in the driver qualification file, retained "for a period of 3 years after the driver's
  employment is terminated" (391.49(j)(1)).

FMCSA "may revoke an SPE certificate after the person to whom it was issued is given notice of the proposed revocation
and has been allowed a reasonable opportunity to appeal" (391.49(k)).
