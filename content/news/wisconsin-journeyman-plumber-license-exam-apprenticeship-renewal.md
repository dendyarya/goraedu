Plumbing licenses are issued by each state, and this explainer covers **Wisconsin only**, where plumbers are licensed
by the Department of Safety and Professional Services (DSPS). It answers one practical question from DSPS's own
journeyman plumber pages: who can take the Wisconsin **Journeyman Plumber** exam, and what does DSPS say about the exam
itself? Nothing here describes a rule change, and nothing here applies to any other state.

## Who can take the Wisconsin journeyman plumber exam?

DSPS's "Other Application Methods" page lists the qualifications for the exam route. It states: "Applicants who plan to
use the examination pathway for licensure must have completed one of the following:" and then gives two options.

- A plumbing apprenticeship: "A person applying for a Journeyman Plumber license examination shall have completed a
  plumbing apprenticeship program" "recognized under Wisconsin Statute chapter 106 or the Federal Department of
  Labor".
- An out-of-state plumbing apprenticeship, described as a "5-year program, including plumbing related instruction of a
  minimum of 576 hours", recognized under Wisconsin law or by the Federal Department of Labor.

In other words, the exam route on this page starts from a completed apprenticeship. The page does not list a route to the
exam based on work hours alone.

## What paperwork shows I finished my apprenticeship?

For the Wisconsin apprenticeship route, the page asks applicants to "Upload a copy of a letter from the Wisconsin Bureau
of Apprenticeship Standards stating that you have satisfied" the apprenticeship. The page lists separate documents for
each of the other routes, so check the section for your own route before you apply.

## What is the exam like?

DSPS's exam information on the same page states:

- "This is a two part exam"
- "The exam is open book, and the passing score is 70%"

The page lists the reference materials applicants may bring: a binder of Wisconsin statutes, administrative code
chapters and printed notes (the page says it "can be PHCC"), the "NFPA 13D - 2019 Standard - National Fire Protection Association", the
"2019 NFPA 24 of the National Fire Protection Association", five component manuals (for example the "Mound Component Manual") and
the "Multi-Purpose Piping Systems Design Manual". It then warns: "Please note - Pearson and DSPS exam locations will
only allow the nine reference items above into the exam". It also states: "Architect and Engineer Scale Rulers are
permitted".

The page says applicants must take and pass the exam in accordance with Wisconsin Administrative Code 305.09, and that "Exam instructions
will be provided after required qualifications for examination have been met." So exam instructions come only after the
qualifications for the exam are met.

## Are there routes that skip the Wisconsin exam?

The same page lists pathways under the line: "The below pathways do not require applicants to complete the Wisconsin
Journeyman Plumber examination". One is for a person who completed an out-of-state apprenticeship, passed a journeyman
plumber or equivalent exam in another state, "has practiced for at least five years under a journeyman plumber, or
equivalent, license issued by another state", in a state whose plumber licensing rules DSPS determines are "substantially
similar" to Wisconsin's, and has "not been the subject of any disciplinary actions related to that
license or any other equivalent license". The other is for those who "Hold a Wisconsin Master Plumber credential that
can be exchanged for a Journeyman Plumber credential."

The section for that route is worded differently: its summary says it "Requires completing an apprenticeship, passing
the Journeyman Plumber license examination, and practiced for at least five (5) years under a journeyman plumber's
license or equivalent license issued by another state," while its detailed list asks for a person who "passed a
journeyman plumber's examination in any other state." The page does not say plainly whether this route also needs the
Wisconsin exam, so ask DSPS before you apply.

## What does renewal require once I am licensed?

DSPS's main journeyman plumber page states: "License shall be renewed in accordance with SPS 305.07" and "Renewal shall
be contingent upon obtaining at least 24 hours of acceptable continuing education (CE)". It adds that "Continuing
Education must be entered by course provider", and that the online system will not let you submit a renewal "until all
required continuing education (CE) hours have been entered by the provider." The page also states: "You will be able
to renew your credential approximately 4 weeks prior to your license expiring." CE finished after the license expires
brings a late CE fee, according to the same page.

## Where to check before you act

These are the requirements as shown on DSPS's journeyman plumber pages on the date above. DSPS is the authority, so
confirm the current qualifications, documents, exam rules and renewal steps on its site before you apply, book an exam
or plan your continuing education. These rules describe Wisconsin only; other states set their own. For other plumbing
topics on goraedu, see our [plumbing page](/plumbing/).
