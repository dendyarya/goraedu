The robotic arc welding credential (CRAW) is run by the American Welding Society (AWS). AWS is a private certifying
body, not a government agency, and this explainer uses only AWS's own CRAW pages for facts about its own program. It
answers one practical question for welding certification: what is a CRAW, who can take it, what does the test involve,
and how long does it last? Nothing here describes a rule change. Whether an employer requires a CRAW is outside this
post.

## What is a CRAW?

AWS's program page states: "A Certified Robotic Arc Welder (CRAW) is an AWS credential for professionals who work with
robotic arc welding systems." It adds that the credential "verifies that an individual has demonstrated the knowledge
and hands-on ability to set up, operate, program," "and support robotic welding equipment in accordance with industry
standards."

## What is the difference between Operator and Technician?

There are two levels, Operator (CRAW-O) and Technician (CRAW-T). AWS says: "CRAW-T (Technician) is ultimately responsible for the weld quality
in their plant or department." The Technician level also needs a second AWS credential: "CRAW Technicians must hold a
current Certified Welding Inspector (CWI) certification." Without it, the page says, "only the CRAW-O certification can
be attained."

## Who can take it?

AWS lists these requirements, in its words:

- Education (both levels): "High school diploma or military approved high school equivalent (e.g., GED) is required".
- Arc welding (both levels): "A minimum of six months' experience in manual or semiautomatic arc welding processes is
  necessary".
- Welding experience: "12 months of experience as a welder (including robotic and automatic operation) for Operators"
  and "18 months (about 1 and a half years) for Technicians."
- Related work: "Operators require at least three years of welding experience in an occupation related to welding or
  robotics, such as inspection or quality control, while Technicians require five years."
- Operator training: "completion of an original equipment manufacturer or equivalent Robotic Programming Course" "from
  an AWS Approved Testing Center (ATC)."
- Technician training: "Technicians should have training in using quality measuring tools, including software for
  measuring weld cross-sections," "and familiarity with personal computers." AWS adds: "AWS currently does not offer
  this training."

On degrees, AWS says degrees in "welding, technical, robotic, technology, electrical, or engineering disciplines may
substitute" "for up to two years of experience" "but not for the required six months of manual or semiautomatic arc
welding."

## What does the test involve?

AWS states: "Please note that the same two exams are given to CRAW-certified
Operators and Technicians."

- Written exam: "A closed book exam, that lasts two hours and covers subjects listed in AWS QC19." The page lists
  subject weights; the largest is Robot Programming and Logic, at about 20% (AWS labels the weights "Approximate %").
- Practical performance test: a series of tasks "which will include a GMA welding test plate made to a Welding
  Procedure Specification (WPS)." AWS says "There is no body of knowledge for the practical exam," and that its "CRAW
  Performance Test - Candidate Information Booklet provides the tasks that test candidates are expected to complete."

The page adds: "If you fail any part of the CRAW exam, schedule a retest with your ATC
representative."

## Where is the test given?

Through AWS Approved Testing Centers. AWS's testing-center page says such a center "must employ or have access to" a
CRAW-T, who supervises testing and "cannot conduct any training or preparatory activities for those candidates." The
center needs "at least one operating arc welding robot that can be programmed for operation in three axes" and "the
equipment to perform mechanical and macroetch testing of completed weld coupons."

## How long does a CRAW last?

AWS says: "CRAW certification remains valid for three year renewal cycles, with renewal requirements to maintain active
status." Its FAQ places renewals at "three and six years after initial certification," and the page says: "Every nine
years after initial certification, you will need to recertify."

## How is it renewed?

To renew, AWS says "you must demonstrate two years of relevant welding activity" "within your three-year certification
period or retake the exam." Training can count toward this: documented completion or instruction of "a minimum of 40 contact hours" "in training courses related
to welding skills, applications, control, materials, and processes", and "One of the two years can be fulfilled through
this educational option."

## What happens at nine years?

The page gives two options:

- "Retake the current CRAW Operator or Technician performance exam, which is based on the test version in use" "when you
  apply for recertification."
- "Prove that you have been active in related fields like inspection, welding, design, production, construction, or
  repair" "for at least two years within your last three certified years." "Plus, show evidence of participation in
  activities listed in QC19 for at least 80 contact hours." These hours "can be documented as 8 Continuing Education Units
  (CEUs) or 80 Professional Development Hours (PDHs)."
