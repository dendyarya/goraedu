The federal drinking water rules deal with uncovered finished water storage in one short section: 40 CFR 141.714, part of Subpart W, "Enhanced Treatment for Cryptosporidium."
This explainer answers one practical question from the eCFR text (version of November 16, 2026): can a water system keep
using an uncovered finished water storage facility, and if so, on what conditions? Nothing here describes a rule
change.

## What counts as an "uncovered finished water storage facility"?

40 CFR 141.2 defines it: an "Uncovered finished water storage facility is a tank, reservoir, or other facility used to
store water that will undergo no further treatment to reduce microbial pathogens except residual disinfection and is
directly open to the atmosphere."

Two conditions both have to be true. The stored water gets no further pathogen treatment apart from residual
disinfection, and the facility is directly open to the atmosphere. A reservoir that holds raw water before treatment
would not, read with the definition, meet the first condition.

The same section defines "finished water" as "water that is introduced into the distribution system of a public water
system and is intended for distribution and consumption without further treatment, except as treatment necessary to
maintain water quality in the distribution system (e.g., booster disinfection, addition of corrosion control
chemicals)."

## Which systems does the rule apply to?

141.714 sits in Subpart W, and 141.700(b) sets its scope: "The requirements of this subpart apply to all subpart H
systems, which are public water systems supplied by a surface water source and public water systems supplied by a
ground water source under the direct influence of surface water."

So the section is about systems that use surface water or ground water under the direct influence of surface water
(GWUDI). 141.700(c)(4) lists it among the subpart's requirements: "Systems with uncovered finished water storage
facilities must comply with the requirements to cover the facility or treat the discharge from the facility as described
in § 141.714."

## What does 141.714 require?

The section opens with 141.714(a): "Systems using uncovered finished water storage facilities must comply with the
conditions of this section." It then sets two steps, each tied to a date in the text:

- **Notify the State.** 141.714(b): "Systems must notify the State of the use of each uncovered finished water storage
  facility no later than April 1, 2008."
- **Cover or treat.** 141.714(c): "Systems must meet the conditions of paragraph (c)(1) or (2) of this section for each
  uncovered finished water storage facility or be in compliance with a State-approved schedule to meet these conditions
  no later than April 1, 2009."

Both dates are in the regulation as written; they are past deadlines, not upcoming ones.

## What are the two options?

141.714(c) gives a choice, applied to each uncovered facility (or, instead of meeting either option by April 1, 2009, a system could be in compliance with a State-approved schedule to meet them):

1. **Cover it.** 141.714(c)(1): "Systems must cover any uncovered finished water storage facility."
2. **Treat what leaves it.** 141.714(c)(2): "Systems must treat the discharge from the uncovered finished water storage
   facility to the distribution system to achieve inactivation and/or removal of at least 4-log virus, 3-log Giardia
   lamblia, and 2-log Cryptosporidium using a protocol approved by the State."

The treatment option has three conditions in one sentence: it treats the discharge to the distribution system, it
reaches all three log levels (at least 4-log virus, 3-log Giardia lamblia and 2-log Cryptosporidium), and the protocol
is approved by the State.

## What if a system does not comply?

141.714(d) states: "Failure to comply with the requirements of this section is a violation of the treatment technique
requirement."

## Is there a reporting rule too?

Yes. The Subpart W reporting section, 141.721(b), states: "Systems must report the use of uncovered finished water
storage facilities to the State as described in § 141.714."

## What this means for exam study

For drinking water operator study, the points to keep straight are the definition (no further pathogen treatment except
residual disinfection, and directly open to the atmosphere), the scope (subpart H systems), the two options (cover, or
treat the discharge to 4-log virus, 3-log Giardia lamblia and 2-log Cryptosporidium with a State-approved protocol) and
the result of not complying (a treatment technique violation). Who certifies operators and what each exam covers is set
by your State; see our post on [who certifies drinking water operators](/news/who-certifies-drinking-water-operators-epa-or-state/).
