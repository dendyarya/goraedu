This explainer covers **Washington State only**, where home inspectors are licensed by the Washington State Department
of Licensing (DOL), with a home inspector advisory licensing board set up by state law. It answers one practical
question from DOL's own license page, the state law (RCW chapter 18.280) and the department's rules (WAC chapter 308-408A):
what training, field training and exam does Washington ask for before it issues a home inspector license? Nothing here
describes a rule change, and nothing here applies to any other state.

## Who needs a Washington license?

DOL's "Get your license" page answers in one line: "Anyone who examines the conditions of a home for a fee." The law
itself, RCW 18.280.020(1), says a person shall not act "in the capacity of a home inspector within this state
without first obtaining a license as provided in this chapter." RCW 18.280.040(1) states: "The state home inspector
advisory licensing board is created."

## What classroom training comes first?

Step 1 on DOL's page, "Meet the education and training requirements," states: "You must take a Washington State Home
Inspectors Board-approved, 120-hour Fundamentals of Home Inspection course."

The law sets the same floor. RCW 18.280.070(2) asks for "Proof of a minimum of one hundred twenty hours of classroom
instruction approved by the board". WAC 308-408A-020(1) adds that "The candidate must pass a course examination
approved by the director."

## How much field training, and with whom?

DOL's Step 1 continues: "After you take your Fundamentals of Home Inspection course, you must mentor with a home
inspector who has at least 4 years of licensure." It then states: "You must complete 40 hours of field training and 5
actual inspections." Step 2 lists a "Training log showing 40 hours of field training" among the documents to gather.

WAC 308-408A-020(1) words it as "proof of forty hours of field training approved by the department." The statute is
worded differently: RCW 18.280.070(3) asks for "Proof of up to forty hours of field training supervised by a licensed
home inspector". DOL's page and the WAC both state 40 hours; the law says "up to" forty. Ask DOL how it applies the
hours to your application before you plan around either wording.

## Is there a time limit on the training?

Yes. WAC 308-408A-020(1) states: "This course and the required field training must be completed within two years
prior to applying for the home inspector's license examination." The same section says DOL's approval of the exam application "is valid
for six months from the date of the approval letter. If the approval expires, the candidate must apply to be
reapproved."

## Which exam do new inspectors take?

RCW 18.280.080 says applicants "must pass an exam that is psychometrically valid, reliable, and legally defensible by
the state." If a national exam is used, "a section specific to Washington shall be developed by the director and
included as part of the entire exam."

DOL's table lists "New Inspectors" as "Those who have not actively performed home inspections," with "Fundamentals of
Home Inspection (120 clock hours)," "Field training (40 hours)" and the "Full exam." You first apply to take the exam
(Step 3), online with "a SecureAccess Washington account" or by mail, then wait for "An exam approval letter or email
from our testing vendor" (Step 4). On exam day, "You must bring your photo ID to the testing site".

## What if you fail a portion?

DOL's Step 5 states: "You must wait 30 days before you can attempt to retake the exam" and "There are no restrictions
regarding the number of attempts". It adds: "You must pass the applicable portion(s) within your 2-year eligibility
period before applying for a license". When you apply for the license (Step 6), "You must upload a copy of the report
stating you passed the state and national exams."

## What about inspectors licensed in another state?

DOL's table lists "Out-of-state inspectors" as "Those who have been licensed as a home inspector in good standing for at
least 2 of the last 4 years," who need "Documentation of education and training that meets or exceeds Washington's requirements (WAC 308-408A-040)" and take the "State portion of the exam." WAC 308-408A-040(1) sets the condition in full:
the person must have been "licensed and actively practices as a home inspector for two years in the last four years in
another jurisdiction that meets or exceeds the requirements under chapter 18.280 RCW" and kept the license "in good
standing." Section (2) asks for "a license verification form completed by an administrative officer of the licensure
authority in such jurisdiction."

## Where to check before you act

These are the requirements as shown on DOL's license page and in RCW 18.280 and WAC 308-408A on the date above. The
Washington State Department of Licensing is the authority, so confirm the current course list, forms, fees and exam
details with its Home Inspector Program before you enroll, start field training or book the exam. These requirements
describe Washington only; other states set their own. For the federal lead disclosure and radon topics that home
inspectors meet, see our [home inspector topics](/home-inspector/), and see the cluster's status on our
