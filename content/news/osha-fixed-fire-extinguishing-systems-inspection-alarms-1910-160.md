Some workplaces have a fire extinguishing system that is built in rather than carried, for example a dry chemical,
gaseous agent, water spray or foam system (the agent rules are in 1910.161 to 1910.163). OSHA's general industry
rules for these systems are in **29 CFR 1910.160**, "Fixed extinguishing systems, general." This explainer answers one
practical question from that section: what does an employer have to do to inspect, maintain and warn people about such
a system? It describes the section as published in the eCFR (current as of November 23, 2026); nothing here describes a rule change.

## Which systems does 1910.160 cover?

Under 1910.160(a)(1), the section "applies to all fixed extinguishing systems installed to meet a particular OSHA
standard except for automatic sprinkler systems which are covered by § 1910.159."

It also reaches some systems that no OSHA standard required. Paragraph (a)(2) covers "fixed systems not installed to
meet a particular OSHA standard, but which, by means of their operation, may expose employees to possible injury,
death, or adverse health consequences caused by the extinguishing agent." Those systems are subject only to paragraphs
(b)(4) through (b)(7) and (c). Paragraph (a)(3) exempts such systems entirely when they are "installed in areas with no
employee exposure."

The rest of this post describes systems installed to meet an OSHA standard. For the other covered systems, only the
safeguards, signs, annual inspection and refillable-container checks ((b)(4) to (b)(7)) and the total flooding rules in
(c) apply.

## How often must the system be inspected?

Three system checks have set intervals (the training review in (b)(10), below, is also annual):

- **Annual inspection.** Under (b)(6), the employer "shall assure that fixed systems are inspected annually by a person
  knowledgeable in the design and function of the system to assure that the system is maintained in good operating
  condition."
- **Refillable containers, semi-annually.** Under (b)(7), "the weight and pressure of refillable containers is checked
  at least semi-annually." A container goes to maintenance if it "shows a loss in net content or weight of more than 5
  percent, or a loss in pressure of more than 10 percent."
- **Factory charged nonrefillable containers, semi-annually.** Under (b)(8), those "which have no means of pressure
  indication are weighed at least semi-annually," and a container with a loss in net weight of more than 5 percent
  "shall be replaced."

## What records are required?

Paragraph (b)(9) requires that "inspection and maintenance dates are recorded on the container, on a tag attached to
the container, or in a central location." For the semi-annual check it adds: "A record of the last semi-annual check
shall be maintained until the container is checked again or for the life of the container, whichever is less."

## What happens when the system is out of service?

Paragraph (b)(2) states that if a fixed system "becomes inoperable, the employer shall notify employees and take the
necessary temporary precautions to assure their safety until the system is restored to operating order." It also
requires that "Any defects or impairments shall be properly corrected by trained personnel."

## What alarms and signs are required?

- **Discharge alarm.** Paragraph (b)(3) requires "a distinctive alarm or signaling system" that complies with 1910.165
  and "is capable of being perceived above ambient noise or light levels," in the parts of the workplace the system
  covers, to show when it is discharging. It adds: "Discharge alarms are not required on systems where discharge is
  immediately recognizable."
- **Keeping people out.** Paragraph (b)(4) requires "effective safeguards to warn employees against entry into
  discharge areas where the atmosphere remains hazardous to employee safety or health."
- **Signs.** Paragraph (b)(5) requires hazard warning or caution signs "at the entrance to, and inside of, areas
  protected by fixed extinguishing systems which use agents in concentrations known to be hazardous to employee safety
  and health."

Paragraph (b)(15) requires "at least one manual station" for discharge activation of each fixed system, and (b)(16)
requires that manual operating devices "are identified as to the hazard against which they will provide protection."

## Who needs training?

Paragraph (b)(10) covers only the employees designated to inspect, maintain, operate or repair the system. The employer "shall train employees designated to
inspect, maintain, operate, or repair fixed extinguishing systems and annually review their training to keep them
up-to-date in the functions they are to perform."

Paragraph (b)(17) also requires the employer to provide, and assure the use of, "the personal protective equipment
needed for immediate rescue of employees trapped in hazardous atmospheres created by an agent discharge."

## What extra rules apply to total flooding systems?

Paragraph (c) covers "Total flooding systems with potential health and safety hazards to employees." Paragraph (c)(2) exempts from all of paragraph (c) "Systems installed in areas where employees cannot enter during or
after the system's operation." For other total flooding systems, (c)(3) requires "a pre-discharge employee alarm" that complies with 1910.165 and gives "employees time to
safely exit from the discharge area prior to system discharge." Under (c)(4), they need automatic actuation "by means
of an approved fire detection device" interconnected with that pre-discharge alarm.

Paragraph (c)(1) requires an emergency action plan under 1910.38 for each area protected by a total flooding system
"which provides agent concentrations exceeding the maximum safe levels" set in 1910.162(b)(5) and (b)(6).

## Are any agents banned?

Yes, where people can be exposed. Paragraph (b)(11) states: "The employer shall not use chlorobromomethane or carbon
tetrachloride as an extinguishing agent where employees may be exposed."
