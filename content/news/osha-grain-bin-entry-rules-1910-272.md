OSHA's general industry rule for grain handling facilities is 29 CFR 1910.272, in Subpart R (Special Industries). This
explainer answers one question from the eCFR text (version of November 19, 2026): what must happen before a worker
enters a grain bin, silo or tank? Nothing here describes a rule change.

## Which facilities does 1910.272 cover?

Under 1910.272(a), the section "applies in addition to all other relevant provisions of part 1910 (or
part 1917 at marine terminals)."

Under (b)(1), paragraphs (a) through (n), including the entry rules, apply to "grain elevators, feed mills, flour mills, rice mills, dust
pelletizing plants, dry corn mills, soybean flaking operations, and the dry grinding operations of soycake." Only
paragraphs (o), (p) and (q) are limited to grain elevators.

## Which structures does paragraph (g) cover?

Paragraph (g) applies to employee entry into bins, silos, tanks "and other grain storage structures." It has one stated
exception: "Entry through unrestricted ground level openings into flat storage structures in which there are no
toxicity, flammability, oxygen-deficiency, or other atmospheric hazards is covered by paragraph (h) of this section." For paragraph (g),
"grain" includes "raw and processed grain and grain products" in facilities covered by (b)(1).

## Is a written permit required?

Usually. Under 1910.272(g)(1)(i), "The employer shall issue a permit for entering bins, silos, or tanks unless the
employer or the employer's representative (who would otherwise authorize the permit) is present during the entire
operation." The permit "shall certify that the precautions contained in this paragraph (§ 1910.272(g)) have been
implemented prior to employees entering," and it "shall be kept on file until completion of the entry operations."

## What must be done with the equipment?

Under 1910.272(g)(1)(ii), "All mechanical, electrical, hydraulic, and pneumatic equipment which presents a danger to
employees inside grain storage structures shall be deenergized and shall be disconnected, locked-out and tagged,
blocked-off, or otherwise prevented from operating by other equally effective means or methods."

## When must the air be tested?

Paragraph (g)(1)(iii) sets two triggers:

- Combustible gases, vapors and toxic agents are tested "when the employer has reason to believe they may be present."
- Oxygen content is tested "unless there is continuous natural air movement or continuous forced-air ventilation before
  and during the period employees are inside."

The same paragraph lists the unsafe conditions: oxygen "less than 19.5%," combustible gas or vapor "in excess of 10% of
the lower flammable limit," or toxic agents above "the ceiling values listed in subpart Z" or at levels that prevent
"self-rescue or communication to obtain assistance." If any is found, "Ventilation shall be provided until the unsafe condition or conditions are eliminated," and it "shall be continued as long as there is a possibility of recurrence of the unsafe condition" ((g)(1)(iii)(A)). If toxicity or oxygen deficiency cannot be eliminated by ventilation, entrants "shall wear an appropriate respirator" under § 1910.134 ((g)(1)(iii)(B)).

## Can a worker walk grain down?

No. Under 1910.272(g)(1)(iv), "'Walking down grain' and similar practices where an employee walks on grain to make it flow
within or out from a grain storage structure, or where an employee is on moving grain, are prohibited."

## When is a lifeline required?

Paragraph (g)(2) has two triggers: when an employee enters "from a level at or above the level of the stored grain or
grain products," or "walks or stands on or in stored grain of a depth which poses an engulfment hazard." The employer must then equip the employee with "a body harness with lifeline, or a boatswain's chair that meets the requirements of subpart D," and the
lifeline must "prevent the employee from sinking further than waist-deep in the grain."

Where that protection "is not feasible or creates a greater hazard," the employer must provide an
alternative means "demonstrated to prevent the employee from sinking further than waist-deep." A note adds that the lifeline or alternative means may be disconnected or removed on a surface the employer "demonstrates is free from engulfment hazards."

## Who must be outside, and what rescue gear is needed?

- (g)(3): "An observer, equipped to provide assistance, shall be stationed outside the bin, silo, or tank," with
  communications "(visual, voice, or signal line)" maintained with the entrant.
- (g)(4): rescue equipment "specifically suited for the bin, silo, or tank being entered."
- (g)(5): the observer "shall be trained in rescue procedures, including notification methods for obtaining additional
  assistance."

Paragraph (g)(6) adds: "Employees shall not enter bins, silos, or tanks underneath a bridging condition, or where a
buildup of grain products on the sides could fall and bury them."

## What training applies to bin entry?

Under 1910.272(e)(2), employees assigned special tasks "such as bin entry" must be trained "to perform these tasks
safely." Its note says this training "includes
training about engulfment and mechanical hazards and how to avoid them."

## Key points for study

Remember the (b)(1) facility list, the flat storage exception sending some entries to (h), and the (g)(1) steps: permit (unless the authorizing person is present throughout), equipment deenergized, atmosphere
tested under the stated triggers, no walking down grain. Key numbers: 19.5% oxygen, 10% of the lower flammable limit,
"waist-deep." The

[lockout/tagout study guide](/osha-general-industry/lockout-tagout/study-guide/) cover 1910.146
and 1910.147; the [OSHA general industry hub](/osha-general-industry/) has the rest.
