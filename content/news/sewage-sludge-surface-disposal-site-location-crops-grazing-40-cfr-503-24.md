Subpart C of the federal sewage sludge rule, 40 CFR Part 503, covers surface disposal: placing sewage sludge on land
for final disposal. Section 503.24 lists the management practices for it, and several of them decide where a
disposal unit may sit and how the land may be used while the unit is active. From the eCFR text (current as of November 13, 2026), this explainer answers: where may an active unit not be located,
and can crops be grown or animals grazed on it? It is not a rule change. Your State program and your permit may add
requirements of their own.

## What do "sewage sludge unit" and "surface disposal site" mean?

Section 503.21 defines the terms used throughout. A "Sewage sludge unit is land on which only sewage sludge is placed
for final disposal." The definition excludes land on which sewage sludge "is either stored or treated." An "Active sewage sludge unit is a sewage sludge unit that has not closed." A "Surface disposal
site is an area of land that contains one or more active sewage sludge units."

## Where may an active unit not be located?

Section 503.24 sets these location rules:

- **Wetlands.** "An active sewage sludge unit shall not be located in a wetland, except as provided in a permit
  issued pursuant to section 402 or 404 of the CWA." (503.24(f))
- **Unstable areas.** "An active sewage sludge unit shall not be located in an unstable area." (503.24(e)) Section
  503.21 defines an unstable area as "land subject to natural or human-induced forces that may damage the structural
  components of an active sewage sludge unit."
- **Floodplains.** The rule does not ban flood areas outright; it states that "An active sewage sludge unit shall not
  restrict the flow of a base flood." (503.24(b))
- **Faults.** A unit must be "60 meters or more from a fault that has displacement in Holocene time, unless otherwise
  specified by the permitting authority." (503.24(d))

Section 503.22(b) adds a deadline: an active unit within 60 meters of such a fault, in an unstable area, or in a
wetland (except as a section 402 or 404 permit provides) "shall close by March 22, 1994," and only in the fault case
may the permitting authority specify otherwise.

## What about earthquakes and endangered species?

A seismic impact zone does not rule a site out. Instead, "When a surface disposal site is located in a seismic impact
zone, an active sewage sludge unit shall be designed to withstand the maximum recorded horizontal ground level
acceleration." (503.24(c)) Section 503.21 defines a seismic impact zone as "an area that has a 10 percent or greater
probability that the horizontal ground level acceleration of the rock in the area exceeds 0.10 gravity once in 250
years."

For wildlife, 503.24(a) states: "Sewage sludge shall not be placed on an active sewage sludge unit if it is likely to
adversely affect a threatened or endangered species listed under section 4 of the Endangered Species Act or its
designated critical habitat."

## Can crops be grown or animals grazed on an active unit?

Not by default. Section 503.24(k) states that "A food crop, a feed crop, or a fiber crop shall not be grown on an
active sewage sludge unit, unless the owner/operator of the surface disposal site demonstrates to the permitting
authority that through management practices public health and the environment are protected from any reasonably
anticipated adverse effects of pollutants in sewage sludge when crops are grown."

Grazing follows the same pattern. Under 503.24(l), "Animals shall not be grazed on an active sewage sludge unit,"
again unless the owner/operator makes the same kind of demonstration to the permitting authority for grazing.

## How is groundwater protected?

Section 503.24(n)(1) states: "Sewage sludge placed on an active sewage sludge unit shall not contaminate an aquifer."
Under 503.21, "contaminate an aquifer" means introducing a substance that causes the maximum contaminant level for
nitrate in 40 CFR 141.62(b) to be exceeded in ground water, or that raises nitrate in ground water already above
that level.

The proof comes from a specialist. Section 503.24(n)(2) requires that "Results of a ground-water monitoring program
developed by a qualified ground-water scientist or a certification by a qualified ground-water scientist shall be
used to demonstrate" that the sludge does not contaminate an aquifer. Section 503.21 defines a qualified ground-water
scientist as someone with "a baccalaureate or post-graduate degree in the natural sciences or engineering" who has
sufficient training and experience in ground-water hydrology and related fields.

## What else does 503.24 cover?

The section also has run-off and leachate collection rules, methane limits and public access limits. Our study guide
covers the run-off system's storm capacity, the methane limits for a covered active unit, and public access; for liner-and-leachate units, 503.24(h) and (i) require the
leachate collection system to be operated and maintained, and leachate collected and disposed, while the unit is
active "and for three years after the sewage sludge unit closes."
