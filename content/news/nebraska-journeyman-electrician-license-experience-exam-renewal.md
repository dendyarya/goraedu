This explainer covers **Nebraska only**, where electricians are licensed under the State Electrical Act by the Nebraska State
Electrical Board. It answers one practical question from the
board's pages and from the Act: what experience, exam and renewal steps apply to a Nebraska **journeyman
electrician** license? Nothing here describes a rule change, and nothing here applies to any other state.

## How much experience does a Nebraska journeyman applicant need?

Section 81-2109 of the Act, subsection (1), states: "An applicant for a journeyman electrician license shall have at
least four years' experience". The board's Journeyman Electrician page
puts the same rule in hours: "Have at least four years of verifiable experience (8000 Hours), acceptable to the
board, in the electrical trade."

Both the Act and the page add that "Registration as an apprentice electrician for those years shall, on the approval of
the board," "constitute evidence of such experience."

## Does schooling or military service count?

Section 81-2109 says the board "may by rule or regulation provide for the allowance of one year of experience credit"
"for successful completion of a two-year post-high school electrical course approved by the board." The board's page
ties that credit to a two-year course "with an Associates' degree in electrical technology."

On military service, the page states: "Work experience credit may be given for qualified electrical experience gained in
military service." The page does not say how many hours of credit that may be.

## What is a "Restricted" license?

The board's page states: "If your verifiable experience is less than 4000 hours in the State of Nebraska, then you
will have a "Restricted" license" "and it will not be able to reciprocate with any other state." The page does not
explain what else, if anything, the restriction changes.

## What does the exam involve?

Section 81-2115 says that "In addition to the education and experience requirements imposed in the State Electrical
Act", an applicant must, "prior to issuance of a license under the act, pass a written examination given by the board to insure his or her competence."
The section adds: "Examinations shall be given at least twice yearly."

The exam "shall contain reasonable questions based upon the then current National Electrical Code" "and upon
electrical theory." It also states: "When answering questions based upon the National Electrical Code, the applicant may
refer to an open copy of such code."

The board's Exam Information page says the exam for all license types except apprentice registration "will cover,
but is not limited to the following": "National Electrical Code - 2023", "Basic Electricity - Theory", "Nebraska State
Electrical Act" and "Emergency Circuits - Including Fire Alarm Circuits". It also states: "If you successfully pass your
exam, you will be awarded 6 hours of continuing education credit" "towards your total needed for license renewal!"

That page does not state the number of questions, the time limit or the passing score. It names a waiting period for
retesting ("Policy 37 - Effective July 11, 2025"), but the policy itself is in a separate document not covered here.

## When does a license expire, and how is it renewed?

Section 81-2118 states: "All licenses or registrations issued under the State Electrical Act shall expire on December 31
of each even-numbered year." It describes renewal "beginning October 1" of even-numbered years and a late renewal period
"from December 1 through December 31 of even-numbered years". It also states: "All holders of expired licenses or
registrations shall apply for a new license or registration."

Section 81-2115 adds that "Any licensee failing to renew his or her license by April 1 after its expiration" "shall take
the examination before he or she is issued a new license."

## How much continuing education is needed to renew?

Section 81-2117.01, subsection (1), states: "In order to renew a license issued under the State Electrical Act, the
licensee shall be required to complete twelve contact hours of continuing education by January 1 of each odd-numbered
year." The board's Continuing Education page adds that "The contact hours shall include a minimum of six contact
hours studying the National Electrical Code" and that the courses "shall be approved by the State Electrical Board".

Under section 81-2117.01(1)(c) of the Act, "a contact hour means fifty minutes of classroom attendance at an approved
course under a qualified instructor approved by the board." Section 81-2117.01, subsection (3), says
the licensee "shall retain the attendance certificate" "and attach it to the application for renewal" and "shall have
the responsibility for record keeping" "and providing proof of attendance at continuing education courses."

## Where to check before you act

These are the requirements as shown on the board's pages and in the State Electrical Act on the
date above. The board is the authority, so confirm the current experience rules, exam content, retest policy, renewal
dates and approved courses with it before you count hours, schedule an exam or renew. The residential journeyman and
Class B journeyman licenses, apprentice registration and reciprocity with other states are set out separately and are
not covered here. These rules describe Nebraska only; other states set their own. Our
