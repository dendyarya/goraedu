This explainer covers **Connecticut only**, where home inspectors are licensed by the Department of Consumer Protection
(DCP) with the Home Inspection Licensing Board (section 20-490a). It answers one practical question: what does it take to get and keep a
Connecticut home inspector license? It is built from Chapter 400f of the Connecticut General Statutes on the General
Assembly's site, the DCP's license and intern permit application (form HOI-01, Rev. 12/2023) and the DCP's continuing
education page, all read on 2026-11-26. Nothing here describes a rule change, and nothing here applies to any other
state.

## Do you need a license to inspect homes in Connecticut?

Yes, unless an exemption applies. Section 20-492(a) says "No person shall engage in home inspection without a license
issued under section 20-492a" or subsection (b) of section 20-492b. The same subsection says sections 20-490 to 20-495a do
not apply to architects or professional engineers licensed in Connecticut, and lists other people who may act within the scope of their
profession, including state and local government employees acting within the scope of their employment, federal employees, holders of another
license or registration issued under Connecticut's general statutes doing only the work it covers, and intern permit holders inspecting under section 20-493b(c) to
qualify for a license.

## How do you get a home inspector intern permit?

Connecticut's path starts with an intern permit. Section 20-493b(a) says that to be eligible for a home inspector intern
permit, an applicant shall "Have successfully completed high school or its equivalent;" and "Have enrolled in and
completed a board-approved training program that meets the requirements of regulations adopted by the commissioner
pursuant to section 20-491;". The applicant must also have an identified supervisor who "is licensed as a home
inspector", "is in good standing with the state as a home inspector", and "has agreed to perform supervisory functions
as described in subsection (c) of this section". The DCP application asks for a "copy of course completion certificate
with application". The statute and the application do not state the number of training hours; they refer to the
board-approved program and the regulations.

## How does supervision work during the internship?

Section 20-493b(c) says "A home inspector intern shall perform functions of a home inspector only under the supervision
of a licensed home inspector". It adds: "Such supervision shall be direct supervision for a minimum of the first ten
inspections, and then may be indirect for the remaining training period." Direct supervision means the supervisor
"shall be present at the site of a home inspection where the home inspector intern is assigned one hundred per cent of
the time and such supervisor shall countersign all documents prepared and completed by the home inspector intern."

## What does the full license require?

Section 20-492b(a) says an applicant for a home inspector license shall "Have successfully completed high school or its
equivalent;", "Have earned a home inspector intern permit and performed not less than one hundred home inspections in
accordance with subsection (c) of section 20-493b;" and "Have passed an oral, written or electronic competency
examination administered by the department". The DCP application adds that for the 100 inspections "the associated
logs, signed by the Supervisor, must be submitted with the application." It also says: "If you have passed the
National Home Inspector Examination, include your score report with your application."

## What happens if you fail the exam?

Under section 20-493b(b), an intern who completes the board-approved training program may take the licensure
examination. If the intern does not pass, the intern "may take up to two additional examinations during the one-year
period commencing on the date of the applicant's first examination application." The same subsection says: "If the
applicant does not pass a third examination the applicant may not be examined again until one year after the date of
such third examination."

## How often is the license renewed?

Section 20-492a(c) says "All licenses issued under the provisions of this section shall expire biennially". The DCP
application states: "Home Inspector licenses and Intern permits expire on June 30th of odd years."

## How many continuing education hours are required?

The DCP's continuing education page lists "20 credit hours per two-year cycle." It says "All continuing education (CE)
hours must be completed between April 1 of an odd-numbered year and March 31 of the odd-numbered year two years later",
which it notes is "3 months before the renewal deadline." Each cycle "must include a course of at least 3 credit hours
in current home inspection legislation, licensing laws and regulations." Licensees "must attest to the completion of
their required continuing education (CE) as part of the renewal process" and should keep certificates "in case of audit
by the Department of Consumer Protection (DCP)." The Home Inspection Licensing Board may grant an exception "Upon appropriate showing of a bona fide health or other
individual hardship"; requests must be in writing not less than sixty days before license renewal and include an
explanation and verification of the hardship.
