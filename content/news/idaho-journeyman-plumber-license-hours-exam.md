This explainer covers **Idaho only**, where plumbing licenses are issued through the Division of Occupational and
Professional Licenses (DOPL). It answers one practical question from DOPL's own pages and Idaho's plumbing law: what
does it take to get a journeyman plumber license in Idaho? These are Idaho's rules only, nothing here describes a rule
change, and nothing here applies to any other state.

## What are the two pathways?

DOPL's Initial Licensure for Plumbing Journeyman page lays out the steps in order. Both pathways begin with the same
step: "Apply for and Maintain an Active Apprentice Registration". From there, the page shows a choice:

- **Experience plus school:** "Obtain 8,000hrs of Work Experience" "AND" "4 years of approved schooling"
- **Experience only:** "Obtain 16,000hrs of Work Experience"

Both pathways then lead to "Register for and Pass the IAPMO Exam through PSI" and finally "Apply for Licensure through
DOPL".

## How much schooling counts as "approved schooling"?

DOPL's licensing page says "4 years of approved schooling". The Plumbing Journeyman Application (revised October 3,
2024) states the school pathway in hours instead: an applicant who "Attended and completed 576 hours of instruction at
a board approved Plumbing Apprenticeship Program". Read both documents if you are counting your school time, and ask
DOPL if your program's hours are unclear.

## Do I have to be registered as an apprentice?

Yes. DOPL's licensing page lists an active apprentice registration as the first step of both pathways, and Idaho Code 54-2614 says apprentices are not examined for competency, but must register
with DOPL and keep that registration while they gain experience.

The journeyman application says the same thing about counting hours: "If hours are obtained in state you must have held
an active Idaho Plumbing Apprentice Registration while completing the required 8,000 hours of supervised work." On the
work verification section it adds: "Active registrations are required in Idaho for hours to count."

DOPL's Plumbing FAQs page, answering "What happens if I choose to work unlicensed?", states: "Hours worked without being licensed will not count towards
the required OJT time."

## What kind of work counts toward the hours?

Supervised plumbing installation work, verified on DOPL's form. The application asks for a "Work Verification Form
verifying 8,000 hours of supervised plumbing installation work has been completed" on the school pathway, and a form
verifying 16,000 hours for the experience-only pathway. It also states: "Work hours can be obtained in or out of the
State of Idaho."

Some specialty work is excluded. The application states: "Work experience in pipe fitting and appliance plumbing
specialty work will not count towards the requirements to obtain a journeyman license." It also warns that "any of the
hours submitted are subject to additional verification."

## When do I take the exam?

Before you apply. DOPL's licensing page says applicants "must meet the requirements for licensure and pass the IAPMO
examination before submitting an application to DOPL." The application repeats the point: "Both the Idaho
Apprenticeship School and Work Experience Pathways require a passing examination score prior to the issuance of a
license."

## How long is the license good for, and is continuing education required?

Idaho Code 54-2617, the section on certificate expiration and renewal, states: "Certificates of competency shall be
issued for a period of three (3) years". It says a certificate of competency for a plumbing contractor or
journeyman "may be renewed at any time during the month prior to its expiration by providing proof of completion of the
continuing education requirements as established by the board".

On continuing education, the statute ties renewal to that proof. DOPL's Plumbing FAQs page answers the question directly: "There are no Continuing
Education Requirements (CEU) for plumbing." The statute and the FAQ read differently, so check DOPL's renewal
instructions at the time you renew.
