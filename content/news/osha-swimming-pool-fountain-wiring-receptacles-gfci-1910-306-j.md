When a workplace has a pool or fountain, OSHA's general industry electrical rules include a paragraph just for it: 29
CFR 1910.306(j), "Swimming pools, fountains, and similar installations." This explainer answers one practical question: how far from a pool may receptacles and lights be, and
what needs ground-fault circuit interrupter (GFCI) protection? It reads the rule as it stands in the eCFR (text current
to November 23, 2026). It is an explainer, not a rule change. OSHA rules protect employees at workplaces; they do not
license electricians. Electrician licensing is set by each state (and in some places by a city); this post does not cover any of them. For how the two differ, see our post on
[OSHA's "qualified person" and an electrician license](/news/osha-qualified-person-electrical-work-vs-electrician-license/).

## What installations are covered?

1910.306(j) "applies to electric wiring for and equipment in or adjacent to all swimming, wading, therapeutic, and
decorative pools and fountains; hydro-massage bathtubs, whether permanently installed or storable; and metallic
auxiliary equipment, such as pumps, filters, and similar equipment. Therapeutic pools in health care facilities are
exempt from these provisions."

## How far from the pool must receptacles be?

Under 1910.306(j)(1)(i), "A single receptacle of the locking and grounding type that provides power for a permanently
installed swimming pool recirculating pump motor may be located not less than 1.52 m (5 ft) from the inside walls of a
pool. All other receptacles on the property shall be located at least 3.05 m (10 ft) from the inside walls of a pool."

The distance is not measured in a straight line through walls. The note to paragraph (j)(1) says "the distance to be
measured is the shortest path the supply cord of an appliance connected to the receptacle would follow without piercing
a floor, wall, or ceiling of a building or other effective permanent barrier."

Where a pool is installed permanently at a dwelling unit, 1910.306(j)(1)(iii) requires "at least one 125-volt, 15- or
20-ampere receptacle on a general-purpose branch circuit" located "a minimum of 3.05 m (10 ft) and not more than 6.08 m
(20 ft) from the inside wall of the pool," and "not more than 1.98 m (6.5 ft) above the floor, platform, or grade level
serving the pool."

## Which receptacles need GFCI protection?

1910.306(j)(1)(ii) states: "Receptacles that are located within 4.57 m (15 ft), or 6.08 m (20 ft) if the installation
was built after August 13, 2007, of the inside walls of the pool shall be protected by ground-fault circuit
interrupters."

## Where can lights and ceiling fans go?

For outdoor pool areas, 1910.306(j)(2)(i) says lighting fixtures, lighting outlets and ceiling-suspended (paddle) fans
"may not be installed over the pool or over the area extending 1.52 m (5 ft) horizontally from the inside walls of a
pool unless no part of the lighting fixture of a ceiling-suspended (paddle) fan is less than 3.66 m (12 ft) above the
maximum water level." A lighting fixture or outlet installed before April 16, 1981, may be closer than 5 feet
horizontally "if it is at least 1.52 m (5 ft) above the surface of the maximum water level and is rigidly attached to
the existing structure," and it must then be protected by a GFCI in the branch circuit supplying it.

Between 5 and 10 feet horizontally from the inside walls of a pool, 1910.306(j)(2)(ii) requires lighting fixtures and
lighting outlets to be GFCI-protected "unless installed 1.52 m (5 ft) above the maximum water level and rigidly attached
to the structure adjacent to or enclosing the pool."

## Cords, underwater lights and fountains

- Cords (1910.306(j)(3)): flexible cords "may not exceed 0.9 m (3 ft) in length and shall have a copper equipment
  grounding conductor with a grounding-type attachment plug" when used with cord- and plug-connected lighting fixtures
  installed within 16 feet of the water surface of permanently installed pools, and with "Other cord- and
  plug-connected, fixed or stationary equipment used with permanently installed pools."
- Underwater equipment (1910.306(j)(4)): "A ground-fault circuit interrupter shall be installed in the branch circuit
  supplying underwater fixtures operating at more than 15 volts. Equipment installed underwater shall be identified for
  the purpose." Also, "No underwater lighting fixtures may be installed for operation at over 150 volts between
  conductors," and an upward-facing fixture "shall have the lens adequately guarded to prevent contact by any person."
- Fountains (1910.306(j)(5)): "All electric equipment, including power supply cords, operating at more than 15 volts
  and used with fountains shall be protected by ground-fault circuit interrupters."

## Do these rules apply to older pools?

OSHA's extent-of-application paragraph, 29 CFR 1910.302(b), decides by installation date. No part of 1910.306(j) is on the (b)(1) list of
requirements that apply "regardless of when they were designed or installed." Under (b)(2), installations and equipment
"installed or overhauled after March 15, 1972" must comply with 1910.302 through 1910.308, except as noted in (b)(3) and
(b)(4). The fountain rule, 1910.306(j)(5), is listed in (b)(3) as applying "only to electrical installations and
utilization equipment installed after April 16, 1981," and the dwelling-unit receptacle rule, 1910.306(j)(1)(iii), is
listed in (b)(4) as applying only to those "installed after August 13, 2007."
