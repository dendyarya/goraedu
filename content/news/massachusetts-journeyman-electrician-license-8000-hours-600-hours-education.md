This explainer covers **Massachusetts only**, where electricians are licensed by the Board of State Examiners of
Electricians. It answers one practical question from the Board's own pages and regulations on Mass.gov: what does it
take to qualify for the Massachusetts journeyman electrician exam and license? It describes those pages as published on
the date above. Nothing here describes a rule change, and nothing here applies to any other state.

## What are the three main requirements?

The Board's page "Apply for an individual electrical or systems license" lists the journeyman requirements in three
lines:

- "Education: 600 hours of Board-approved education."
- "Continuing Education: 15-hour Code-Update Class."
- "Experience: 8,000 hours of work over at least 4 years under a licensed Massachusetts Journeyman."

The page describes the order of steps: complete a Board-approved education program, take the code-specific continuing
education course, gain the required work experience, then apply online so the exam can be scheduled.

## What does the regulation add?

The eligibility rules are in 237 CMR 13.00. Section 13.03(1) covers the journeyman license:

- **Experience.** 13.03(1)(a) asks for a minimum of 8,000 clock hours in no less than four years before the application
  for examination, as an apprentice under the direct supervision of a Massachusetts licensed journeyman electrician.
  Applicants may also receive credit for electrical shop experience in a public vocational school program approved by
  the Department of Elementary and Secondary Education; all programs are subject to Board approval.
- **Systems work.** Under 13.03(1)(a)(2), applicants whose systems work experience was supervised by a Systems
  Technician "may receive a maximum of 4000 clock hours in not less than 2 years."
- **Old experience.** 13.03(1)(a)(3) states: "Apprentices shall not be given credit for work experience obtained more
  than twenty years prior to the date of application."
- **Education.** 13.03(1)(b) asks for 600 clock hours of classroom instruction, completed without substantial
  interruption, under 237 CMR 22.01. The regulation lists the kinds of schools that may give it (public vocational
  schools, private occupational schools, colleges and universities, and Board-approved trade organizations) and caps the
  hours credited from a public vocational school at 300.
- **Diploma.** 13.03(1)(c) requires the applicant to "furnish documentary proof satisfactory to the Board of having
  obtained a high school diploma or equivalent."

Section 13.05(1) adds the 15-hour continuing education program under 237 CMR 17.00, based on the Massachusetts
Electrical Code (527 CMR 12.00) in effect when you apply. Section 13.05(2) says education expires for licensure purposes
twenty years after the program starts.

## What does the 600 hours cover?

237 CMR 22.01 lists the subjects the 600 hours must include, "but not be limited to". Among them are "DC Theory," "AC
Theory," "Wiring Methods," "Motors and Controls," "Transformers," "Massachusetts Electrical Code and Amendments (527 CMR
12.00)" and "Job-site and Electrical Safety." The section also allows a modular format given in two tiers of 300 hours.

## How are the 20-year limits counted?

The Board's FAQ page explains both limits. For experience: "Work experience will be creditable so long as it was
obtained within 20 years of the date of application". For education, it says education "will generally be creditable
for 20 years, calculated starting from the date the educational program begins".

## Does the Board register apprentices?

No. The FAQ states: "The Board does not register apprentices." The hours are documented when you apply, through the
education and work experience forms in the application checklist. The FAQ adds that employers must keep records for
"no less than nine years," citing 237 CMR 18.01(3).

## How do you apply and take the exam?

The application page states: "Applications, renewals, and reinstatements must be submitted online through the eLIPSE
platform." After the Board approves the application, the exam is scheduled with the testing company named on the page.
Its checklist includes the education and work experience forms, a criminal record (CORI) form, the 15-hour continuing
education certificate and a photo.

## What if you are licensed in another state?

The FAQ states: "MA only has reciprocity with NH." Other out-of-state applicants apply for, sit for and pass the
journeyman exam, and the FAQ says any out-of-state education "would need to be documented on the Out-of-State Education
Program Form." For someone already licensed as a master in another state (other than New Hampshire), the FAQ says to
first apply for, pass and hold the Massachusetts journeyman license; the one-year journeyman requirement for the master
exam can then be waived with proof of the other state's master license, and the master application must document a
Board-approved 150-hour master course or an equivalent 150-hour course from an out-of-state provider.
