A drinking water operator may collect many samples, but a result counts for compliance under the sections listed in
141.28 only if the right person analyzed the sample. The federal rule is short: 40 CFR 141.28, "Certified laboratories", in Subpart C of the
National Primary Drinking Water Regulations. This explainer reads it, and the matching lead and copper rule, as they
stand in the eCFR (current to November 12, 2026). Nothing here describes a rule change.

## Which results must come from a certified laboratory?

141.28(a) states: "For the purpose of determining compliance with §§ 141.21 through 141.27, 141.40, 141.74, 141.89,
141.402, 141.901, and 141.902, samples may be considered only if they have been analyzed by a laboratory certified by
EPA or the State except that measurements of alkalinity, disinfectant residual, orthophosphate, pH, silica,
temperature, and turbidity may be performed by any person acceptable to the State."

The sections it names, by their eCFR headings, are:

| Section(s)        | eCFR heading                                                                                           |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 141.21            | Coliform sampling                                                                                      |
| 141.22            | Turbidity sampling and analytical requirements                                                         |
| 141.23            | Inorganic chemical sampling and analytical requirements                                                |
| 141.24            | Organic chemicals, sampling and analytical requirements                                                |
| 141.25 and 141.26 | Analytical methods for radioactivity; monitoring frequency and compliance requirements for radionuclides in community water systems |
| 141.27            | Alternate analytical techniques                                                                        |
| 141.40            | Monitoring requirements for unregulated contaminants (Subpart E, Special Regulations, Including Monitoring)                                                   |
| 141.74            | Analytical and monitoring requirements (Subpart H, Filtration and Disinfection)                        |
| 141.89            | Analytical methods (Subpart I, Control of Lead and Copper)                                             |
| 141.402           | Ground water source microbial monitoring and analytical methods (Subpart S, Ground Water Rule)         |
| 141.901 and 141.902 | Analytical requirements; monitoring requirements (Subpart Z, Control of Per- and Polyfluoroalkyl Substances (PFAS))                                   |

Rows without a subpart named are in Subpart C. Any compliance result under those sections, other than the seven
measurements listed below, has to come from a laboratory certified by EPA or the State.

## Which measurements can a non-laboratory person take?

The same sentence lists seven: "alkalinity, disinfectant residual, orthophosphate, pH, silica, temperature, and
turbidity." For these, the rule does not require a certified laboratory. It requires "any person acceptable to the
State." That is the State's decision; 141.28 does not say who the State must accept or how.

## What about lead and copper?

141.89(a)(1) sets a similar split for the lead and copper rule, naming four of the seven measurements: "Analyses for alkalinity, orthophosphate, pH, and silica
may be performed by any person acceptable to the State. Analyses under this section for lead and copper shall only be
conducted by laboratories that have been certified by EPA or the State."

It also lists what a laboratory must do to be certified for lead and copper. It must "Analyze Performance Evaluation
samples, which include lead and copper, provided by or acceptable to EPA or the State at least once a year by each
method for which the laboratory desires certification", meet acceptance limits (for lead, "±30 percent of the actual
amount in the Performance Evaluation sample when the actual amount is greater than or equal to 0.005 mg/L"; for copper,
"±10 percent" at or above 0.050 mg/L), achieve a lead method detection limit of 0.001 mg/L, and "Be currently certified
by EPA or the State" to perform those analyses.

## Can the State take its own samples?

Yes. 141.28(b) states: "Nothing in this part shall be construed to preclude the State or any duly designated
representative of the State from taking samples or from using the results from such samples to determine compliance by
a supplier of water with the applicable requirements of this part."

## Who certifies the laboratories?

To receive primary enforcement responsibility (primacy), a State must meet conditions that include a laboratory
certification program, unless EPA's Administrator waives it as 142.10(b)(3) allows. 40 CFR 142.10(b)(3)(i) lists "The establishment and maintenance of a State program for the certification of
laboratories conducting analytical measurements of drinking water contaminants pursuant to the requirements of the State
primary drinking water regulations". 142.10(b)(4) adds "Assurance of the availability to the State of laboratory
facilities certified by the Administrator and capable of performing analytical measurements of all contaminants
specified in the State primary drinking water regulations."

The federal text does not list which laboratories are certified in your state. Ask your state's drinking water program
which laboratories it has certified.

## What this means for operators

- Send compliance samples under the listed sections to a laboratory certified by EPA or the State.
- The seven measurements listed in 141.28(a), such as pH, temperature, turbidity and disinfectant residual, may be
  performed by any person acceptable to the State; check your state's rules for who that is.
- State regulations must be "no less stringent than" the federal rules (40 CFR 142.10(a)) and may differ in detail;
  this post covers only the federal text.
