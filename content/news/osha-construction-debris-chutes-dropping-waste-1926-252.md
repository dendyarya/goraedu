For OSHA construction safety, the federal rule on dropping debris and disposing of waste on a job site is 29 CFR 1926.252, "Disposal of waste materials," in Subpart H (Materials
Handling, Storage, Use, and Disposal) of the construction standards. This explainer reads the section as it stands in
the eCFR (current to November 12, 2026). Nothing here describes a rule change, and it does not cover general industry
workplaces under Part 1910.

## When is a chute required?

1926.252(a) states: "Whenever materials are dropped more than 20 feet to any point lying outside the exterior walls of
the building, an enclosed chute of wood, or equivalent material, shall be used."

That sentence has three parts:

- **Distance:** the drop is "more than 20 feet."
- **Where it lands:** "any point lying outside the exterior walls of the building."
- **What is required:** "an enclosed chute of wood, or equivalent material."

The rule does not tie the chute requirement to the type of material; it says "materials."

## What counts as an enclosed chute?

The same paragraph defines it: "For the purpose of this paragraph, an enclosed chute is a slide, closed in on all
sides, through which material is moved from a high place to a lower one."

The chute must be "closed in on all sides."

## What if debris is dropped through a hole in the floor?

1926.252(b) covers dropping debris through holes in the floor without chutes: "When debris is dropped
through holes in the floor without the use of chutes, the area onto which the material is dropped shall be completely
enclosed with barricades not less than 42 inches high and not less than 6 feet back from the projected edge of the
opening above."

The same paragraph adds two more requirements:

- "Signs warning of the hazard of falling materials shall be posted at each level."
- "Removal shall not be permitted in this lower area until debris handling ceases above."

In short, the landing area is completely enclosed with barricades at least 42 inches high and at least 6 feet back from
the projected edge of the opening above, warning signs go up at each level, and nobody removes debris from the lower
area until debris handling above has ceased.

## Does scrap have to be cleared as the job goes?

Yes. 1926.252(c) states: "All scrap lumber, waste material, and rubbish shall be removed from the immediate work area as
the work progresses."

## Can waste be burned on site?

1926.252 sets no burning conditions of its own; it points to local fire regulations. 1926.252(d) states: "Disposal of waste material or debris
by burning shall comply with local fire regulations." The section itself sets no other burning conditions.

## How must oily rags and solvent waste be kept?

1926.252(e) states: "All solvent waste, oily rags, and flammable liquids shall be kept in fire resistant covered
containers until removed from worksite."

The containers must be both "fire resistant" and "covered," and the rule applies "until removed from worksite."
