This explainer covers **Iowa only**, where plumbers are licensed by the Plumbing and Mechanical Systems Board, whose pages
are on the site of the Iowa Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing (DIAL). Iowa's law and the board's pages use the term
"journeyperson". The post answers one practical question: how do you qualify for the Iowa journeyperson plumber
license, and how is it renewed? It draws on Iowa Code chapter 105 (2026 edition, published by the Iowa Legislature) and
the board's licensing pages as read on the date above. Nothing here describes a rule change, and nothing here applies to
any other state.

## What does Iowa law require for the journeyperson license?

Iowa Code section 105.18(2)(b)(1) lists the requirements. To be licensed "as a journeyperson in the applicable
discipline", a person must do all of the following:

- File an application and pay the fees set by the board. The application must "establish that the person meets the
  minimum educational and experience requirements adopted by the board."
- "Pass the state journeyperson licensing examination in the applicable discipline."
- "Provide the board with evidence of having completed at least four years of practical experience as an apprentice."

The same paragraph adds that, "Commencing January 1, 2010", those four years of practical experience must come from "an
apprenticeship training program registered by the United States department of labor office of apprenticeship."

The board's "Journey Level License" page adds an age minimum: applicants "Must be at least 18 years old." Its
"Post-Examination Licensure" page states that an applicant who is in an apprenticeship "must also have graduated from
the apprentice program."

## Is there a route for plumbers licensed in another state?

The "Journey Level License" page lists one: "Provide evidence that the applicant possesses a master level license in
the same discipline from another jurisdiction." That route is tied to a **master** level license, not a journey level
one.

The page also describes a reciprocity agreement: "In May 2016, the board approved its first reciprocity agreement with
the South Dakota State Plumbing Commission." It says the agreement "applies only to the journeyperson and master
plumbing license requirements" and covers people "who obtained their license on the basis of written examination", meaning a South Dakota
plumbing license. The page says applicants need a non-Iowa address when they apply, must send a copy of the South
Dakota license with a certificate of good standing or license verification from the South Dakota Plumbing Commission,
and must agree to follow all other Iowa laws and rules.

## How does the exam step work?

The "Examination" page says "an applicant must first complete a state of Iowa Examination Candidate Application." The
"Journey Level License" page asks applicants to "Allow up to two weeks for a complete Exam Approval Application to be
processed." The "Examination" page says that if the application is complete and eligibility requirements are met,
"an Exam Approval Letter will be sent to you."

Two timing points come from the same "Examination" page. First, for journeyperson candidates in an apprentice program, "a journeyperson examination applicant may apply to
sit for the examination up to 6 months prior to completion of the 48 months of required apprentice credit". Second,
"Your eligibility to sit for the examination will be valid for one year", and within that period "You may test as many
times as necessary during the approval period". Each attempt requires its own examination fee paid to Kirkwood. The page names "Kirkwood Community College" as "the board's approved
examination provider."

Passing does not finish the process. The page states: "A license is NOT automatically issued after you pass the
examination." The next step is a post-examination licensure application, which the "Post-Examination Licensure" page
says "will not be processed until all required criteria are complete and the certificate of completion from the
United States Department of Labor Office of Apprenticeship has been issued."

## How long does the license last, and how is it renewed?

Iowa Code section 105.20(1) states: "All licenses issued under this chapter shall be issued for a three-year period."
Under section 105.20(2), a license may be renewed "without examination", and the renewal application and fee must reach the board "at
least thirty days prior to the expiration date of the license."

The board's "Licensing Period & Fees" page states that "All Plumbing & Mechanical Systems licenses are on a 3-year
license period" and that licenses end with the shared period "regardless of the issue date." It gives the current
period as July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2029.

Continuing education is part of renewal. Section 105.20(5)(a) states: "The board shall require at least eight
classroom hours of instruction during each three-year licensing term." The hours must come from "courses or seminars
which have been approved by the board."
Section 105.20(5)(b) gives "a thirty-day grace period after expiration of the licensing term to complete all
requirements necessary for license renewal without penalty."

## Where to check before you act

These are the requirements as shown in the Iowa Code and on the board's pages on the date above. The Iowa Plumbing and
Mechanical Systems Board and DIAL are the authority, so confirm the current forms, fees, exam scheduling, continuing
education and renewal steps with them before you apply. These rules describe Iowa only; other states set their own. For
other plumbing topics on goraedu, see our [plumbing page](/plumbing/).
