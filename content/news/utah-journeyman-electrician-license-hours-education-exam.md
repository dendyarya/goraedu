Electrician licenses are issued by each state, and this explainer covers **Utah only**, where electricians are licensed
by the Division of Professional Licensing (DOPL) in the Utah Department of Commerce. It answers one practical question
from DOPL's own electrical pages: what hours, schooling and exams lead to a Utah **Journeyman Electrician** license?
Nothing here describes a rule change, and nothing here applies to any other state.

## Which electrician licenses does Utah have?

DOPL's exam information page states: "There are five electrician license classifications in Utah:" and lists them as
"Apprentice, Journeyman, Master, Residential Journeyman and Residential Master". For the apprentice classification it
states: "There are no exam requirements for this classification". This post is about the (non-residential) journeyman
license; the residential journeyman license has its own hour figures on DOPL's site.

## What is the first route: apprenticeship school plus experience?

DOPL's application page for the journeyman license opens with "The following requirements must be met:" and lists
three items for this route:

- "Completion of a 4 year (576 classroom hours) electrical apprenticeship education program."
- "8,000 hours licensed electrical experience working under supervision of a licensed journeyman or master
  electrician."
- "Pass the Utah Journeyman Electrician Written exam and the Utah Electrician Practical exam."

DOPL's exam information page, under "Testing Eligibility Requirements:", describes the same route as "Completion of a 4
year (min. 576 classroom hours) electrical apprenticeship education program" "or the competency exam equivalence",
together with experience as a licensed apprentice electrician. On the date checked, the two pages gave different hour
figures for that experience: the application page says 8,000 hours, while the exam page gives 6,000 hours (written
"6.000" on the page). DOPL's pages do not explain the difference, so ask DOPL which figure applies to you before you
count your hours.

## Is there a route without the school program?

Yes. The application page lists a second route after the word "-OR-": "16,000 hours of licensed electrical experience
working under supervision of a licensed journeyman or master electrician." followed by the same exam requirement. The
exam page gives this alternative as "16,000 hours of electrical experience as a licensed apprentice electrician".

In both routes, the experience is worked while licensed and under the supervision of a licensed journeyman or master
electrician, as the application page states.

## Which exams are required?

The exam information page names two exams for the journeyman license: the "Utah Journeyman Electrician Written Exam"
and the "Utah Electrician Practical Exam (6 modules)". It also states: "Preapproval for testing is NOT required." The
page links the candidate information bulletin and a page to schedule a Division licensing exam.

DOPL's FAQ answers the question "Can I take my journeyman/master, etc. exam now and earn my on-the-job work hours
later?" with: "No, state law requires on-the-job work hours and school hours/grades be met before one is approved to
take the exam." So the hours and the schooling come first, then the exams.

## Can the exams be taken outside Utah?

The FAQ answers this for one group only: an "out-of-state applicant for a Journeyman Electrician license". For the
written exam it states: "This exam can be taken remotely from home or any location in the U.S." and names
"examroomAI" as the testing location to choose. For the practical exam it states: "This exam requires in-person
attendance and must be taken at an approved Utah Prov testing center."

## What if I am already licensed in another state or country?

DOPL's application page describes an endorsement path. Its endorsement section states: "If the state you are licensed
in shows an equivalent to the Utah license, request a Verification of Licensure" from that state's licensing
authority. If not, it states: "If the state you are licensed in shows no equivalent license to Utah, you will need to
meet the licensing requirements above."

The FAQ also covers Canadian Red Seal holders. It states that the "Construction Electrician" Red Seal "is considered
equivalent to the Utah Journeyman Electrician License", with the condition that applicants are "required to have
held the "Construction Electrician" Red Seal Certification for a minimum of one year prior to submitting their
application". It states: "Applicants must use the endorsement pathway to apply for the Journeyman Electrician License".
It adds: "The "Industrial
Electrician" Red Seal Certification is not recognized by the State of Utah" "as being equivalent to the Utah
Journeyman Electrician License."

## Where to check before you act

These are the requirements as shown on DOPL's electrical pages on the date above. DOPL is the authority, so confirm the
current hours, education rules, exams and forms on its site before you count hours, schedule an exam or apply,
especially the experience-hour figure for the apprenticeship route, which differed between two DOPL pages on that date.
Renewal and continuing education are on DOPL's separate continuing education page, which this post does not cover.
