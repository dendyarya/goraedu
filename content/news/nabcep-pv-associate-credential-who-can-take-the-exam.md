For solar PV certification, the North American Board of Certified Energy Practitioners (NABCEP) offers an entry
credential called the NABCEP Photovoltaic Associate (PVA). NABCEP is not a government agency. Its Associate Candidate
Handbook says: "NABCEP is a non-profit credentialing organization." This explainer answers one practical question from
NABCEP's own pages: what is the PV Associate credential, and who can take its exam? Nothing here describes a rule
change. (Our earlier posts cover the PV Installation Professional exam and its recertification, which are separate.)

## What is the PV Associate credential?

NABCEP's Associate Program page says the program "recognizes individuals who have demonstrated knowledge of the
fundamental principles" of the application, installation, operation and maintenance of PV or solar heating systems.
The handbook calls it NABCEP's "fundamentals credential in the areas of Photovoltaics, Solar Heating and Small Wind,"
aimed at people "at an early stage in their career, or those seeking to bring recognition to their practice."

The page adds: "Obtaining this credential is most often the first step toward a NABCEP Board Certification". The
Associate is not the same as a NABCEP Board Certification such as PV Installation Professional.

## Is it a license?

No. The handbook states: "NABCEP credentials are not professional licenses issued by a government agency" "and do not
authorize a credential holder to practice." It also says: "NABCEP Associates must comply with all legal requirements
related to practice, including licensing laws." Any license your state or city requires for solar or electrical work
is a separate matter.

## Who can take the PVA exam?

Section 1 of the handbook lists the eligibility requirements. An applicant must either:

- "Complete a course with an Associate Registered Training Provider – "Education Pathway"", or
- "Document at least six months of full time equivalent work experience related to photovoltaic, solar heating, or
  small wind technologies – "Experience Pathway""

and must also:

- "Be 18 years of age"
- "Sign and agree to uphold the NABCEP Code of Ethics and Standards of Conduct"
- "Pass a NABCEP Associate Examination"

The handbook also lists paying the applicable fees; this post does not restate amounts.

## How do the two pathways differ?

They differ in who registers you. On the education pathway, the Associate Program page says to "Complete a training
course with a NABCEP Associate Registered Training Provider" and then "pass the relevant NABCEP Associate
Examination." The provider handles the account: "The NABCEP Associate Registered Training Provider will create your
Candidate Account at the end of your course". The page warns: "DO NOT submit an application to NABCEP on you own."

On the experience pathway, the page says to "Document at least six months of full-time equivalent work in a job that
is directly involved" "with the technology you are choosing" and then pass the exam. Here you create your own account
and complete an Associate application.

## What about the old Entry Level certificate?

The handbook says: "NABCEP no longer supports the Entry Level Certificate of Knowledge." "Anyone holding an EL
Certificate is encouraged to convert their certificate to become a NABCEP Associate." The Associate Program page lists
an Entry Level Conversion for holders of NABCEP's Entry Level award for photovoltaics, and adds: "If the award was
earned more than three years ago, documentation of at least 12 hours of continuing education" "within the last three
years will also be required." The handbook instead says applicants "must document at least 12 hours of continuing education completed within the last
three years," without the three-year condition, so check the current page before you apply.

## What does the PVA exam cover?

The Associate Program page states: "As of April 7, 2026, the NABCEP Photovoltaic Associate Credential (PVA) will have
new content domains". It lists Safety, Core Knowledge, Sales & Economics, Design, Installation, and Operations &
Maintenance, and adds: "We should note that this new exam for our PVA Credential is similar to the previous version
from 2017." The handbook states: "The Exam is not an open book exam; no notes or reference material of any kind will
be allowed."

## How long does the credential last?

The handbook states: "NABCEP Associate credentials are valid for three (3) years following the date of issuance." It
adds that "NABCEP Associates are required to complete at least 12 hours of professional development every 3 years."
Without renewal, the handbook says Associates "will be required to reapply and retest to obtain the credential again."

The Associate Program page adds that employers can check status: "Only individuals with active NABCEP Associate credentials are listed in the Directory",
which "serves as a tool for employers to verify an individual's credential."

## Where to check before you act

These are NABCEP's rules as shown on its Associate Program page and its Associate Candidate Handbook v.2.3.5 on the
date above. The handbook carries no date (its file is stored in a 2023 folder on NABCEP's site) and does not mention the April 2026
exam update, so confirm the current version on NABCEP's site
