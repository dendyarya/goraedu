Some commercial drivers work for more than one motor carrier: a few days for one company, then a load for another, or
a driver furnished by another carrier. The driver qualification rules of the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations in 49 CFR Part 391 have two sections for
this. This explainer answers one practical question: when a driver drives for more than one carrier, what may the
second carrier skip, and what must it still do? It reads 49 CFR 391.63 and 391.65, with the definitions in 390.5, as
they stand in the eCFR (text current to November 23, 2026). It is an explainer, not a rule change.

Part 391 is about driver qualification, not the CDL itself. Under 49 CFR 391.1(a), its rules "establish minimum
qualifications for persons who drive commercial motor vehicles as, for, or on behalf of motor carriers" and "minimum
duties of motor carriers with respect to the qualifications of their drivers."

## Single-employer or multiple-employer: the 7-day test

Both sections depend on two definitions in 49 CFR 390.5:

- "Multiple-employer driver means a driver, who in any period of 7 consecutive days, is employed or used as a driver by
  more than one motor carrier."
- "Single-employer driver means a driver who, in any period of 7 consecutive days, is employed or used as a driver
  solely by a single motor carrier. This term includes a driver who operates a commercial motor vehicle on an
  intermittent, casual, or occasional basis."

So a driver who drives only now and then, but only for one carrier in any 7-day period, is a single-employer driver
under these definitions.

## Multiple-employer drivers (391.63)

49 CFR 391.63(a) says a motor carrier that employs a person as a multiple-employer driver "shall comply with all
requirements of this part, except that the motor carrier need not—"

1. "Require the person to furnish an application for employment in accordance with § 391.21;"
2. "Make the investigations and inquiries specified in § 391.23 with respect to that person;"
3. "Perform the annual driving record inquiry required by § 391.25(a); or"
4. "Perform the annual review of the person's driving record required by § 391.25(b)."

Apart from these four items, 391.63(a) still requires the carrier to "comply with all requirements of this part."

What the carrier must do instead is in 391.63(b): "Before a motor carrier permits a multiple-employer driver to drive a
commercial motor vehicle, the motor carrier must obtain the driver's name, the driver's social security number, and the
identification number, type, and issuing driver's licensing authority of the driver's commercial motor vehicle
operator's license. The motor carrier must maintain this information for three years after employment of the
multiple-employer driver ceases."

## Drivers furnished by another carrier (391.65)

The second route is for a driver furnished by another motor carrier for which the driver is a single-employer driver. Under 391.65(a), a motor carrier "may
employ a driver who is not a single-employer driver, as defined in § 390.5, of that motor carrier without complying with
the generally applicable driver qualification file requirements in this part," if the driver "is a single-employer
driver for another motor carrier" and that other carrier certifies in writing that the driver "is fully qualified to
drive a commercial motor vehicle."

The written statement must, among other things listed in 391.65(a)(2):

- be "signed and dated by an officer or authorized employee" of the carrier that employs the driver;
- contain "the driver's name and signature";
- certify that the driver "has been employed as a single-employer driver";
- certify that the driver "is fully qualified to drive a commercial motor vehicle under the rules in part 391";
- state "the expiration date of the driver's medical examiner's certificate";
- specify an expiration date for the certificate, "which shall be not longer than 2 years or, if earlier, the expiration
  date of the driver's current medical examiner's certificate."

It must also be "substantially in accordance with" the form printed in the rule.

The carrier that receives the certificate has two duties under 391.65(b): "Contact the motor carrier which certified the
driver's qualifications under this section to verify the validity of the certificate. This contact may be made in
person, by telephone, or by letter," and "Retain a copy of that certificate in its files for three years."

Under 391.65(c), the certifying carrier "shall be responsible for the accuracy of the certificate," and "The certificate
is no longer valid if the driver leaves the employment of the motor carrier which issued the certificate or is no longer
qualified under the rules in this part."

## What this means for CDL study

These sections are carrier duties under the driver qualification rules, and neither one mentions the CDL knowledge or
skills tests. The main qualification rules (who is qualified, medical certificates, qualification files) are in our
