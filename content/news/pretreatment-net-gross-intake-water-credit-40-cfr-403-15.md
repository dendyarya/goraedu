An industrial facility that sends wastewater to a publicly owned treatment works (POTW) may draw its process water from
a river, lake or well that already carries some of the pollutants its categorical pretreatment standard limits. Does
the facility have to meet the standard on everything in its discharge, or can it get credit for what was already in the
water it took in? EPA's General Pretreatment Regulations answer this in 40 CFR 403.15, "Net/Gross calculation." This
explainer for Wastewater Operator Certification study reads that section as it stands in the eCFR (source note: 70
FR 60198, Oct. 14, 2005). It does not describe a rule change.

## Who is who in 403.15?

Section 403.15 uses three defined terms from 40 CFR 403.3:

- **Industrial User:** "a source of Indirect Discharge" (403.3(j)). An Indirect Discharge is "the introduction of
  pollutants into a POTW from any non-domestic source regulated under section 307(b), (c) or (d) of the Act"
  (403.3(i)).
- **Control Authority:** "The POTW if the POTW's Pretreatment Program Submission has been approved in accordance with
  the requirements of § 403.11; or ... The Approval Authority if the Submission has not been approved" (403.3(f)).
- **POTW:** "a treatment works as defined by section 212 of the Act, which is owned by a State or municipality"
  (403.3(q), first words of the definition).

## Is the credit automatic?

No. 403.15(a) states that categorical Pretreatment Standards "may be adjusted to reflect the presence of pollutants in
the Industrial User's intake water in accordance with this section." The user has to ask: "Any Industrial User wishing
to obtain credit for intake pollutants must make application to the Control Authority."

On the user's request, "the applicable Standard will be calculated on a 'net' basis (i.e., adjusted to reflect credit
for pollutants in the intake water) if the requirements of paragraph (b) of this section are met." The section
itself defines "net" basis as the standard "adjusted to reflect credit for pollutants in the intake water"; it does not
define "gross."

## What must be true before credit is granted?

403.15(b) sets four criteria.

**1. Either the standard says so, or the treatment would work without the intake pollutants.** Under 403.15(b)(1),
either "(i) The applicable categorical Pretreatment Standards contained in 40 CFR subchapter N specifically provide that
they shall be applied on a net basis; or (ii) The Industrial User demonstrates that the control system it proposes or
uses to meet applicable categorical Pretreatment Standards would, if properly installed and operated, meet the
Standards in the absence of pollutants in the intake waters."

**2. Care with generic pollutants such as BOD, TSS and oil and grease.** 403.15(b)(2) states that credit for "generic
pollutants such as biochemical oxygen demand (BOD), total suspended solids (TSS), and oil and grease should not be
granted unless the Industrial User demonstrates that the constituents of the generic measure in the User's effluent are
substantially similar to the constituents of the generic measure in the intake water or unless appropriate additional
limits are placed on process water pollutants either at the outfall or elsewhere." Note the wording: "should not be
granted unless," with two ways to meet it.

**3. Only as much credit as needed, capped at the intake value.** 403.15(b)(3): "Credit shall be granted only to the
extent necessary to meet the applicable categorical Pretreatment Standard(s), up to a maximum value equal to the
influent value." The same paragraph adds: "Additional monitoring may be necessary to determine eligibility for credits
and compliance with Standard(s) adjusted under this section."

**4. Same body of water, unless waived.** 403.15(b)(4): "Credit shall be granted only if the User demonstrates that the
intake water is drawn from the same body of water as that into which the POTW discharges. The Control Authority may
waive this requirement if it finds that no environmental degradation will result."

## Whose body of water counts?

The POTW's. Paragraph (b)(4) compares the user's intake source with the water "into which the POTW discharges," and an
Industrial User's Indirect Discharge is, by definition, "the introduction of pollutants into a POTW" (403.3(i)).

## Summary

| Question                                 | What 403.15 says                                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Who applies, and to whom?                | The Industrial User, to the Control Authority (403.15(a))                                |
| Basic condition                          | The standard provides for net basis, or the user's control system would meet it without intake pollutants (403.15(b)(1)) |
| BOD, TSS, oil and grease                 | "should not be granted unless" constituents are substantially similar or additional limits apply (403.15(b)(2)) |
| How much credit?                         | Only what is needed, "up to a maximum value equal to the influent value" (403.15(b)(3))                |
| Source of intake water                   | Same body of water the POTW discharges into; the Control Authority may waive (403.15(b)(4))            |

This post covers only the federal text of 40 CFR 403.15 and the definitions it uses in 403.3. A State or POTW
pretreatment program may set its own procedures; check with the Control Authority that applies to the facility.
