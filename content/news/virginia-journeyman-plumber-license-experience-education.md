Plumbing licenses are issued by each state, and this explainer covers **Virginia only**, where tradesman licenses
are governed by the Board for Contractors' Individual License and Certification Regulations (18VAC50-30 of the Virginia
Administrative Code), in which "Department" means "the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation" (DPOR). It answers one practical
question from that regulation: what experience and education does Virginia require before you can sit for the
**Journeyman Plumber** exam? Nothing here describes a rule change, and nothing here applies to any other state.

## What does Virginia mean by "journeyman" and "plumber"?

The definitions section, 18VAC50-30-10, says a "Journeyman" is "a person who possesses the necessary ability,
proficiency, and qualifications to install, repair, and maintain specific types of materials and equipment utilizing a
working knowledge sufficient to comply with the pertinent provisions of the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code and
according to plans and specifications."
A "Plumber" is "a tradesman who performs the construction, repair, maintenance, alteration, or removal of plumbing
systems regulated under the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code."

## What experience and education qualify you for the journeyman exam?

Section 18VAC50-30-39 B states that "An applicant for examination to be licensed as a journeyman electrician, plumber,
or HVAC tradesman must furnish evidence that one of the following experience and education standards has been
attained". You need to meet **one** row of its table, not all of them:

| Education                                                                                                       | Practical experience in the trade |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| "Bachelor's degree in an engineering curriculum related to the trade"                                           | One year                          |
| "Associate degree or a certificate of completion from at least a two-year program in a tradesman-related field" | Two years                         |
| "240 hours of formal vocational training in the trade"                                                          | Four years                        |
| "160 hours of formal vocational training in the trade"                                                          | Five years                        |
| "80 hours of formal vocational training in the trade"                                                           | Six years                         |
| "40 hours of formal vocational training in the trade"                                                           | Seven years                       |
| "None"                                                                                                          | Eight years                       |

In each row the experience column reads "practical experience in the trade for which licensure is desired", so for a
journeyman plumber the years must be plumbing experience.

## What counts as "formal vocational training"?

18VAC50-30-10 defines it as "courses in the trade administered at an accredited educational facility; or formal
training, approved by the board, conducted by trade associations, businesses, the military, correspondence schools,
or other similar training organizations." Section 18VAC50-30-48 adds that "An applicant may receive credit for a maximum
of 40 hours for board-approved continuing education training toward formal vocational training required under this
part as applicable to the license or certification sought."

## How do you prove your hours and schooling?

Under 18VAC50-30-47 A, "Except as otherwise provided in this part, experience in the trade must be verified by reference
letters of experience from: building officials, building inspectors, current or former employers, contractors,
engineers, architects, or current or past clients attesting to the applicant's work in the trade." Under 18VAC50-30-47 B,
"Education may be verified by providing a copy of a transcript or diploma from the accredited institution."

## Does an apprenticeship let you skip the exam?

Yes, under one condition. 18VAC50-30-50 B states that "An individual who successfully completes an apprenticeship
program approved by the Commissioner of the Department of Workforce Development and Advancement as outlined in §
54.1-1131 A 3 of the Code of Virginia, as evidenced by providing a certificate of completion or other official
document, is exempt from the journeyman examination."

## What about the exam itself?

18VAC50-30-30 (item 3) says that where an exam is required, the applicant "must follow all rules established by the board with regard to conduct at the
examination," and that failing to follow the rules of the board and the testing organization "shall be grounds for
denial of application." The sections read for this post do not describe the exam's topics or format.

## How does a master plumber license build on the journeyman license?

18VAC50-30-39 D states that "An applicant for examination to be licensed as a master in a trade must furnish evidence
that one of the following experience standards has been attained":

1. "Having held a valid residential HVAC tradesman or residential plumber tradesman license in the trade for which
   licensure is desired for three years;"
2. "Having held a valid journeyman license in the trade for which licensure is desired for a minimum of one year; or"
3. "Nine years of practical experience in the trade for which licensure is desired."

So holding a journeyman plumber license for at least one year is one of three routes to the master exam, not the only
one.
