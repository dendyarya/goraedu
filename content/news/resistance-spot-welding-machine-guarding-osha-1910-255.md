For welding certification topics, this post looks at resistance welding machines. In general industry, spot, seam,
projection and flash welding machines are covered by 29 CFR 1910.255, "Resistance welding," in Subpart Q of OSHA's
general industry standards. The section also points to other OSHA rules (Subpart S, §§ 1910.217, 1910.219 and
1910.252). This explainer answers one practical question from that
section: how must these machines be guarded? It reads the section as it stands in the eCFR (text current to November
16, 2026). Nothing here describes a rule change, and it does not cover construction work under Part 1926.

## Who may run the machine?

1910.255(a)(3) states: "Workmen designated to operate resistance welding equipment shall have been properly
instructed and judged competent to operate such equipment." The section does not set a training length or name a
certificate.

## How must the point of operation be guarded?

For nonportable spot and seam welding machines, 1910.255(b)(4) states: "All press welding machine operations, where
there is a possibility of the operator's fingers being under the point of operation, shall be effectively guarded by
the use of a device such as an electronic eye safety circuit, two hand controls or protection similar to that
prescribed for punch press operation, § 1910.217 of this part."

The same paragraph covers moving parts: "All chains, gears, operating bus linkage, and belts shall be protected by
adequate guards, in accordance with § 1910.219 of this part."

Clamps are covered for all resistance welding equipment by 1910.255(a)(4): "Controls of all automatic or air and
hydraulic clamps shall be arranged or guarded to prevent the operator from accidentally activating them."

## What about foot switches, stop buttons and safety pins?

1910.255(b) adds three rules for nonportable spot and seam welding machines:

- Foot switches: "All foot switches shall be guarded to prevent accidental operation of the machine." (b)(6)
- Stop buttons: "Two or more safety emergency stop buttons shall be provided on all special multispot welding
  machines, including 2-post and 4-post weld presses." (b)(7)
- Safety pins: "On large machines, four safety pins with plugs and receptacles (one in each corner) shall be provided
  so that when safety pins are removed and inserted in the ram or platen, the press becomes inoperative." (b)(8)

## How are workers and passers-by protected from sparks?

For nonportable spot and seam welding machines, 1910.255(b)(5) states: "The hazard of flying sparks shall be, wherever practical, eliminated by installing a shield
guard of safety glass or suitable fire-resistant plastic at the point of operation. Additional shields or curtains
shall be installed as necessary to protect passing persons from flying sparks."

Flash welding has its own rule in 1910.255(d): "Flash welding machines shall be equipped with a hood to control
flying flash." Fire-resistant curtains or suitable shields must also be set up around the machine to protect the
operators of nearby equipment, "in such a manner that the operators movements are not hampered" (1910.255(d)(2)).

## How must doors and access panels be secured?

1910.255(b)(3) states: "All doors and access panels of all resistance welding machines and control panels shall be
kept locked and interlocked to prevent access, by unauthorized persons, to live portions of the equipment."

For stored energy or capacitor discharge equipment and control panels involving high voltage (over 550 volts),
1910.255(b)(2) requires suitable insulation and complete enclosures whose doors have interlocks and contacts wired
into the control circuit; these must "effectively interrupt power and short circuit all capacitors when the door or
panel is open." A manually operated switch or suitable positive device must also be installed "as an added safety
measure assuring absolute discharge of all capacitors."

## What about portable welding guns?

Portable welding guns have their own rules in 1910.255(c):

- "All portable welding guns shall have suitable counterbalanced devices for supporting the guns, including cables,
  unless the design of the gun or fixture makes counterbalancing impractical or unnecessary." (c)(1)
- Portable welding guns, transformers and related equipment suspended from overhead structures, eye beams,
  trolleys, etc. "shall be equipped with safety chains or cables," and these
  "shall be capable of supporting the total shock load in the event of failure of any component of the supporting
  system." (c)(2)
- Initiating switches on the gun must have "suitable guards capable of preventing accidental initiation through
  contact with fixturing, operator's clothing, etc." (c)(4)
- "The movable holder, where it enters the gun frame, shall have sufficient clearance to prevent the shearing of
  fingers carelessly placed on the operating movable holder." (c)(5)

## What if the operator finds a defect?

1910.255(e) states: "The operator shall be instructed to report any equipment defects to his supervisor and the use
of the equipment shall be discontinued until safety repairs have been completed." The same paragraph requires periodic
inspection by qualified maintenance personnel and a certification record.
