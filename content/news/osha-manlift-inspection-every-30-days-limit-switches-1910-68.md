Belt manlifts carry people from floor to floor on a moving belt. OSHA's general industry rules cover it in 29 CFR 1910.68, "Manlifts," in Subpart F. This explainer
answers one practical question: how often must a manlift be inspected, and what goes in the record? It reads the
section in the eCFR (text current to November 24, 2026). It is an explainer, not a rule change.

## What counts as a manlift?

1910.68(a)(5) defines it as "A device consisting of a power-driven endless belt moving in one direction only, and
provided with steps or platforms and handholds attached to it for the transportation of personnel from floor to floor."

Under 1910.68(b)(1), "It is not intended that this section cover moving stairways, elevators with enclosed platforms (“Paternoster”
elevators), gravity lifts, nor conveyors used only for conveying material." It "applies to manlifts used to carry only
personnel trained and authorized by the employer in their use."

## How often is the inspection?

1910.68(e)(1) states: "All manlifts shall be inspected by a competent designated person at intervals of not more than 30
days. Limit switches shall be checked weekly. Manlifts found to be unsafe shall not be operated until properly
repaired."

So there are two intervals: the whole manlift at least every 30 days, and the limit switches every week. 1910.68(a)(4)
defines a limit switch as a device "the purpose of which is to cut off the power to the motor and apply the brake to stop
the carrier in the event that a loaded step passes the terminal landing." The two automatic "Up limit stops" in
1910.68(c)(5)(i) must "cut off the power and apply the brake when a loaded step passes the upper terminal landing."

## What must the inspection cover?

Under 1910.68(e)(2), the inspection "shall cover but is not limited to" a list of items. The list includes steps and
step fastenings, rails and their supports, rollers and slides, belt and belt tension, handholds, floor landings,
guardrails, lubrication, limit switches, warning signs and lights, illumination, the drive and bottom (boot) pulleys,
the motor, driving mechanism and brake, electrical switches, vibration and misalignment, and "“Skip” on up or down run
when mounting step (indicating worn gears)."

## What record is required?

1910.68(e)(3) states: "A certification record shall be kept of each inspection which includes the date of the
inspection, the signature of the person who performed the inspection and the serial number, or other identifier, of the
manlift which was inspected. This record of inspection shall be made available to the Assistant Secretary of Labor or a
duly authorized representative."

The section names those three items. It does not set how long the record must be kept.

## Can a manlift carry freight or materials?

Not the items the rule lists. 1910.68(d)(1) states: "No freight, packaged goods, pipe, lumber, or construction materials of
any kind shall be handled on any manlift."

## Which signs and stops must be there?

Among the section's equipment rules:

- **Instructions:** signs "giving instructions for the use of the manlift shall be posted at each landing or stenciled
  on the belt" (1910.68(c)(7)(i)), reading approximately (1910.68(c)(7)(i)(b)) "Face the Belt. Use the Handholds. To Stop—Pull Rope."
- **Top floor:** an illuminated sign reading "TOP FLOOR—GET OFF" (1910.68(c)(7)(ii)(a)), plus "a red warning light of
  not less than 40- watt rating" below the upper landing (1910.68(c)(7)(ii)(b)).
- **Visitors:** "AUTHORIZED PERSONNEL ONLY" at each landing (1910.68(c)(7)(iii)).
- **Emergency stop:** "An emergency stop means shall be provided" within easy reach of both runs
  (1910.68(c)(6)(i) and (ii)).
- **Reset:** after an automatic stop, "it shall be necessary to reset the automatic stop manually," and "It shall not be
  possible to reset the device from any step or platform" (1910.68(c)(5)(ii)).
- **Torn belts:** "A belt that has become torn while in use on a manlift shall not be spliced and put back in service"
  (1910.68(c)(1)(ii)(c)).

The section also sets design numbers (floor openings, clearances, step spacing, speed) that are not covered here.
