OSHA's general industry standards cover vehicle-mounted aerial lifts in 29 CFR 1910.67, "Vehicle-mounted elevating and
rotating work platforms." This explainer answers two practical questions from that section: who may operate an aerial
lift, and what does the rule require of the people working from one? It reads the section as it stands in the eCFR and
does not describe a rule change.

## What counts as an aerial lift

Paragraph (a)(1) defines an aerial device as "Any vehicle—mounted device, telescoping or articulating, or both, which is
used to position personnel." The platform is "Any personnel-carrying device (basket or bucket) which is a component of
an aerial device" (paragraph (a)(7)), and a vehicle is "Any carrier that is not manually propelled" (paragraph (a)(8)).

Paragraph (b)(1) lists the types covered: "(i) Extensible boom platforms, (ii) aerial ladders, (iii) articulating boom
platforms, (iv) vertical towers, and (v) a combination of any of the above." The same paragraph adds that aerial
equipment "may be powered or manually operated; and are deemed to be aerial lifts whether or not they are capable of
rotating about a substantially vertical axis."

Two limits on scope are in paragraph (b)(3): "The requirements of this section do not apply to firefighting equipment
or to the vehicles upon which aerial devices are mounted, except with respect to the requirement that a vehicle be a
stable support for the aerial device."

## Who may operate one

Paragraph (c)(2)(ii) is one sentence: "Only trained persons shall operate an aerial lift." It sits in paragraph (c)(2),
"Extensible and articulating boom platforms," so in 1910.67 it applies to those platforms. The section's only specific
rule for ladder trucks and tower trucks, paragraph (c)(1), covers securing the ladder before highway travel. Section
1910.67 does not list what the training must cover or how long it
lasts.

## Checks before and during use

Every rule from here to the section on insulated lifts is in paragraph (c)(2), which applies to extensible and
articulating boom platforms. Paragraph (c)(2) also requires:

- **Daily control test.** "Lift controls shall be tested each day prior to use to determine that such controls are in
  safe working condition" (paragraph (c)(2)(i)).
- **Load limits.** "Boom and basket load limits specified by the manufacturer shall not be exceeded" (paragraph
  (c)(2)(vi)).
- **Brakes, outriggers and chocks.** "The brakes shall be set and outriggers, when used, shall be positioned on pads or
  a solid surface. Wheel chocks shall be installed before using an aerial lift on an incline" (paragraph (c)(2)(vii)).

## How workers in the basket must be protected

- **Fall protection.** "A personal fall arrest or travel restraint system that meets the requirements in subpart I of
  this part shall be worn and attached to the boom or basket when working from an aerial lift" (paragraph (c)(2)(v)).
- **No belting off to something else.** "Belting off to an adjacent pole, structure, or equipment while working from an
  aerial lift shall not be permitted" (paragraph (c)(2)(iii)).
- **Feet on the floor.** "Employees shall always stand firmly on the floor of the basket, and shall not sit or climb on
  the edge of the basket or use planks, ladders, or other devices for a work position" (paragraph (c)(2)(iv)).
- **No climbers.** "Climbers shall not be worn while performing work from an aerial lift" (paragraph (c)(2)(x)).

## Upper and lower controls

Paragraph (c)(2)(ix) says articulating boom and extensible boom platforms "primarily designed as personnel carriers,
shall have both platform (upper) and lower controls." It adds: "Upper controls shall be in or beside the platform within easy reach of the operator." And "Lower controls shall provide for overriding the upper controls." The paragraph ends: "Lower
level controls shall not be operated unless permission has been obtained from the employee in the lift, except in case
of emergency."

## Moving the truck

- With people aloft: "An aerial lift truck may not be moved when the boom is elevated in a working position with men in
  the basket, except for equipment which is specifically designed for this type of operation in accordance with the
  provisions of paragraphs (b)(1) and (b)(2) of this section" (paragraph (c)(2)(viii)).
- Before travel: "Before moving an aerial lift for travel, the boom(s) shall be inspected to see that it is properly
  cradled and outriggers are in stowed position, except as provided in paragraph (c)(2)(viii) of this section"
  (paragraph (c)(2)(xii)).

## Insulated lifts and power lines

Paragraph (c)(2)(xi) states: "The insulated portion of an aerial lift shall not be altered in any manner that might
reduce its insulating value." For work near power lines, paragraph (b)(4) points elsewhere: "For operations near
overhead electric lines, see § 1910.333(c)(3)."
