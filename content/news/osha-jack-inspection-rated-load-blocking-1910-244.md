OSHA's general industry standards cover jacks in one short paragraph: 29 CFR 1910.244(a), in Subpart P (hand and
portable powered tools and other hand-held equipment). This explainer answers one practical question from that text as
it stands in the eCFR (version of November 16, 2026): what does OSHA require for a jack, and how often must it be
inspected? Nothing here describes a rule change.

## What rating and marking does a jack need?

Two rules come first, under "Loading and marking":

- 1910.244(a)(1)(i): "The operator shall make sure that the jack used has a rating sufficient to lift and sustain the
  load."
- 1910.244(a)(1)(ii): "The rated load shall be legibly and permanently marked in a prominent location on the jack by
  casting, stamping, or other suitable means."

The first rule puts the check on the operator, and it covers both lifting and sustaining the load, not only raising it.
The second rule is about the jack itself: the rating has to be on the jack, legible and permanent.

## When must the base or the cap be blocked?

1910.244(a)(2)(i) states: "In the absence of a firm foundation, the base of the jack shall be blocked. If there is a
possibility of slippage of the cap, a block shall be placed in between the cap and the load."

Both rules are conditional. Blocking under the base is required when there is no firm foundation; a block between the
cap and the load is required when the cap could slip.

## How far can a jack be raised?

The stop indicator sets the limit. 1910.244(a)(2)(ii) states: "The operator shall watch the stop indicator, which shall
be kept clean, in order to determine the limit of travel. The indicated limit shall not be overrun."

## What must happen once the load is up?

1910.244(a)(2)(iii) requires the raised load to be secured as well. It states: "After the load has been raised, it shall be
cribbed, blocked, or otherwise secured at once."

Note the words "at once": the rule requires the load to be secured immediately after it is raised.

## What maintenance does the rule require?

Two maintenance rules apply to the jack itself:

- 1910.244(a)(2)(iv): "Hydraulic jacks exposed to freezing temperatures shall be supplied with an adequate antifreeze
  liquid."
- 1910.244(a)(2)(v): "All jacks shall be properly lubricated at regular intervals."

The antifreeze rule is limited to hydraulic jacks exposed to freezing temperatures. The lubrication rule applies to all
jacks; the text does not set a number of days or hours for "regular intervals."

## How often must a jack be inspected?

This is the part of 1910.244 with numbers in it. 1910.244(a)(2)(vi) states: "Each jack shall be thoroughly inspected at
times which depend upon the service conditions. Inspections shall be not less frequent than the following:"

- "For constant or intermittent use at one locality, once every 6 months,"
- "For jacks sent out of shop for special work, when sent out and when returned,"
- "For a jack subjected to abnormal load or shock, immediately before and immediately thereafter."

So the schedule depends on how the jack is used. A jack in constant or intermittent use at one locality is inspected at least every 6 months.
A jack that leaves the shop for special work is inspected when it goes out and again when it comes back. A jack that
takes an abnormal load or shock is inspected immediately before and immediately after. These are minimums ("not less
frequent than"); the first sentence ties the actual timing to the service conditions.

## What about repairs and jacks that are out of order?

Two final rules close the jack paragraph:

- 1910.244(a)(2)(vii): "Repair or replacement parts shall be examined for possible defects."
- 1910.244(a)(2)(viii): "Jacks which are out of order shall be tagged accordingly, and shall not be used until repairs
  are made."

The tag rule has two parts: the jack is tagged as out of order, and it stays out of use until it is repaired.

## What else is in 1910.244?

The same section has one more paragraph, (b), on abrasive blast cleaning nozzles: "The blast cleaning nozzles shall be
equipped with an operating valve which must be held open manually. A support shall be provided on which the nozzle may
be mounted when it is not in use." 1910.244 has no other paragraphs.

## What this means for exam study

(legibly and permanently), when blocking is required (no firm foundation; possible cap slippage), when the raised load
is secured (at once), and the three minimum inspection triggers (every 6 months at one locality; out of and back into
the shop; before and after abnormal load or shock). For the neighboring Subpart P rules on hand and portable powered
