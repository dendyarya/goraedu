The North American Board of Certified Energy Practitioners (NABCEP) runs board certifications for solar PV work.
NABCEP is a private certifying body, not a government agency, and goraedu is independent of it. This explainer answers
one practical question from NABCEP's Certification Handbook ("2025 Update"): how do you ask for special testing
conditions, and what can you do if you disagree with a decision or an exam result? Nothing here describes a rule
change.

## Who can ask for special testing accommodations?

Section 16.2 ("Special Testing Accommodations") states that NABCEP "complies with the provisions of the Americans with
Disabilities Act" and "with Title VII of the Civil Rights Act". It covers two reasons for a request: "All Candidates who
desire special testing accommodations due to a disability or religious observance must make this request at the time
of exam scheduling."

## How and when do you make the request?

The timing is set at scheduling, not later. Section 16.2 puts the paperwork on the candidate: "It is the responsibility
of the Candidate to provide advance notice and appropriate documentation of the disability or religious observance,
along with specifications of the type of accommodations or aids that will be required, when the application
registration workflow is submitted."

NABCEP answers before test day: it "shall notify the Candidate regarding the acceptance of the
request in advance of the Examination date."

## Who pays, and are there limits?

Section 16.2 splits the costs. NABCEP "will not pay any costs an individual may incur in obtaining the required
diagnosis and recommendation for accommodation." However, "NABCEP will pay for any reasonable accommodation(s) that are
provided during the exam."

There is one firm limit. Arrangements "shall be made for all Candidates with disabilities who file the appropriate
request and include appropriate documentation, unless an accommodation changes the nature of the test." The same
section repeats it: "Under no conditions will an accommodation be made which changes the nature of the test."

## Can you flag a question you think is wrong?

Yes, right after the exam. Section 18.6 ("Comment Prior to Scoring") states that "Candidates may comment in writing on
any questions they believe contain errors". Comment forms "will be available in the examination room at the conclusion
of the exam," and they go to NABCEP's Examination Committee. If that committee "judges that an item(s) should not be
scored", the handbook says the affected candidates' scores are corrected.

## Can you challenge your score?

Section 19.7 ("How to Challenge Examination Results") starts with a caution: "Due to the accuracy of electronically-
scanned scoring, changes as a result of re-scoring are highly unlikely." Even so, "a Candidate may request a hand-score
of their answer sheets" using NABCEP's request form, and a hand grading fee applies (the handbook points to NABCEP's
website for fees; this post lists no amounts). "All such requests must be sent to NABCEP as directed in the form."

After the hand-graded score has been provided, Section 19.7 continues: "Candidates who believe their score is inaccurate
can send an official appeal to the NABCEP President & CEO within thirty (30) days of receiving their score report."

## What decisions can be appealed?

Section 23.1 ("Grounds for Appeal") says the appeal process "is the only way to resolve all NABCEP application,
eligibility, examination, and other certification or recertification challenges, complaints, and/or claims of
irregularities." An applicant, candidate or certificant may appeal "an adverse NABCEP action or decision" on these
grounds:

- "The Candidate or Certificant was found to be ineligible to take or unable to complete the Certification Examination;"
- "The Candidate did not pass or successfully complete the Certification Examination; or,"
- the applicant "failed to satisfy a certification requirement or recertification requirement", including those on
  "qualifications, education, and experience".

The deadline is short: "the written request must be received by NABCEP within thirty (30) days of the date of the
adverse action."

## What goes into an appeal?

Section 23.2 ("Appeal Procedures") lists what the Certification Appeals Committee needs. The appeal must contain "The
identity and signature" of the person appealing; "A detailed explanation of; the reasons and basis for the appeal";
"All objections, corrections, and factual information the Candidate or Certificant believes to be relevant"; contact
details of "any persons with factual information relevant to the appeal"; and "Copies of any and all relevant
documents, exhibits, or other information" offered in support.

Complaints that are not appeals of a decision fall under a separate policy. Section 23.3 says the "Complaint, Dispute,
and Grievance Resolution Policy (available online or by contacting NABCEP)" applies "to any grievance matter concerning
an action, policy or practice of NABCEP as a corporate body" and to grievances involving NABCEP candidates, certificants, board members, staff, committee members and volunteers.

## What if you simply want to try again?

An appeal is not the only route after a failed exam. Section 19.8 says that "All Candidates, except System Inspectors,
who fail the examination will be permitted to retake the examination during the 12-month period during which the
Candidate's application is "accepted"". A candidate who wishes to retake the exam "may do so fourteen (14) days after their failed exam." System Inspector
candidates follow a different path, as the handbook notes.
