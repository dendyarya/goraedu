Plumbing licenses are issued by each state, and this explainer covers **Massachusetts only**, where plumbers are
licensed by the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters. It answers one practical question from the
board's own regulation, 248 CMR 11.00 ("Education and Experience Standards and Requirements for Licensure"), in the
version printed in Mass. Register #1559 (10/24/2025): what hours and schooling lead to a Massachusetts **journeyman
plumber** license? Nothing here describes a rule change, and nothing here applies to any other state.

## Who can sit for the journeyman exam?

248 CMR 11.02(1)(a) starts with the apprentice license. It applies to "An applicant who is a licensed apprentice plumber
in accordance with M.G.L. c. 142, § 3A", who files a Board-approved application and meets the experience and education
criteria below.

## How many work hours are required?

Under 11.02(1)(a)1, the experience must total "no less than 6,800 clock hours as a licensed apprentice". The work is
done "under the direct supervision of a Commonwealth of Massachusetts licensed master plumber" or of a licensed
journeyman plumber employed by a master plumber.

The same paragraph excludes experience installing HVAC components for heating systems, steam piping and hydronic heat
piping from the count. It also states that "the Board shall have discretion to grant partial credit for relevant
experience in a related trade".

Hours count only while licensed. 248 CMR 11.02(8) states that the Board "shall not grant any work experience credit to
an individual for any period in which they did not hold a valid license" issued by the Board, with an exception for work
performed under a Public Comprehensive High School Career/Vocational Technical School Program.

## How much schooling is required?

248 CMR 11.02(1)(a)2 asks for two things:

- "documentary proof of having received a high school diploma or the equivalent"; and
- that the applicant has "successfully completed 550 clock hours of plumbing and gas fitting theory", ending in an exam
  given by the school or instructor.

The theory hours are spread out: they take place "over a period of no less than four years", and "For each calendar
year, no more than 165 hours of education may be credited for licensure purposes." The regulation also sets a start
date: "Apprentices shall begin their education program within nine months of the initial issuance of their apprentice
license."

## How do the work hours and school years fit together?

11.02(1)(a)3 links them: an apprentice "must accrue 1,700 clock hours of qualifying work experience as a licensed
apprentice" for each year of 165 education hours, subject to listed exceptions. One exception lets an apprentice
complete "all 220 hours of Tier 1 and Tier 2 in a single calendar year", and another lets an unlicensed person complete
Tier 1 of the education before licensure. The regulation adds: "The Board may permit additional exceptions from these
restrictions for good cause shown."

## Are there other routes to credit?

248 CMR 11.02(1)(a)4 covers students in a Board-approved public career and technical (CVTE) high school program. They
may be credited with "a maximum of the first three tiers, comprising 330 hours of educational theory credit from that
program", and may accrue "a maximum of 1,700 hours of experience credit" while enrolled, under the conditions listed in
that paragraph.

Under 11.02(1)(d), licensed journeyman or master gas fitters may petition to "utilize up to 220 hours of gas fitting
education and 3,400 hours of gas fitting work experience" toward a journeyman plumber license.

## What does the exam require?

Under 248 CMR 11.03(3)(a), the applicant must "Receive a score of no less than 70% on each portion of the examination".
A failed portion is retaken within the time limit in 11.03(3)(b), "within one year from date of failure." Under
11.02(1)(b), an applicant "shall not be permitted to attempt the journeyman license examination more than six times
without special Board approval".

## Does the apprentice license last forever?

No. Under 11.02(1)(c), an apprentice license "shall not be renewed (nor shall the licensee be eligible for a new
license)" after ten years from the date stated in that paragraph, unless the Board reinstates it on a petition for good
cause.

## What comes after the journeyman license?

The master plumber license. Under 11.02(2)(a), a "licensed journeyman plumber" needs "no less than one year practical
experience totaling a minimum of 1,700 clock hours" as a Massachusetts journeyman plumber, plus "the 110 hour tier five
of the five tier Plumber Educational Program". The regulation adds: "Individuals who completed this education as an
apprentice shall not be required to complete any additional education."

## Where to check before you act

These are the requirements as printed in 248 CMR 11.00 on the date above. The Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and
Gas Fitters is the authority, so confirm the current regulation, forms and approved schools on mass.gov before you
count hours, enroll or apply. These rules describe Massachusetts only; other states set their own. For other plumbing
