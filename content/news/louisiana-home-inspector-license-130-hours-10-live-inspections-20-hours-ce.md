This explainer covers **Louisiana only**, where home inspectors are licensed by the state's Board of Home Inspectors.
It answers one question from the state law, R.S. 37:1477, and the board's rules in the Louisiana Administrative Code,
Title 46, Part XL: what does it take to get and keep a home inspector license? Nothing here describes a rule change or
applies to any other state.

## Who can apply?

R.S. 37:1477(C) lists six requirements for "An applicant for licensure as a home inspector": "Attainment of eighteen
years of age", "Successful completion of high school or its equivalent", "Passage of the required examination",
payment of the fees, "Submission of a license application as prescribed by the board", and "Submission of a criminal
background request form to the office of state police."

Under the rules, §119.E, "Before registering for the 90 hours of course work with a
certified pre-licensing educational provider," the trainee "must first apply with the board."

## How many hours of training are required?

R.S. 37:1477(D) asks a first-time applicant for "at least one hundred twenty hours of
instruction", of which "At least thirty, but not more than forty, of the required hours shall be obtained in course
work containing actual practical home inspections."

Section 119.B of the rules requires "at least 130 hours of required home inspection instruction and
training" from board-approved providers. Section 119.C splits the 130 hours into three parts:

- "90 hours of home inspection course work approved by the board and taught by a certified pre-licensing education
  provider";
- "a minimum of 30 hours of in-field platform training from a certified infield trainer";
- "a minimum of 10 hours of instruction and training from a certified infield trainer, which shall consist of attending
  10 live home inspections at a residential structure where a fee is paid and a report is provided to a client."

Under §119.D.3, the trainee must have "completed the 90 hours of course work within 180 days of commencement" and
"passed, with a grade of 70 percent or higher, the final examination and all periodic examinations" given by the
provider.

## Which exams must an applicant pass?

Section 119.A says applicants "must pass a board-approved licensing examination covering home inspection methods and
techniques, the standards of practice set forth in §301 et seq., and code of ethics set forth in §501." On top of the
130 hours, §119.C.4 says the applicant "shall also attend the report writing seminar conducted on behalf of the board"
and "pass the board-approved examination of the standards of practice and Code of Ethics."

The texts used here do not name the licensing exam, state its passing score or say how long a passing result stays
valid.

## What insurance is required?

Section 127.A states: "All active, licensed home inspectors shall carry errors and omissions insurance as well as
general liability insurance." Section 127.C sets a minimum amount for each policy. If a policy is cancelled and "a gap in coverage may occur," §127.E says the licensee "shall immediately inform the board and
cease performing home inspections." Section 127.F adds: "Failure to maintain insurance is grounds for license
revocation, non-renewal or other disciplinary action."

## How often is the license renewed, and how much continuing education is needed?

Section 115.C states: "Licenses shall be renewed on an annual basis." Renewal forms "must be received at least two weeks
prior to the expiration date of the current license." Under §115.B, the licensee "shall submit a copy of a completed
inspection report form" from the previous licensing period.

Continuing education is set by §121:

- A licensee "(not renewing a license for the first time) must certify completion of at least 20 hours of continuing
  education during the previous licensing period, in courses approved by the board." "No more than 10 hours of live and
  or interactive live streaming continuing education credit may be carried over into the following year."
- "a first year inspector, renewing a license for the first time, must certify completion of at least 30 hours of
  continuing education during the previous licensing period."
- "All licensees must attend a board-approved report writing and standards of practice seminar at least once every
  three years."

## Can a license be inactive?

Yes. Under §115.E, "A licensee may hold inactive status by maintaining license renewals and continuing education
requirements." "All insurance requirements for inactive licensees are waived", but "Licensees holding inactive status
shall not perform home inspections."

## What records must an inspector keep?

Section 125.C states: "Records shall be kept for three years from the day the inspection report was provided to the
client." A report "questioned by the board or any legal entity shall be retained for a period of five years from the
date the inquiry was received by the licensee."
