The Certified Radiographic Interpreter (CRI) credential is run by the American Welding Society (AWS). AWS is a private
certifying body, not a government agency, and this explainer uses only AWS's own CRI program page for facts about its
own program. It answers one practical question for welding certification: once you hold a CRI, how long is it valid,
and what does AWS ask for to keep it? Nothing here describes a rule change. Whether an employer, code or contract
requires a CRI is outside this post.

## What is a CRI?

AWS's page states: "A CRI is a professional trained to read radiographic (X-ray) images of welds and materials." The
page adds that their job "is to spot discontinuities and determine if a weld meets applicable code and quality
standards." It is a separate credential from the Certified Welding Inspector (CWI).

## Who can earn it in the first place?

Renewal builds on the original requirements, so here they are briefly, in AWS's words:

- Education: "You need at least a high school diploma or a GED equivalent."
- Training: "You must complete a minimum of 40 hours of formal training in radiographic interpretation/examination".
- Experience: "You must have at least a year's experience in radiographic interpretation", "either certified by a
  company or nationally, or under the direct supervision of someone who is." AWS adds: "Sometimes, up to six months of
  other nondestructive evaluation (NDE) experience or education can count towards this."
- Vision: an eye exam "showing you can read Jaeger Number 2 Letters" "at a distance of at least twelve inches with one
  eye."

The page lists three exams for new candidates: a General Knowledge exam, a Code Knowledge exam and a Practical (Film
Interpretation) exam.

## How long is a CRI valid?

Three years. AWS's page states: "Your CRI certification lasts three years and can be renewed twice", "first at the
three-year mark, and again at six years." The page's FAQ says the same in short form: "Certification is valid for three
years."

## What does each renewal require?

AWS sums up renewal this way: "you must show ongoing involvement in radiographic interpretation, pass the current
Practical Exam, and have a valid recent eye exam." Its renewal list names the three items in these words:

- "Written documentation to support evidence of continued activity in the occupational or teaching position"
- "Achievement of a passing score on the current Practical (Film Interpretation) exam"
- "Evidence of a current valid vision examination."

So under the program page, a renewal is not paperwork alone: the holder documents continued work or teaching in
radiographic interpretation and also passes the current Practical exam.

The eye exam has a timing rule. AWS says the vision test "should be taken within seven months before your scheduled
exam or renewal."

The FAQ is more general, saying CRIs "must meet recertification requirements to maintain credentials" and that
these "may include re-examination or verification of continued professional experience." The renewal section above is
the more specific statement; if the two seem to differ for your case, ask AWS.

## What happens at nine years?

The two renewals take a CRI to nine years. After that, the page says: "To maintain CRI credentials after two (2)
consecutive renewals (a total of nine years of continuous certification), the CRI certificate holder must take the
Practical (Film Interpretation) exam and pass with the minimum percentage requirements as defined in AWS QC15:2008,
Specification for the Certification of Radiographic Interpreters."

The page does not state those minimum percentages in its recertification section, and it names the governing document
in two ways ("AWS QC15:2008" there and "AWS QC15-AMD1" in its list of governing documents). Check with AWS which edition
applies when you recertify.

## What about CWI holders?

The CRI page describes only the CRI's own renewal schedule. How AWS's CWI rules treat other credentials at the CWI's
nine-year recertification is set on the CWI program's own pages; see our earlier post on
[CWI renewal and nine-year recertification](/news/aws-certified-welding-inspector-cwi-renewal-nine-year-recertification/).

## What this post does not cover

The CRI is AWS's private credential for reading radiographs. It is not a license from any government agency, and this
post says nothing about the federal rules for people who operate radiography sources; those are on our
[industrial radiography page](/industrial-radiography/). OSHA's welding safety rules are covered in our
[OSHA general industry welding study guide](/osha-general-industry/welding/study-guide/) and
[OSHA construction welding and cutting study guide](/osha/welding-cutting/study-guide/). This post makes no claim that
OSHA requires a CRI.
