This explainer covers **Indiana only**, where home inspectors are licensed by the Home Inspectors Licensing Board
through the Indiana Professional Licensing Agency (PLA). It answers one practical question from the board's own pages:
what does it take to get and keep an Indiana home inspector license? It describes those pages as published on the date
above. Nothing here describes a rule change, and nothing here applies to any other state.

## Do you need a license to inspect homes in Indiana?

Yes, if you are paid for it. Indiana Code 25-20.2-5-1, as printed in the board's compilation of laws and rules, says
no one may perform a home inspection for compensation without a license unless exempt. IC 25-20.2-1-1(b) lists the
exemptions. They include state or local code enforcement officials acting within the scope of their employment, and
registered architects, registered professional engineers and licensed plumbing contractors or journeyman plumbers acting
within the scope of their own registration or license, among others.

## What are the requirements for a new license?

IC 25-20.2-5-2(a) sets the qualifications, which include that the applicant must be at least 18 years old, hold a high school diploma or
an Indiana equivalent, complete a board-approved program or course on home inspections and report writing, pass an
examination the board prescribes or approves, and submit a certificate of insurance or other evidence of financial
responsibility acceptable to the board. The PLA's licensing page lists the
documents:

- **Education.** A high school diploma or GED and "a board approved training course", shown with the pre-licensure
  certificate. The application instructions say the course must be at least 60 hours, with at least 40 classroom hours
  and 12 practical hours, and must cover nine subject areas, including Indiana licensure law and report writing.
- **Exam.** The National Home Inspector Examination, shown with the score report.
- **Insurance.** General liability insurance of at least $100,000 from an insurer authorized in Indiana, naming the
  state as an additional insured and stating that cancellation or nonrenewal does not take effect until the board
  receives at least 10 days' prior written notice.
- **Other states.** Applicants who hold or have held a license in another state, territory or country need verification
  from each of them. A copy of the license is not enough, and the application instructions say the other regulator
  returns the verification directly to the PLA.

The licensing page says an application not completed within one year is abandoned. The page and the application
instructions differ on some details, such as how the application is submitted and whether originals or copies of the
certificate and score report are needed, so check the current instructions with the PLA before you send anything.

## How often is the license renewed?

The licensing page says licenses must be renewed "by October 1st of odd numbered years", and that renewal requires
continuing education and proof of liability insurance. IC 25-20.2-6-2(b) says that, subject to IC 25-1-2-6(e), a license not renewed
on or before the renewal date expires and becomes invalid without any action by the board.

## How many continuing education hours are required?

The board's rule 878 IAC 1-5-1 states that a renewing licensee "shall complete not less than thirty-two (32) continuing
education hours in each renewal period." Under 878 IAC 1-5-1(e), a licensee whose first renewal comes after at least 12
but less than 24 months of licensure completes 16 hours, and one licensed less than 12 months is exempt for that
renewal. Under 878 IAC 1-5-1, hours must be earned in the renewal period they are used for and do not carry over, and
records are kept for two years after the end of the renewal period in which the hours were earned.

Under 878 IAC 1-5-2, at least 75% of the hours must be in Category I, which covers heating, cooling, electrical and
plumbing systems, structural components, foundations, roof coverings, and exterior and interior components, and no more than 25% may be in Category II, which covers
topics such as site aspects, business, contracts, ethics, report writing and legal liability.

## What does reinstatement require?

The licensing page's reinstatement section lists a work history letter explaining why the license expired, proof of 32
hours of continuing education obtained within the previous 24 months, passing the National Home Inspector Examination
and a certificate of insurance of at least $100,000. Its fee table lists a separate reinstatement fee for a license
expired more than three years. Under IC 25-20.2-6-2, the board may reinstate a license expired more than three years
if the holder meets the reinstatement requirements in IC 25-1-8-6(d).

## Where to check before you act

These are the requirements as shown on the PLA's home inspector page, its application instructions and the board's
July 1, 2024 compilation on the date above. The compilation says it is not an official version of the Indiana Code or
the Indiana Administrative Code, and the board is the authority, so confirm the current rules, forms, fees and approved
courses with the PLA before you enroll or apply. These pages describe Indiana only; other states set their own rules.
