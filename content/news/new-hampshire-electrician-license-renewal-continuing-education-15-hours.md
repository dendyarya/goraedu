This explainer covers **New Hampshire only**, where electrician licenses are issued by the Electricians' Board through
the Office of Professional Licensure and Certification (OPLC). It answers one practical question from the board's own
rules and pages: how does continuing education work when a New Hampshire journeyman or master electrician renews a
license? It describes the rules and pages as published on the date above. Nothing here describes a rule change, and
nothing here applies to any other state.

## How often is a New Hampshire electrician license renewed?

Board rule Elec 401.01 states: "The following license types shall be renewed 2 years from the date of issuance". The
list includes "Master electricians;" and "Journeyman electrician;". OPLC's electrician FAQ gives the same period:
"Beginning on July 15, 2023, licenses issued will expire two years from the date of issuance."

Under Elec 401.04, renewal may be denied, after notice and a hearing, for "Noncompliance with the continuing education
requirements of Elec 403".

## How many hours of continuing education are required, and on what?

Elec 403.01(a) states: "Each journeyman or master electrician shall obtain 15 hours of instruction on the following:"
first, "The changes to the latest published edition of NFPA 70, the National Electrical Code", and second, "At least
one hour of instruction in changes to:" "RSA 319-C;", "These rules; and" "The installation concerns for electrical
installations, as determined by the board and available on the board's website", under the heading "licensing and
installation concerns".

OPLC's continuing education page lists the same total for both license types, "CE Hours Total=15 hours", with the
required subject area named "Code Update".

## When must the code update be finished?

The rule ties the deadline to the edition of the National Electrical Code. Elec 403.01(b) states that the requirements "shall be completed within 12 months from January 1st of the year following the issuance
of the latest published edition of NFPA 70, the National Electrical Code."

OPLC's page words it as "due within one year from the January 1 following the publication date of the latest version
of the NFPA 70" and lists "CE Cycle 1 year".

The pages do not say how the code update deadline lines up with each person's two-year license renewal date. Ask the
board which code cycle applies to your renewal.

## Can hours carry over, and can courses be online?

OPLC's page asks "Can CEs roll over?" and answers "No". It lists "Courses (Live or Online)" as a category of CE, and
answers "No" when it asks whether there is a restriction on CE delivery methods, such as live courses only.

## Who can teach the course, and how is it recorded?

Courses must be approved in advance. OPLC's page answers "Yes" to "Do courses need to be preapproved by the Board?"
and states: "Continuing education courses shall be approved by the Board."

Elec 403.01(c) states that the 15 hours "shall be completed by a continuing education provider, who shall record proof
of completion with the board within 7 days of the course ending." Under Elec 403.01(e), approved providers "shall take
attendance once at the start of the program, once at the midway point, and once at the end of the day", and "Photographic identification and electrician's license shall be
required as proof of identity at registration and for proof of course completion before the certificate is issued to
licensees."

OPLC's page states that "Licensees in New Hampshire have free access to CE Broker", which it describes as offering "CE
Tracking". For CE audits it lists "10%; Plc 308.04(d)".

## Do apprentices have continuing education too?

OPLC's continuing education page says apprentice CE is "Required only for those seeking fifth renewal of apprentice
card", with "CE Hours Total=30 hours". The board's apprenticeship page states that apprentice identification cards
"shall be valid for a period of 12 months" and that, starting with the fifth renewal of the card, an apprentice who "has completed the schooling
requirements" but has not yet obtained a journeyman license must "successfully complete 30 hours of classroom instruction each year before the ID card shall be renewed."

## What if a license lapses?

Elec 402.01(a) states: "Any master or journeyman electrician whose license is not renewed within one year of the
expiration date of the license shall" either "Meet the requirements of this section and pass the licensing examination
set forth in Elec 305; or" "Apply to take the master or journeyman examination, as determined by the expired license."
OPLC's FAQ says that a license expired "for less than one year" uses the reinstatement application, and one expired
"for one year or more" needs "the initial application".
