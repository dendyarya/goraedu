For OSHA construction safety, the rule on fire extinguishers at a job site is 29 CFR 1926.150, Fire protection, in
Subpart F of the construction standards. This explainer reads that section as it stands in the eCFR. It does not
describe a rule change, and it does not cover general industry workplaces (those fall under Part 1910).

## Who is responsible for fire protection on the site?

The employer. 1926.150(a)(1) states: "The employer shall be responsible for the development of a fire protection
program to be followed throughout all phases of the construction and demolition work, and he shall provide for the
firefighting equipment as specified in this subpart." The same paragraph adds: "As fire hazards occur, there shall be
no delay in providing the necessary equipment."

Paragraph (a) also sets three basic duties for the equipment itself:

- "Access to all available firefighting equipment shall be maintained at all times."
- "All firefighting equipment, provided by the employer, shall be conspicuously located."
- "All firefighting equipment shall be periodically inspected and maintained in operating condition. Defective
  equipment shall be immediately replaced."

## How many fire extinguishers are required?

The count is based on floor area. 1926.150(c)(1)(i) states: "A fire extinguisher, rated not less than 2A, shall be
provided for each 3,000 square feet of the protected building area, or major fraction thereof." The same paragraph
limits the distance: "Travel distance from any point of the protected area to the nearest fire extinguisher shall not
exceed 100 feet."

Floors are covered separately. Under (c)(1)(iv), "One or more fire extinguishers, rated not less than 2A, shall be
provided on each floor. In multistory buildings, at least one fire extinguisher shall be located adjacent to
stairway."

## What about fuel and flammable gas on the job?

A second kind of extinguisher is required near flammable materials. 1926.150(c)(1)(vi) states: "A fire extinguisher,
rated not less than 10B, shall be provided within 50 feet of wherever more than 5 gallons of flammable or combustible
liquids or 5 pounds of flammable gas are being used on the jobsite." The same paragraph sets one limit on its scope:
"This requirement does not apply to the integral fuel tanks of motor vehicles."

## Can something else take the place of an extinguisher?

The section allows a few substitutes for a 2A extinguisher, each with conditions:

- A drum of water: "One 55-gallon open drum of water with two fire pails may be substituted for a fire extinguisher
  having a 2A rating" (c)(1)(ii).
- A garden-type hose: a 1/2-inch hose line "not to exceed 100 feet in length and equipped with a nozzle" may be
  substituted, "providing it is capable of discharging a minimum of 5 gallons per minute with a minimum hose stream
  range of 30 feet horizontally" (c)(1)(iii). The hose lines "shall be mounted on conventional racks or reels," and
  their number and location must be "such that at least one hose stream can be applied to all points in the area."
- A fire hose: "One hundred feet, or less, of 1 1/2-inch hose, with a nozzle capable of discharging water at 25
  gallons or more per minute, may be substituted for a fire extinguisher rated not more than 2A in the designated area
  provided that the hose line can reach all points in the area" (c)(2)(i).

## Are any extinguishers not allowed?

Yes. Under (c)(1)(vii), "Carbon tetrachloride and other toxic vaporizing liquid fire extinguishers are prohibited."
The extinguishers used must be listed or approved: (c)(1)(ix) states that "Fire extinguishers which have been listed
or approved by a nationally recognized testing laboratory, shall be used to meet the requirements of this subpart."

## Do extinguishers need inspection and protection from the cold?

The section requires both. Paragraph (c)(1)(viii) states: "Portable fire extinguishers shall be inspected periodically
and maintained in accordance with Maintenance and Use of Portable Fire Extinguishers, NFPA No. 10A-1970." Paragraph
(c)(1)(v) adds: "Extinguishers and water drums, subject to freezing, shall be protected from freezing." For choosing
the right type, (c)(1)(x) says "Table F-1 may be used as a guide for selecting the appropriate portable fire
extinguishers."

## What else does 1926.150 cover?

Beyond portable equipment, the section also covers water supply, sprinklers, standpipes, alarms and fire cutoffs. Two
examples: under (e)(1), "An alarm system, e.g., telephone system, siren, etc., shall be established by the employer
whereby employees on the site and the local fire department can be alerted for an emergency," and under (b)(1), a
water supply able to run the firefighting equipment "shall be made available as soon as combustible materials
accumulate."
