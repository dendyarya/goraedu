Construction sites may use hoists to move materials or people. Whether workers may ride one, an OSHA construction
safety question, depends on which kind of hoist it is. This explainer quotes
29 CFR 1926.552, "Material hoists, personnel hoists, and elevators", from the eCFR (up to date as of November 17, 2026).
Nothing here describes a rule change.

## Can anyone ride a material hoist?

Only for two purposes. Section 1926.552(b)(1)(ii) states: "No person shall be allowed to ride on material hoists
except for the purposes of inspection and maintenance."

The posted rules must say so too. Under (b)(1)(i), "Operating rules shall be established and posted at the operator's
station of the hoist. Such rules shall include signal system and allowable line speed for various loads. Rules and
notices shall be posted on the car frame or crosshead in a conspicuous location, including the statement 'No Riders
Allowed.'"

## What else does a material hoist need?

Paragraph (b) lists the requirements. Some of them:

- Landing entrances: "All entrances of the hoistways shall be protected by substantial gates or bars which shall guard
  the full width of the landing entrance", painted "with diagonal contrasting colors, such as black and yellow
  stripes" ((b)(2)). Bars must be "not less than 36 inches nor more than 42 inches above the floor" ((b)(2)(i)), and
  gates or bars "shall be equipped with a latching device" ((b)(2)(ii)).
- Overhead protection on the cage or platform: "2-inch planking, 3/4-inch plywood, or other solid material of
  equivalent strength" ((b)(3)), and overhead protection at the operator's station ((b)(4)).
- "Car arresting devices shall be installed to function in case of rope failure" ((b)(6)).
- "All material hoist towers shall be designed by a licensed professional engineer" ((b)(7)).

## Who rides a personnel hoist, and what does it need?

Personnel hoists are covered by paragraph (c). Among its requirements:

- "Cars shall be permanently enclosed on all sides and the top, except sides used for entrance and exit which have car
  gates or doors" ((c)(5)).
- "Doors or gates shall be provided with electric contacts which do not allow movement of the hoist when door or gate
  is open" ((c)(8)).
- "An emergency stop switch shall be provided in the car and marked 'Stop'" ((c)(13)).
- "Internal combustion engines shall not be permitted for direct drive" ((c)(11)).
- Towers "shall be anchored to the structure at intervals not exceeding 25 feet" ((c)(3)).

## How often must a personnel hoist be inspected?

Section 1926.552(c)(15) sets the schedule: "Following assembly and erection of hoists, and before being put in
service, an inspection and test of all functions and safety devices shall be made under the supervision of a competent
person. A similar inspection and test is required following major alteration of an existing installation. All hoists
shall be inspected and tested at not more than 3-month intervals."

The employer keeps proof. The same paragraph requires "a certification record which includes the date the inspection
and test of all functions and safety devices was performed; the signature of the person who performed the inspection
and test; and a serial number, or other identifier, for the hoist that was inspected and tested. The most recent
certification record shall be maintained on file."

Personnel hoists used in bridge tower construction have a shorter cycle: "These hoists shall be inspected and
maintained on a weekly basis", and after winds "exceeding 35 miles per hour" they must be "inspected and put in
operable condition before reuse" ((c)(17)(iii)).

## Rules for all hoists

Paragraph (a) applies to every hoist and elevator in the section. "The employer shall comply with the manufacturer's
specifications and limitations applicable to the operation of all hoists and elevators" ((a)(1)). "Rated load
capacities, recommended operating speeds, and special hazard warnings or instructions shall be posted on cars and
platforms" ((a)(2)). Two practices are banned outright: "The installation of live booms on hoists is prohibited"
((a)(5)), and "The use of endless belt-type manlifts on construction shall be prohibited" ((a)(6)).

Wire rope comes out of service under the conditions in (a)(3), for example "six randomly distributed broken wires in
one rope lay or three broken wires in one strand in one rope lay" in hoisting ropes, or "Evidence of any heat damage
resulting from a torch or any damage caused by contact with electrical wires".
