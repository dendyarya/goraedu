This explainer covers **Wisconsin only**, where electricians are licensed by the Department of Safety and Professional
Services (DSPS) under chapter SPS 305 of the Wisconsin Administrative Code. It answers one practical question from the
code and the department's own application instructions: what does a Wisconsin **journeyman electrician** license
require? Nothing here describes a rule change, and nothing here applies to any other state.

## Who needs an electrician license in Wisconsin?

The DSPS application instructions (Form #3106) explain that under state law "no person may install, repair, or maintain
electrical wiring unless the person is licensed as an electrician by the Department," or is enrolled as a registered
electrician. The same instructions, citing SPS 305.40, say a journeyman "shall perform electrical wiring activities
under the general supervision of a person who holds" a master electrician license or registration (or, for wiring
related to dwellings, a residential master electrician license).

## What qualifies a person to take the exam?

SPS 305.44(3) states: "A person applying for a journeyman electrician license examination shall have at least one of the
following qualifications." The two routes in the code are:

- **Apprenticeship (SPS 305.44(3)(a)):** completion of a "construction electrician apprenticeship program" in
  installing, repairing and maintaining electrical wiring that lasts at least 3 years and is approved by the U.S.
  Department of Labor or the Wisconsin Department of Workforce Development.
- **Experience (SPS 305.44(3)(b)):** experience installing, repairing and maintaining electrical wiring "during a period
  of not less than 48 months, with at least 8,000 hours of experience over that period."

Under the same paragraph, a two-year degree or diploma from an electrical-related program at a school of electrical
engineering or an accredited technical or vocational school counts as "equivalent to 12 months and 2,000 hours of
experience."

The DSPS application instructions also describe other ways to count hours and a route for holders of certain
out-of-state journeyman licenses. Those details are in the form, not in the SPS 305.44 text, so check them with DSPS
before relying on them.

## Is there an exam?

Yes. SPS 305.44(4) says an applicant "shall take and pass an examination in accordance with s. SPS 305.09." Under SPS
305.44(2), the applicant first submits an application under SPS 305.01 with the fees in SPS 305.02. The DSPS
instructions ask applicants to submit the application "to the Department at least 30 days in advance of the exam date
chosen." After passing, the license itself is obtained with a further application and license fee (SPS 305.44(5)).

## How long is the license good for?

SPS 305.44 does not state the term itself. The DSPS application instructions describe the credential fee as "based on a
4-year term from June 30th."

## What does renewal take?

- SPS 305.44(6)(a)1. states: "A person may renew his or her license as a journeyman electrician," and SPS
  305.44(6)(a)2. states: "A journeyman electrician license shall be renewed in accordance with s. SPS 305.07."
- **Continuing education:** under SPS 305.44(6)(b)1., renewal is contingent upon the journeyman "obtaining at least 24
  hours of acceptable continuing education," earned before the license expires as specified in SPS 305.08 and Table
  305.06.
- **Waiver:** under SPS 305.44(6)(b)2., a licensee "may apply to the department for waiver of the continuing education
  requirements" because of prolonged illness, disability or similar circumstances; each request is decided on its
  merits.
- **Licensed without an exam:** under SPS 305.44(6)(b)3., a person who got the license without passing an exam and is
  denied renewal for missing the continuing education must "take and pass the journeyman electrician license examination
  in order to reacquire" the license.
